# OP-05 | Fire Protection and Sodium Leakage | topic | fire protection
range: 8.2-1 8.2-8
sec: Sec. 8.2, pp. 8.2-1 to 8.2-8
intro: Sodium fires change the fire protection picture. Passive features do the work, and water stays away from sodium. This lesson pairs with SY-15.
obj: State the fire protection defense-in-depth approach for conventional and sodium fires.
obj: Describe the sodium leak design features.
obj: Explain how each category of sodium leak is handled.
obj: Describe how leak sizes and locations are postulated.
obj: Explain the fire safe shutdown strategy.
obj: State the preliminary firefighting strategies and the role of operators and responders.

== Leak categories | 3
T Category | Handling
R Operational leakage | Mechanical joint leak, less than design basis, into a catch pan with no deck @@ Operational leakage occurs from a mechanical joint or component
R Into a guard enclosure | Contained and drained to a catch pan through a heat traced line @@ Design basis sodium leakage is contained within the guard enclosure
R Within a process cell | Contained or drained, with sloped floors and steel plates to a catch pan @@ Each process cell is designed to contain or drain design basis leakage for the cell
R Beyond design basis | Evaluated against the Quantitative Health Objectives @@ will be evaluated for acceptable consequences with respect to the Quantitative Health Objectives

== Leak postulation | 4
S Leaks are postulated for all piping that normally contains sodium. @@ Leaks are postulated for all piping that normally contain sodium
S Design basis leakage cracks are Dt/4 for seismic piping, and full circumferential breaks for non-seismic piping. @@ a Dt/4 crack for seismically qualified piping and a full-circumferential break for non-seismically qualified piping
S Sodium and salt systems are evaluated as moderate energy systems. @@ Liquid sodium containing systems are evaluated as moderate energy systems
S SSCs in the HAA are protected from the worst-case leak regardless of stress. @@ SSCs in the HAA are protected from the worst-case leak, regardless of stress
S A top of pipe spray could make a faster, hotter fire. A bottom leak could make a larger volume flood. @@ could create a faster, hotter fire that directly impacts some SSCs
K What leak size is postulated for seismically qualified piping? | Full circumferential break | Dt/4 crack | 1 inch hole | None | 1 | It is bounded by a Dt/4 crack. @@ a Dt/4 crack for seismically qualified piping and a full-circumferential break for non-seismically qualified piping

== Firefighting strategy | 6
S There is no active or manual fire suppression for large sodium fires. @@ There is no active or manual fire suppression for large sodium fires
S Sodium leaks and fires are mitigated passively by NNA features. @@ Sodium leaks and resulting sodium fires are mitigated by passive means
S Class D extinguishers with Sodium Chloride-based dry powder are staged for personnel protection. @@ Class D fire extinguishers containing Sodium Chloride-based dry powder
S Incipient conventional fires in the RAB and RXB use Class A, B, or C extinguishers. @@ manual firefighting methods using Class A, B, or C fire extinguishers are used
S Beyond the incipient stage, offsite responders with specialized training are requested. @@ offsite responders with specialized training are requested to respond
S Deep-seated sodium fires are contained in NNA features and are passively self-suppressing. @@ are passively self-suppressing
S The RXB and RAB have no water-based suppression, to avoid sodium-water interaction. @@ do not contain water-based fire suppression systems to avoid sodium-water interaction
S A Fire Protection Program will be developed at the OL stage to meet 10 CFR 50.48(a). @@ A Fire Protection Program for operations will be developed at the operating license stage
