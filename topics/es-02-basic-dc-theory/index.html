DC sources and rectifiers

Objectives 1.1, 1.2, 1.3

"There are four common ways that DC voltages are produced." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.11
  • Batteries
  • DC generators
  • Thermocouples
  • Rectifiers

DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.11

A battery converts chemical energy into electrical energy. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.1

A DC generator delivers pulsating DC through commutator action. A thermocouple converts heat energy into a voltage output. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.3

"The purpose of a rectifier circuit is to convert AC power to DC." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.5
"The diode acts as a gate, which allows current to pass in one direction and blocks current in the other direction." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.5

A diode is forward biased when the positive terminal of a voltage source is connected to its anode and the negative terminal to its cathode. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.5

With reverse bias, the anode is connected to the negative side of the source, which causes a high resistance that prevents current flow. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.7

With a single diode, current flows only during one-half of the input voltage cycle. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.8

"The output of a half-wave rectifier circuit is pulsating DC." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.8
"A full-wave rectifier circuit is a circuit that rectifies the entire cycle of the AC sine-wave." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.9

The full-wave bridge rectifier is another type of full-wave rectifier circuit. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.10

"This circuit utilizes four diodes." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.10

Its output is a pulsating DC, with all of the waves of the input AC being transferred. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.10

Circuit terms

Objectives 1.5, 1.6

"Resistivity is defined as the measure of the resistance a material imposes on current flow." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.16

The resistance of a conductor depends on its resistivity, its length, and its cross-sectional area. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.16

"Temperature coefficient of resistance, α (alpha), is defined as the amount of change of the resistance of a material for a given change in temperature." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.17

A positive value means resistance increases with temperature, a negative value means it decreases, and zero means it is constant. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.17

Each electrical circuit has at least four basic parts: a source of electromotive force, conductors, a load or loads, and some means of control. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.18

"A closed circuit (Figure 13) is an uninterrupted, or unbroken, path for current from the source (EMF), through the load, and back to the source." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.19
"An open circuit, or incomplete circuit, (Figure 14) exists if a break in the circuit occurs; this prevents a complete path for current flow." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.19

A short circuit offers very little resistance to current flow and can cause dangerously high current through a circuit. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.20

"A series circuit is a circuit where there is only one path for current flow." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.20
"Parallel circuits are those circuits which have two or more components connected across the same voltage source" DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.21

Current through each branch can be different, but voltage throughout a parallel circuit is equal. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.21

"In all cases, the equivalent resistance will be less than any of the individual parallel circuit resistors." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.21

Series and parallel calculations

Objectives 1.7, 1.8, 1.9, 1.15

"The total resistance in a series circuit is equal to the sum of all the parts of that circuit" DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.23

RT = R1 + R2 + R3 ... DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.23

The total voltage across a series circuit is equal to the sum of the voltages across each resistor. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.24

"The sum of the voltage drops in any series circuit is always equal to the applied voltage." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.29

The sum of the currents through each branch of a parallel circuit is equal to the total current flow in the circuit. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.29

1/RT = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3 ... DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.33

"Total resistance of equal resistors in a parallel circuit is equal to the resistance of one resistor divided by the number of resistors." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.34

For two unequal resistors in parallel, multiply the two resistances and divide the product by the sum. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.35

RT = (R1 x R2) / (R1 + R2) DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.35

"A voltage divider, or network, is used when it is necessary to obtain different values of voltage from a single energy source." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.36

Sometimes branch currents in a parallel circuit must be found when only resistance and total current are known. The resistances divide the total current into fractional currents in each branch. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.38

"This process is known as current division." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.38

I1 = IT x R2 / (R1 + R2) DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.38

"each branch current is inversely proportional to the branch resistance." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.38

For a series-parallel circuit, follow the series rules for the series part and the parallel rules for the parallel part. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.58

"Solving these circuits can be simplified by reducing the circuit to a single equivalent resistance circuit" DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.58

Polarity and current direction

Objectives 1.10, 1.11

The direction of electron flow is from a point of negative potential to a point of positive potential. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.40

"The direction of positive charges, or holes, is in the opposite direction of electron flow." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.40

This flow of positive charges is known as conventional flow. Both conventions are in use and they are essentially equivalent. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.40

To find the polarity of a voltage drop, use the direction of the electron current as a basis. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.40

"where the electron current enters the load, the voltage is negative" DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.40

The drop across the load is opposite to that of the source. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.40

Kirchhoff's laws

Objectives 1.12, 1.13, 1.14

Many circuits are extremely complex and cannot be solved with Ohm's Law. Kirchhoff's two laws solve them. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.42

Law 1, Kirchhoff's voltage law: DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.42

"The sum of the voltage drops around a closed loop is equal to the sum of the voltage sources of that loop" DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.42

ΣEsource = ΣIR DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.43

The voltage law can be applied only to closed loops. A closed loop must have one or more voltage sources and a complete path for current flow. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.43

Law 2, Kirchhoff's current law: DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.42

"The current arriving at any junction point in a circuit is equal to the current leaving that junction" DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.42

With more than one voltage source, a direction of current flow must be assumed. Sources that aid the assumed direction are positive, and sources that oppose it are negative. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.44

"If the assumed direction is correct, the answer will be positive." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.44

A negative answer means the assumed direction was wrong, but the magnitude is correct. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.44

Kirchhoff's laws are only restating the laws governing energy and charge conservation. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.42

Objective 1.12Q650

Kirchhoff's voltage law says the sum of the voltage drops around a closed loop equals:

Objective 1.13Q651

15 amps arrives at a junction with two paths leaving it. One path carries 10 amps. What does the other carry?

The answer is A. The current arriving at a junction equals the current leaving it, so the other path carries 15 - 10 = 5 amps. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.42

Objective 1.14Q652

A 30 volt source drives a loop with 5 ohm and 10 ohm resistors. Using Kirchhoff's voltage law, what is the current?

The answer is B. ΣE = ΣIR gives 30 = 5I + 10I = 15I, so I = 2 amps. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.42

DC circuit faults

Objectives 1.16, 1.17

A series circuit has only one path for current. If this path is broken, no current flows and the circuit becomes an open circuit. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.66

"Since no current flows in an open series circuit, there are no voltage drops across the loads." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.66

No power is consumed by the loads, and total power consumed by the circuit is zero. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.66

"If a parallel circuit is opened at a point where only a branch current flows, then only that branch is open, and current continues to flow in the rest of the circuit" DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.67
"In a DC circuit, the only current limit is the circuit resistance." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.68

If the resistance suddenly becomes zero, a very large current will flow. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.68

With an increase in circuit current, the terminal voltage of the energy source will decrease, due to the voltage drop across its internal resistance. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.68

The increased current from a short circuit can damage power sources, burn insulation, and start fires. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.68

"Fuses are provided in circuits to protect against short circuits." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.68
"Parallel circuits are more likely than series circuits to develop damaging short circuits." DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.69

Each parallel load is connected directly across the power source. In a series circuit, the other loads keep the circuit resistance from dropping to zero. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.69

Worked examples

Objective 1.7

Total series resistance

A series circuit has a 60 Ω, a 100 Ω, and a 150 Ω resistor. What is the total resistance?

  1. RT = R1 + R2 + R3.
  2. RT = 60 + 100 + 150.

Answer: 310 Ω

DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.23

Objective 1.7

Voltage drops in a series circuit

A 120 V battery is in series with 40 Ω, 60 Ω, and 100 Ω resistors. Find the voltage across each resistor.

  1. RT = 40 + 60 + 100 = 200 Ω.
  2. I = VT / RT = 120 / 200 = 0.6 amps.
  3. V1 = (0.6)(40) = 24 volts, V2 = (0.6)(60) = 36 volts, V3 = (0.6)(100) = 60 volts.
  4. Check: 24 + 36 + 60 = 120 volts.

Answer: 24 V, 36 V, and 60 V

DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.29

Objective 1.14

Branch current

Two branches are across a 120 V source. Total current is 30 A, and branch R1 takes 22 A. What is the current in branch R2?

  1. IT = I1 + I2.
  2. I2 = IT - I1 = 30 - 22.

Answer: 8 amps

DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.31

Objective 1.7

Equal resistors in parallel

Five lamps, each with a resistance of 40 Ω, are connected in parallel. Find the total resistance.

  1. RT = R / N.
  2. RT = 40 / 5.

Answer: 8 Ω

DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.34

Objective 1.8

Voltage divider

24 volts is applied to 4 Ω, 8 Ω, and 12 Ω resistors in series. Find each voltage drop.

  1. Total current: I = 24 / (4 + 8 + 12) = 1 amp.
  2. Drops: (1)(4) = 4 volts, (1)(8) = 8 volts, (1)(12) = 12 volts.

Answer: 4 V, 8 V, and 12 V

DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.37

Summary

  • DC voltages are commonly produced by batteries, DC generators, thermocouples, and rectifiers. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.11
  • A rectifier converts AC to DC. Half-wave rectifiers convert only one-half of the sine wave, and full-wave rectifiers convert all of it. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.11
  • A closed circuit has a complete path for current flow, and an open circuit does not. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.22
  • A series circuit has one path for current flow. A parallel circuit has two or more components across the same voltage source. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.22
  • Where the electron current enters the load, the voltage is negative. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.41
  • Kirchhoff's voltage law and current law restate the laws governing energy and charge conservation. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.48
  • An open series DC circuit consumes no power, and a shorted DC circuit has a sudden and very large increase in current. DOE-HDBK-1011/1-92 ES-02 p.69
