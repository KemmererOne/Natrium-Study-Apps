Newton's three laws

Objectives 1.1, 1.2, 1.3

Newton's first law of motion states: DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.1

"an object remains at rest (if originally at rest) or moves in a straight line with constant velocity if the net force on it is zero." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.1

Newton's second law states: DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.1

"the acceleration of a body is proportional to the net (i.e., sum or resultant) force acting on it and in the direction of that net force." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.1

F = ma DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.1

F is force, m is mass, and a is acceleration. This law is used to define force units. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.1

For an object's weight at the surface of the earth, the acceleration is g, which equals 9.8 m/sec² or 32.17 ft/sec², so the equation becomes F = mg. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.1

Newton's third law of motion states: DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.2

"if a body exerts a force on a second body, the second body exerts an equal and opposite force on the first." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.2

It has also been stated as, for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.2

"forces always occur in pairs of equal and opposite forces." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.2

Under Newton's first law, an object moves in a straight line with constant velocity when:

The answer is B. Newton's first law: an object remains at rest or moves in a straight line with constant velocity if the net force on it is zero. DOE-1010 (DOE-HDBK-1010-92), Newton's Laws of Motion, CP-03 p. 1

Universal gravitation

Objectives 1.4

Each mass in the universe exerts a mutual, attractive gravitational force on every other mass. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.2

"For any two masses, the force is directly proportional to the product of the two masses and is inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.2

F = G m1 m2 / r² DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.2

G is the universal constant of gravitation, m1 and m2 are the two masses, and r is the distance between their centers. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.2

The value of g varies with altitude, but 30 km of height only changes it from 9.8 m/sec² to 9.7 m/sec². So g is normally treated as a constant for objects close to the surface of the earth. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.3

Objective 1.4Q595

The distance between two masses is doubled. What happens to the gravitational force between them?

The answer is C. The force is inversely proportional to the square of the distance. Doubling the distance divides the force by 2², which is 4. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.2

Momentum

Objectives 1.5

Momentum is, in a sense, the measure of the motion of a moving body. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.5

"It is the result of the product of the body's mass and the velocity at which it is moving." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.5

P = mv DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.5

"Momentum is a vector quantity since it results from the velocity of the object." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.5

If different momentum quantities are added vectorially, the direction of each must be taken into account. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.5

"The rate at which momentum changes with time is equal to the net force applied to an object." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.6

F = ΔP / Δt DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.6

Conservation of momentum

Objectives 1.6, 1.7

"if no net external force acts upon an object, the momentum of the object remains constant." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.7

If force is equal to zero, then ΔP = 0. This is most important for collisions and explosions, where the external force is negligible. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.7

When a gun is fired, the momentum of the recoiling gun is equal and opposite to the momentum of the bullet. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.7

"the sum of a system's initial momentum is equal to the sum of a system's final momentum." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.8
"In an elastic collision, both momentum and kinetic energy (i.e., energy due to an objects velocity) are conserved." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.8
"In an inelastic collision, momentum is conserved, but system kinetic energy is not conserved." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.8

When two bodies collide and have identical final velocities: DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.8

m1v1 + m2v2 = (m1 + m2)vf DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.8

vf = (m1v1 + m2v2) / (m1 + m2) DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.9

Knowledge check: Conservation of momentum

Objective 1.6Q597

Which statement is true of an inelastic collision?

The answer is D. In an inelastic collision, momentum is conserved, but system kinetic energy is not conserved. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.8

Report a problem
Objective 1.7Q598

A 1000 lbm car moving at 20 ft/sec couples with a 3000 lbm car moving at 4 ft/sec in the same direction. What is their final velocity?

The answer is A. vf = [(1000)(20) + (3000)(4)] / (1000 + 3000) = 32,000 / 4000 = 8 ft/sec. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.8

Worked examples

Objective 1.5

Momentum of a bowling ball

Calculate the momentum for a 16 lbm bowling ball rolling down a lane at 22 ft/sec.

  1. P = mv.
  2. P = (16 lbm)(22 ft/sec).

Answer: 352 ft-lbm/sec

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.6

Objective 1.5

Force from a change in velocity

A rocket with a mass of 5 tons must increase its velocity by 110 ft/sec in 9 sec. Calculate the required thrust.

  1. The change in velocity, v - vo, is 110 ft/sec.
  2. F = m (v - vo) / (t - to).
  3. F = (10,000 lbm)(110 ft/sec) / (9 sec).

Answer: 1.2 x 10⁵ ft-lbm/sec²

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.7

Objective 1.7

Coupled railroad cars

Car 1 (2300 lbm) moves at 29 ft/sec and overtakes car 2 (2800 lbm) moving at 11 ft/sec. They couple. Calculate the final velocity.

  1. vf = (m1v1 + m2v2) / (m1 + m2).
  2. vf = [(2300 lbm)(29 ft/sec) + (2800 lbm)(11 ft/sec)] / (2300 lbm + 2800 lbm).

Answer: 19.1 ft/sec

Summary

  • An object remains at rest or moves in a straight line with constant velocity if the net force on it is zero. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-03 p.4
