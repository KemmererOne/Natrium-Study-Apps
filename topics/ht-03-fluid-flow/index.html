Density and buoyancy

Objectives 1.1, 1.2

"As the temperature of the fluid increases, the density decreases and the specific volume increases." DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.2

Liquids are considered incompressible, so a pressure rise makes no real change in density. Gases are very compressible. DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.3

"Buoyancy is defined as the tendency of a body to float or rise when submerged in a fluid." DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.2

When a body is placed in a fluid, it is buoyed up by a force equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces. Word: Fluid Statics and Dynamics - Part 1.docx, slide 1.48 What happens to an object placed in a fluid according to the principle of buoyancy?

"A floating body displaces its own weight of the fluid in which it floats." DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.3

Flow rates

Objectives 1.5, 1.6

"The volumetric flow rate ( V̇ ) of a system is a measure of the volume of fluid passing a point in the system per unit time." DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.9

V̇ = Av DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.9

"The mass flow rate (ṁ) of a system is a measure of the mass of fluid passing a point in the system per unit time." DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.9

ṁ = ρV̇ = ρAv DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.10

Other common units for volumetric flow include gallons per minute. Mass flow rate may be in pounds-mass per hour. DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.9

"Head loss is a measure of the reduction in the total head (sum of elevation head, velocity head and pressure head) of the fluid as it moves through a fluid system." DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.31

Head loss comes from friction with the pipe walls, friction between fluid particles, and turbulence when flow is redirected. DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.31

Head loss is inversely proportional to the diameter of the pipe. DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.31

"The Moody Chart can be used to determine the friction factor based on the Reynolds number and the relative roughness." DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.32

Hf = f (L/D)(v²/2g) DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.32

"A transfer of momentum to the moving fluid increases the fluid’s velocity." DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.47

NPSH = Psuction - Psaturation DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.49

Two identical centrifugal pumps in series produce:

Two identical centrifugal pumps run in parallel. How does system flow compare with one pump?

Objective 1.3

Pressure at depth

A tank is filled with water at 62.4 lbm/ft³. What is the pressure at a depth of 10 feet?

  1. P = ρhg/gc = 624 lbf/ft²
  2. (624 lbf/ft²)(1 ft²/144 in²)

Answer: 4.33 lbf/in²

DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.5

Objective 1.6

Volumetric flow rate

A pipe with an inner diameter of 4 inches has water flowing at 14 ft/sec. Find the volumetric flow rate.

  1. V̇ = (πr²)v
  2. V̇ = (3.14)(2/12 ft)²(14 ft/sec)

Answer: 1.22 ft³/sec

DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.9

Objective 1.6

Mass flow rate

The water has a density of 62.44 lbm/ft³ and the flow is 1.22 ft³/sec. Find the mass flow rate.

  1. ṁ = ρV̇
  2. ṁ = (62.44)(1.22)

Answer: 76.2 lbm/sec

DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.10

Objective 1.8

Piping expansion

Steady flow goes from a 6 in. pipe at 22.4 ft/sec to an 8 in. pipe at constant density. Find the velocity in the 8 in. section.

  1. ρ1A1v1 = ρ2A2v2
  2. v2 = v1 (πr1²/πr2²)
  3. v2 = 22.4 (3 in)²/(4 in)²

Answer: 12.6 ft/sec

DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.12

Objective 1.8

Multiple outlets

500 lbm/sec enters a Y. The 10 in. leg (0.545 ft²) flows at 10 ft/sec, and the 8 in. leg is 0.349 ft². Density is 62.4 lbm/ft³. Find the velocity in the 8 in. leg.

  1. ṁ8 = ṁ12 - ṁ10
  2. v8 = (500 - (62.4)(0.545)(10))/((62.4)(0.349))

Answer: 7.3 ft/sec

DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.15

Objective 1.17

Bernoulli in a conical pipe

Frictionless flow at 125.6 ft³/sec passes through a horizontal cone from 2.0 ft to 4.0 ft diameter. Pressure head at the small end is 16 ft. Find the velocities and the head at the large end.

  1. v1 = 125.6/π(1 ft)² = 40 ft/sec
  2. v2 = 125.6/π(2 ft)² = 10 ft/sec
  3. Head = 16 ft + (40² - 10²)/2(32.17)

Answer: 39.3 ft

DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.24

Objective 1.19

Pump head with Extended Bernoulli

A system has a 65 ft elevation increase, v1 = 0, v2 = 0.178 ft/sec, and 2 ft of friction head. Find the pump head Hp.

  1. Hp = (z2 - z1) + (v2² - v1²)/2g + 0 + Hf
  2. The velocity term is negligible

Answer: 67 ft

DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.27

Objective 1.23

Darcy head loss

Flow at 5.35 ft/sec passes through 100 ft of 20 in. pipe, with friction factor f = 0.012. Find the head loss.

  1. Hf = f (L/D)(v²/2g)
  2. Hf = 0.012 (100 ft / (20/12 ft)) (5.35²/2(32.17))

Answer: 0.32 ft

DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.33

Objective 1.24

Equivalent length of a valve

A fully open gate valve is in a 10 in. pipe. Find the equivalent length.

  1. Leq = (L/D) D
  2. Leq = 10 (10 inches)

Answer: 100 inches

Pump laws

A pump at 1800 rpm has a head of 48 ft and uses 45 kW. Find head and power at 3600 rpm.

  1. Head: 48 ft (3600/1800)²
  2. Power: 45 kW (3600/1800)³

Summary

  • The principle of conservation of mass gives the continuity equation for a control volume. DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.16
  • Laminar flow has a parabolic velocity profile, and turbulent flow is fairly flat across the center of a pipe. DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.20
  • An increasing Reynolds number indicates an increasing turbulence of flow. DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.20
  • Bernoulli's equation is an application of the First Law of Thermodynamics, and it can be modified to take into account friction losses and pump work. DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.30
  • Head loss is the reduction in the total head of a fluid caused by friction in the fluid's motion. DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.36
  • Natural circulation needs a heat source, a heat sink above it, and flowpaths between the warm and cold fluid. DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.40
  • Water hammer is a liquid shock wave from a sudden starting or stopping of flow. DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.46
  • Pump laws give the effect of speed on flow, head, and power, and the operating point is where the pump and system curves cross. DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92 HT-03 p.56
