Ionization terms

Objectives 1.1, 1.2

Ionization is the process of removing one or more electrons from a neutral atom. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.1

"The products of a single ionizing event are called an electron-ion pair." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.1
"Specific ionization is that number of ion pairs produced per centimeter of travel through matter." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.1

Specific ionization depends on the mass, charge, and energy of the particle and on the electron density of matter. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.1

"A particle’s charge has the greatest effect on specific ionization." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.1
"As the energy of a particle decreases, it produces more ion pairs for the same amount of distance traveled." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.1
"Stopping power or linear energy transfer (LET) is the energy lost per unit path length." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.2
"Stopping power, or LET, is proportional to the specific ionization." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.2

Alpha and beta particles

Objectives 1.3

"The alpha particle is a helium nucleus produced from the radioactive decay of heavy metals and some nuclear reactions." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.4

It has two protons and two neutrons, with a charge of +2. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.4

"Alpha particles are the least penetrating radiation." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.4
"Due to its highly charged state, large mass, and low velocity, the specific ionization of an alpha particle is very high." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.4
"The beta particle is an ordinary electron or positron ejected from the nucleus of a beta-unstable radioactive atom." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.5

The beta has a single negative or positive charge and a very small mass. It follows a tortuous path through absorbing material. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.5

"The specific ionization of a beta particle is low due to its small mass, small charge, and relatively high speed of travel." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.5

Gamma rays

Objectives 1.3

"The gamma ray is a photon of electromagnetic radiation with a very short wavelength and high energy." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.6
"There are three methods of attenuating (reducing the energy level of) gamma-rays: photoelectric effect, compton scattering, and pair production." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.6

In the photoelectric effect, the total energy of the gamma is expended in ejecting the electron from its orbit. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.6

"The photoelectric effect is most predominant with low energy gammas" DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.6
"Compton scattering is an elastic collision between an electron and a photon" DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.6

Compton scattering is most predominant with gammas in the 1.0 to 2.0 MeV range. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.6

In pair production, a high energy gamma close to a heavy nucleus disappears, and an electron and a positron appear. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.7

"the original gamma must have at least 1.02 MeV energy." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.7

Neutrons

Objectives 1.3

"Neutrons have no electrical charge and have nearly the same mass as a proton" DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.8

Because of its size and lack of charge, the neutron is fairly difficult to stop and has a relatively high penetrating power. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.8

"Neutrons may collide with nuclei causing one of the following reactions: inelastic scattering, elastic scattering, radiative capture, or fission." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.8

Inelastic scattering leaves the nucleus excited, and the excitation energy is emitted as a gamma ray. Elastic scattering shares kinetic energy without exciting the nucleus. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.8

"Radiative capture (n, γ) takes place when a neutron is absorbed to produce an excited nucleus." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.8
"Secondary ionization caused by the capture of neutrons is important in the detection of neutrons." DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.9

Neutrons interact with B-10 to produce Li-7 and He-4, which share the energy and produce easily detectable secondary ionizations. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.9

Summary

  • Stopping power is proportional to specific ionization. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.3
  • Alpha, beta, gamma, and neutron radiation interact with matter in different ways. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.10
  • Multiplication of electrons in a gas-filled detector is called gas amplification. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.13
  • The alpha curve is higher than the beta curve from Region I to part of Region IV. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.18
  • A compensated ion chamber sets its voltages so the gamma currents of the two chambers cancel. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.39
  • The source range uses a proportional counter, the intermediate range a compensated ion chamber, and the power range an uncompensated ion chamber. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.62
  • Gamma compensation is not required in the power range, since neutrons outnumber gammas by a large number. DOE-HDBK-1013/2-92 IC-06 p.71
