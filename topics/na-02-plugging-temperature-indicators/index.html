Objectives 1

"The purity of the coolant plays a critical role in preventing corrosion of structural materials within the reactor system" Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.5 Sodium Purity Monitoring Theory

"uncontaminated sodium coolant minimizes the risk of material activation by neutron irradiation" Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.5 Sodium Purity Monitoring Theory

"a pure coolant contributes to predictable and stable reactor performance" Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.5 Sodium Purity Monitoring Theory

Plugging temperature indicators (PTIs) are one way to monitor sodium purity. Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.5 Sodium Purity Monitoring Theory

Theory of operation

Objectives 1

As sodium gets colder and approaches its freezing point: Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.7 Plugging Temperature Indicator

"impurities will precipitate out of solution and begin to crystallize" Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.7 Plugging Temperature Indicator
"As impurities build up in the coolant, the temperature at which crystallization occurs rises." Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.7 Plugging Temperature Indicator

A PTI uses this to measure the level of impurities in the sodium. Sodium is sent through the PTI and gradually cooled. Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.5 Sodium Purity Monitoring Theory

When the sodium at the orifice reaches the impurity saturation temperature, impurities such as oxygen and hydrogen begin to precipitate and deposit on the orifice surface, which changes the sodium flow rate. The temperature measured when the flow begins to decrease is the plugging temperature. Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.7 Plugging Temperature Indicator

"The more the impurities the higher the temperature a decrease in flow rate will be detected." Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.5 Sodium Purity Monitoring Theory
"Monitoring changes in the plugging temperature helps determine the level of impurities present in the sodium coolant." Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.7 Plugging Temperature Indicator

Objective 1Q265

As impurities build up in sodium coolant, a plugging temperature indicator senses a ____ in flow past an orifice due to a ____ in impurity saturation temperature.

The answer is D. A rise in impurity crystallization temperature causes impurities to deposit on the orifice, so flow is reduced. Basic construction

Objectives 2

"A plugging temperature indicator measures flow past an orifice." Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.7 Plugging Temperature Indicator
"Impurities in the sodium will begin to precipitate on an orifice." Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.5 Sodium Purity Monitoring Theory
"When the flow rate decrease is detected by a flow meter the temperature of the sodium is noted." Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.5 Sodium Purity Monitoring Theory

So the lesson plan's text names two parts: the orifice, where the impurities deposit, and the flow meter, which detects the flow decrease. The diagrams of the rest of the indicator are pictures in the lesson plan and are not used. Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.5 Sodium Purity Monitoring Theory

Use with cold traps

From the cited sources

"Plugging temperature indicators can be used to determine the effectiveness of cold traps, and when they should be placed in service if not operated continuously." Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.8 Cold Trap

A cold trap cools a small fraction of the main flow to just above the sodium freezing point, so impurities precipitate and adhere to the mesh packing and walls. The clean sodium returns to the main loop. Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.8 Cold Trap

PTIs at Natrium

Objectives 1

The Natrium Sodium Processing System (SPS) uses plugging temperature indicators. The PSAR describes them this way: PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.1, p. 7.2-51

"The key process indicator for sodium purity is the saturation temperature which correlates directly with impurity concentration." PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.1, p. 7.2-51
"PTI operation requires correlating flow rate of the sodium through a plugging orifice at a given temperature to the impurity concentrations." PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.1, p. 7.2-51
"The SPS operates with the sodium above this saturation temperature to avoid plugging passageways in the system." PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.1, p. 7.2-51
"The saturation temperature is also used in conjunction with the traps to adjust processing temperatures for impurity removal." PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.1, p. 7.2-51

Advantages of PTIs

Objectives 3

A PTI has one limit. It: Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

"does not discriminate the species of the plugging impurity" Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

It does detect the impurity's presence, and it offers these principal advantages over grab sampling: Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

"on-line systems avoid contamination of the sample by air or other foreign agents" Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank
"on-line systems are not subject to analytical errors caused by segregation of impurities to the walls of a container during cooling of sodium samples" Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

PTI analysis is not delayed by radioactive decay periods before a sample can be removed, which were up to five days on the EBR-II primary sodium system. Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

PTIs also: Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

"eliminate the need for transporting radioactive samples or for using remote handling equipment during analysis" Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

PTIs give a continuous measurement, or one that can be repeated at short intervals. This helps identify the cause of unexpected changes in impurity concentrations after operations such as reactor shutdown, cold trap isolation or fuel handling. Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

"the precision of a measurement is more readily obtained from a continuous or semi-continuous signal" Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

EBR-II experience

From the cited sources

At EBR-II, PTIs: Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

"have been used since the initial fill of sodium in the primary and secondary systems of EBR-II in 1963" Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

From 1964 to 1967 they were the principal indicators of sodium purity. The practice was to run without cold trapping until the plugging temperature exceeded 149°C, then run the cold trap until it fell below 107°C, then shut the trap off again. Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

More-or-less continuous cold trap operation began in 1967, with plugging temperature measurements made once per day. Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

Copper in the primary sodium was discovered in 1967 by the irreversible plugging of the PTI. Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

"The source of the copper was found to be the exposed bus bars of the primary auxiliary pump." Word: Plugging Temperature Indicators.docx, slide 1.11 Advantages of PTI Fill-in-blank

Worked examples

Objective 1

Reading a PTI during the EBR-II years

It is 1965 at EBR-II, the cold trap is off, and the indicated plugging temperature has just exceeded 149°C. Using the practice of those years, what happens next?

  1. A plugging temperature above 149°C meant it was time to operate the cold trap.
  2. The cold trap ran until the indicated plugging temperature was below 107°C.
  3. The cold trap was then shut off again.

Answer: Run the cold trap until the plugging temperature is below 107°C, then shut it off.
