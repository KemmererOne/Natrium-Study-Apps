Battery terms and purpose

Objectives 1.1, 1.2

"The term voltaic cell is defined as a combination of materials used to convert chemical energy into electrical energy." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.1
"A battery is a group of two or more connected voltaic cells." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.1
"An electrode is a metallic compound, or metal, which has an abundance of electrons (negative electrode) or an abundance of positive charges (positive electrode)." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.1
"An electrolyte is a solution which is capable of conducting an electric current." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.1

If the electrolyte is a paste, the cell is a dry cell. If it is a solution, the cell is a wet cell. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.1

"Specific gravity is defined as the ratio comparing the weight of any liquid to the weight of an equal volume of water." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.1

Lead-acid batteries use an electrolyte that contains sulfuric acid. The specific gravity of the electrolyte falls between 1.000 and 1.835, and it is normally mixed to be less than 1.350. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.2

"Specific gravity is measured with a hydrometer." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.2
"An ampere-hour is defined as a current of one ampere flowing for one hour." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.2

Multiplying the current in amperes by the time of flow in hours gives the total ampere-hours. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.2

"The purpose of a battery is to store chemical energy and to convert this chemical energy into electrical energy when the need arises." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.4

How a cell works

Objectives 1.3

"a chemical cell (or voltaic cell) consists of two electrodes of different types of metals or metallic compounds and an electrolyte solution" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.4

In a cell with zinc and copper electrodes, the zinc electrode accumulates a much larger negative charge because it dissolves into the electrolyte. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.4

Electrons are removed from the copper electrode, leaving it with an excess of positive charge. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.5

If a load is connected across the electrodes, free electrons in the negative zinc electrode move through the wire and load toward the positive copper electrode. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.5

The potential difference that results allows the cell to function as a source of applied voltage. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.5

The lead-acid battery

Objectives 1.4, 1.5

Two types of lead are acted upon electro-chemically by a solution of diluted sulfuric acid. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.6

"The positive plate consists of lead peroxide (PbO2), and the negative plate is sponge lead (Pb)" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.6

On discharge, the electrolyte divides, its hydrogen combines with oxygen to produce water, and the sulfate combines with the lead of both plates to form lead sulphate. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.6

Discharge: PbO2 + Pb + 2H2SO4 → 2PbSO4 + 2H2O DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.6

On charge, the action is reversed, and the return of acid to the electrolyte increases the specific gravity. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.7

Charge: PbO2 + Pb + 2H2SO4 ← 2PbSO4 + 2H2O DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.7

"The specific gravity decreases as the battery discharges and increases to its normal, original value as it is charged." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.8
"the value of specific gravity at any given time is an approximate indication of the battery’s state of charge." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.8

The decrease in specific gravity on discharge is proportional to the ampere-hours discharged. The rise while charging is not uniform. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.8

Connecting cells and rating batteries

Objectives 1.6, 1.7, 1.8

"The output voltage of a battery connected in series is equal to the sum of the cell" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.13

In a series connection, the positive terminal of one cell is connected to the negative terminal of the next. Four 1.5 V cells provide a total of 6 volts. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.10

In a parallel connection, all the positive terminals are connected together and all the negative terminals are connected together. The total voltage is the same as that of a single cell. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.11

The advantage of connecting cells in parallel: DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.11

"it will increase the current-carrying capability of the" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.11
"Cells that cannot be returned to good condition, or recharged after their voltage output has dropped to a value that is not usable, are called primary cells." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.11
"Cells that can be recharged to nearly their original condition are called secondary cells." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.11

Dry cells in flashlights are examples of primary cells. The most common secondary cell is the lead-acid automobile battery. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.11

"The capacity of a storage battery determines how long the storage battery will operate at a certain discharge rate and is rated in ampere-hours." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.11

Internal resistance is due mainly to the resistance of the electrolyte between electrodes. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.12

"internal resistance reduces both the current and voltage available to the load." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.12
"The shelf life of a battery is the time which a battery may be stored and not lose more than 10 percent of its original capacity." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.13

The output voltage of cells connected in series equals:

What is the advantage of connecting cells in parallel?

Battery hazards

Objectives 1.10, 1.11, 1.12

Cell short circuits can be caused by faulty separators, metal particles forming a circuit between the plates, buckling of the plates, or excessive sediments in the bottom of the jar. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.17

"Overcharging and overdischarging should be avoided at all costs." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.17
"With each shorted cell, battery capacity is reduced by a percentage equal to one over the total number of cells." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.17

A lead-acid battery cannot absorb all the energy from the charging source near the completion of the charge. The excess energy produces hydrogen and oxygen. This process is known as gassing. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.17

Gassing is first noticed when cell voltage reaches 2.30-2.35 volts per cell. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.17

"Since hydrogen is highly explosive, it is necessary to provide adequate ventilation to the battery whenever charging is in progress." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.7

No smoking, electric sparks, or open flames are allowed near a charging battery. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.7

"To reduce the amount of gassing, charging voltages above 2.30 volts per cell should be minimized" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.18

The operating temperature of a battery should preferably be maintained in the nominal band of 60-80°F. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.18

"the current flowing through the battery will cause heat to be generated by the electrolysis of water." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.18

The current also generates heat as it passes through the internal resistance, during both charge and discharge. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.18

P = I²Ri DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.18

"Very high temperatures, 125°F and higher, can actually do damage to the battery and cause early failure." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.18

Low temperatures lower battery capacity but prolong battery life under floating operation or storage. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.18

Worked examples

Objective 1.5

State of charge from specific gravity

A lead-acid battery reads 1.175 specific gravity. Its full charge specific gravity is 1.260, and it has a normal gravity drop of 120 points. Estimate its state of charge.

  1. Fully charged 1.260 minus present charge 1.175 is 85 points.
  2. The battery is 85 points below its fully charged state, out of a 120 point drop.
  3. 85/120 is about 71 percent.

Answer: About 71 percent discharged

DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.8

Objective 1.1

Battery capacity

How long will a 120 ampere-hour battery run at a discharge rate of 10 amps?

  1. Capacity is rated in ampere-hours.
  2. 120 ampere-hours / 10 amps.

Answer: 12 hours

Summary

  • A voltaic cell converts chemical energy into electrical energy, and a battery is two or more connected voltaic cells. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.3
  • Specific gravity of a lead-acid battery decreases proportionally during discharge, so it is an approximate indication of state of charge. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.9
  • A battery connected in series has an output voltage equal to the sum of the cell voltages. One connected in parallel has a greater current-carrying capability. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.13
  • Secondary cells can be recharged, and primary cells cannot. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.13
  • Short circuits cause a great reduction in battery capacity. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.19
  • If gassing occurs and the gases are allowed to collect, an explosive mixture of hydrogen and oxygen can be produced. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.19
  • Charging current generates heat by the electrolysis of water and by I²Ri. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-04 p.19
