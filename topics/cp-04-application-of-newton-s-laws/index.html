Force and weight

Objectives 1.1

"Force is defined as a vector quantity that tends to produce an acceleration of a body in the direction of its application." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.2

F = ma DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.2

"Force is characterized by its point of application, its magnitude, and its direction." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.2

Two or more forces may act upon an object without affecting its state of motion. A book on a table has a downward force from gravity and an upward force from the table top, and the net force is zero. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.2

Weight is a special application of the concept of force. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.2

"It is defined as the force exerted on an object by the gravitational field of the earth, or more specifically the pull of the earth on the body." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.2

W = mg / gc DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.2

W is weight in lbf, m is mass in lbm, g is the local acceleration of gravity (32.17 ft/sec²), and gc is a conversion constant equal to 32.17 ft-lbm/lbf-sec². DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.2

"The mass of a body is the same wherever the body is, whether on the moon or on the earth." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.3

The weight of a body depends upon the local acceleration of gravity, so an object weighs less on the moon than on the earth. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.3

Free-body diagrams

Objectives 1.2, 1.3

To account for all the forces on a body, the body must be isolated from all other bodies. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.5

"The diagram of such an isolated body with the representation of all external forces acting on it is called a Free-Body Diagram." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.5

The isolation of a body clearly separates cause and effect and focuses attention on the literal application of a principle. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.5

A book resting on a table has its weight (W) and the normal force (N) from the table, which is equal to the weight of the book. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.5

"A normal force is defined as any perpendicular force with which any two surfaces are pressed against each other." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.5

A free-body diagram is usually constructed in four steps: DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.6

  • Determine which body or combination of bodies is to be isolated.
  • Isolate it with a diagram that represents its complete external boundaries.
  • Represent all forces that act on the isolated body, in their proper positions.
  • Indicate the choice of coordinate axes directly on the diagram.

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.6

"Do not show the forces that the object exerts on anything else, since these forces do not affect the object itself." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.6

A car being towed has four forces on its diagram: the applied force, the frictional force that opposes it, the weight of the car, and the normal force from the road. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.7

Force equilibrium

Objectives 1.4, 1.5

"The force which results from all the forces acting on a body is defined as the net force." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.11

Forces are vector quantities, so both the magnitude and the direction of each force must be accounted for. This is best done using a free-body diagram. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.11

Newton's first law of motion is also called the law of inertia. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.12

"Inertia is the tendency of a body to resist a change in its state of motion." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.12
"If the sum of all forces acting upon a body is equal to zero, that body is said to be in force equilibrium." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.15

ΣF = 0 DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.13

This condition applies to objects in motion with constant velocity and to bodies at rest. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.13

"If the sum of all the forces is not equal to zero, any force or system of forces capable of balancing the system is defined as an equilibrant." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.15

Tensile, compressive, and frictional forces

Objectives 1.6, 1.7, 1.8

"If an applied force on a member tends to pull the member apart, it is said to be in tension." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.17

If a force tends to compress the member, it is in compression. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.17

"ropes, cables, etc., that are attached to bodies can only support tensile loads" DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.17
"fluid forces are almost always compressive forces." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.17

Forces from two surfaces in contact, where one surface is attempting to move parallel to or over the other, are friction forces. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.17

With a small applied force, a block does not move, because the static-friction force balances the applied force. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.18

As the applied force increases, the static-friction force increases until it reaches a maximum value, FM. Beyond that, the block starts sliding. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.18

"As soon as the block has been set in motion, the magnitude of F drops from FM to a lower value" DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.18

That lower value, FK, is the kinetic-friction force, which remains approximately constant as the block slides. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.18

FM = μs N DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.19

FK = μK N DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.19

"do not depend upon the area of the surfaces in contact." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.19
"Both coefficients, however, depend strongly on the nature of the surfaces in contact." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.19
"frictional forces are always opposite in direction to the motion (or impending motion) of the object." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.19

Centripetal and centrifugal force

Objectives 1.9

An object moving at constant speed in a circle is not in equilibrium, because the direction of its velocity is continually changing. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.19

A net force acts towards the center of the circular path. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.19

"This force is known as centripetal force." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.19

Without this force, an object will move in a straight line. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.19

Centrifugal force appears to be a force directed away from the center of the circular path. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.20

"This is actually a fictitious force, but is an apparent force that is used to describe the forces present due to an object's rotation." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.20

If a string holding a plane in a circle is cut, the plane flies off in a straight line tangent to the circle. If an actual centrifugal force were present, it would fly directly away from the circle. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.20

How does centrifugal force differ from centripetal force?

The answer is B. Centripetal force acts towards the center of the path. Centrifugal force is a fictitious, apparent force that appears to be directed away from the center. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.20

Worked examples

Objective 1.1

Weight on the earth

Calculate the weight of a person with a mass of 185 lbm.

  1. W = mg / gc.
  2. W = (185 lbm)(32.17 ft/sec²) / (32.17 ft-lbm/lbf-sec²).

Answer: 185 lbf

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.3

Objective 1.1

Weight on the moon

Calculate the weight of the same person on the moon, where gravity is 5.36 ft/sec².

  1. W = mg / gc.
  2. W = (185 lbm)(5.36 ft/sec²) / (32.17 ft-lbm/lbf-sec²).

Answer: 28.19 lbf

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.3

Objective 1.4

Tension in a cable

An object weighing 125 lbf hangs motionless from cables. Calculate the tension T1 in the cable at 30° with the horizontal.

  1. The object is in equilibrium, so the sum of the y components is zero.
  2. Choose + as upward. The upward component of T1 is T1 sin 30°, T3 is -125 lbf, and T2 has no y component.
  3. (T1 sin 30°) - 125 lbf = 0.
  4. 0.5 T1 = 125 lbf.

Answer: T1 = 250 lbf

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.15

Summary

  • Force is a vector quantity that tends to produce an acceleration of a body in the direction of its application. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.4
  • Weight is the force exerted on an object due to gravity. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.4
  • A free-body diagram isolates a body and illustrates all the forces that act on the body. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.10
  • If the vector sum of all the forces acting on an object is equal to zero, then the object is in equilibrium. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.16
  • Any force or system of forces capable of balancing a system so that the net force is zero is an equilibrant. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.16
  • Static-frictional forces are present when an object is stationary, and kinetic-frictional forces are present between two objects that are moving. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.21
  • Centripetal force is directed towards the center of the path. Centrifugal force is a fictitious force that appears directed away from it. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-04 p.21
