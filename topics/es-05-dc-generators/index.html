DC equipment terms

Objectives 1.1, 1.2, 1.3

"Terminal voltage, as applied to DC generators, is defined as the voltage that can be measured at the output of the generator." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.1

Applied voltage is the voltage that is delivered across the load. It should be the same as terminal voltage, but circuit faults and losses may reduce it. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.1

In a generator with a rotating armature, voltage is induced in the armature conductors. It opposes the applied voltage and reduces the current flow through the armature. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.1

"This induced voltage acts counter to applied voltage; therefore, it is called counter-electromotive force (CEMF)." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.1

Commutation is the positioning of the DC generator brushes so that the commutator segments change brushes at the same time the armature current changes direction. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.1

"commutation is the mechanical conversion from AC to DC at the brushes of a DC machine" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.1

DC machine components

Objectives 1.4

DC motors and generators have the same basic construction, differing primarily in the energy conversion. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.3

"The purpose of the armature is to provide the energy conversion in a DC machine" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.3

In a DC generator, the armature converts mechanical energy to electrical energy. In a DC motor, it converts electrical energy into mechanical energy in the form of torque. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.3

"The purpose of the rotor is to provide the rotating element in a DC machine" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.3

In both types of DC machines, the rotor is the armature. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.3

"The stator is the part of a motor or generator that is stationary" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.4

In DC machines, the purpose of the stator is to provide the magnetic field. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.4

"The purpose of the field in a DC machine is to provide a magnetic field for producing either a voltage (generator) or a torque (motor)" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.4

The field is produced by a permanent magnet or an electromagnet. Electromagnets are normally used because their magnetic strength is greater and more easily varied. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.4

Producing the voltage

Objectives 1.5, 1.6, 1.7

"there are three conditions necessary to induce a voltage" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.5
  • A magnetic field
  • A conductor
  • Relative motion between the two

DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.5

A basic DC generator has four basic parts: a magnetic field, a single conductor or loop, a commutator, and brushes. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.5

The stronger the field or the more flux lines cut for a given period of time, the larger the induced EMF. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.6

The left-hand rule for generators gives the direction of the induced current. If you DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.6

"point the index finger of your left hand in the direction of the magnetic field (from North to South) and point the thumb in the direction of motion of the conductor, the middle finger will point in the direction of current flow" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.6
"The commutator converts the AC voltage generated in the rotating loop into a DC voltage." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.7

The voltage between the brushes fluctuates between zero and some maximum value, but is always of the same polarity. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.8

Output voltage depends on the number of conductor loops in series in the armature, armature speed, and magnetic field strength. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.9

The field strength can be changed quite easily by varying the current through the field winding. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.9

"This is the most widely used method for regulating the output voltage of a DC generator" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.9

Ratings and losses

Objectives 1.8, 1.9

"A DC generator contains four ratings." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.10
  • Voltage: based on the insulation type and design of the machine.
  • Current: based on the size of the conductor and the amount of heat that can be dissipated in the generator.
  • Power: based on the mechanical limitations of the device that turns the generator, and on the thermal limits of conductors, bearings, and other components.
  • Speed: the upper limit is the speed at which mechanical damage is done, and the lower limit is based on the limit for field current.

DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.10

"There are four internal losses that contribute to lower efficiency of a DC generator." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.10
  • Copper losses
  • Eddy-current losses
  • Hysteresis losses
  • Mechanical losses

DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.12

"Copper loss is the power lost as heat in the windings" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.10
"These circulating currents within the iron core are called eddy-currents." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.10
"the armature and field cores are constructed from laminated (layered) steel sheets." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.11

Hysteresis losses occur when the armature rotates in a magnetic field. Rotating magnetic domains in the iron cause friction and heat. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.11

Mechanical losses can be caused by bearing friction, brush friction on the commutator, or air friction called windage. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.11

Shunt-wound and series-wound generators

Objectives 1.10

"When the field winding of a generator is connected in parallel with the generator armature, the generator is called a shunt-wound" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.13

A shunt-wound generator has a much more stable voltage output than a series-wound generator. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.14

As load current increases, the voltage drop across the armature coil increases, so output voltage decreases. The field current then decreases, which lowers voltage further. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.14

"When the field winding of a DC generator is connected in series with the armature, the generator is called a series-wound generator" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.15

The excitation current in a series-wound generator is the same as the current the generator delivers to the load. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.15

"A series generator has poor voltage regulation" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.15

As a result, series generators are not used for fluctuating loads. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.15

Compound generators

Objectives 1.11, 1.12

A compound generator has a field winding in parallel with the armature and a field winding in series with it. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.16

"If the two fields of a compound generator are wound so that their magnetic fields aid one another, the generator is said to be cumulatively-compounded." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.16
"If the two fields are wound so that their flux fields oppose one another, the generator is said to be differentially-compounded." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.16

As load current increases in a cumulatively-compounded generator, the series field strength increases and compensates for the decrease in shunt field strength. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.16

"In practical compounded generators, the change in output voltage from no-load to full-load is less than 5 percent." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.17

A generator with this characteristic is said to be flat-compounded. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.17

In an over-compounded generator, the series winding overcompensates for a change in the shunt field. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.17

"The output gradually rises with increasing load current over the normal operating range of the machine." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.17

In an under-compounded generator, the series winding undercompensates for the change in shunt field strength. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.17

"The output voltage decreases gradually with an increase in load current." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.17

Objective 1.6

Using the left-hand rule

In a generator, the conductor closest to the N pole is traveling upward across the field. Which way does the current flow?

  1. Point the index finger along the magnetic field, from North to South.
  2. Point the thumb in the direction of motion of the conductor.
  3. The middle finger points in the direction of current flow.
  4. Applying the rule to both sides of the loop shows that current flows in a counter-clockwise direction.

Answer: To the right, lower corner in Figure 4

DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.6

Objective 1.7

Raising terminal voltage

The output voltage of an operating DC generator must be raised. Which of the three factors should change?

  1. The number of conductors cannot be changed in a normally operating generator.
  2. It is usually impractical to change the armature speed.
  3. The magnetic field strength can be changed easily by varying the field winding current.

Answer: Vary the current through the field winding

Summary

  • Terminal voltage is the voltage that can be measured at the output of the generator. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.2
  • In a DC generator, commutation provides for the conversion of AC to a DC output that is generated in the armature windings. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.2
  • The armature provides the energy conversion, the rotor is the rotating element, and the field provides a magnetic field. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.4
  • A voltage is induced when there is a magnetic field, a conductor, and relative motion between the two. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.12
  • The terminal voltage of a DC generator is adjusted by varying the field strength. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.12
  • A shunt-wound generator has its field in parallel with the armature winding, and its voltage decreases with an increase in load. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.18
  • A series-wound generator has its field in series with the armature winding, and its voltage increases sharply with an increase in load. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.18
  • In a cumulatively-compounded generator the fields aid one another, and in a differentially-compounded generator they oppose one another. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-05 p.18
