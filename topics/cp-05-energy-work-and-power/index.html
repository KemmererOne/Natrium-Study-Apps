Energy

Objectives 1.1

"Energy is defined as the measure of the ability to do work." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.1

Energy determines the capacity of a system to perform work and may be stored in various forms. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.1

Both thermal and mechanical energy can be separated into two categories, transient and stored. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.1

"Transient energy is energy in motion, that is, energy being transferred from one place to another." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.1
"Stored energy is the energy contained within a substance or object." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.1

Thermal energy is related to temperature: the higher the temperature, the greater the molecular movement and the greater the energy. Thermal energy in motion is commonly called heat. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.3

"Internal energy is the energy stored in a substance because of the motion and position of the particles of the substance." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.3

Stored mechanical energy exists in one of two forms, kinetic or potential. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.3

"Transient mechanical energy is commonly referred to as work." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.3

Potential and kinetic energy

Objectives 1.1, 1.2, 1.3

"Potential energy is defined as the energy stored in an object because of its position." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.1

The measure of an object's position is its vertical distance above a reference point. Its potential energy represents the work required to elevate it to that position from the reference point. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.2

PE = mgz / gc DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.2

PE is in ft-lbf, m is mass in lbm, g is 32.17 ft/sec², gc is 32.17 (lbm-ft)/(lbf-sec²), and z is height above a reference in ft. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.2

"the g c is used only when using the English system of measurement." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.2
"Kinetic energy is defined as the energy stored in an object because of its motion." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.2

KE = mv² / 2gc DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.2

KE is in ft-lbf, m is mass in lbm, v is velocity in ft/sec, and gc is 32.17 (lbm-ft)/(lbf-sec²). DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.3

An object's kinetic energy represents the energy required to increase its velocity from rest to its final velocity. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.3

Objective 1.1Q609

A body's energy is stored because of its position. What type of energy is this?

The answer is C. Potential energy is defined as the energy stored in an object because of its position. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.1

Objective 1.2Q610

Which expression gives kinetic energy in English units?

The answer is B. KE = mv² / 2gc. The expression mgz / gc is potential energy, and the others are power. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.2

Objective 1.3Q611

What is the potential energy of a 20 lbm object held 10 feet above the ground?

The answer is C. PE = mgz / gc = (20 lbm)(32.17 ft/sec²)(10 ft) / 32.17 = 200 ft-lbf. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.1

Work

Objectives 1.1, 1.2, 1.3

"Work is done by a force acting on a moving object if the object has some component of motion in the direction of the force." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.4

W = F x d DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.4

W is work done in ft-lbf, F is the force applied in lbf, and d is the distance the object is moved in ft. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.4

"if no movement is achieved, no work has been accomplished." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.4

Even if you feel fatigued, no work has been done if nothing moved. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.4

Knowledge check: Work

Objective 1.1Q488

The work done by a force on an object is the product of the force and:

The answer is D. Work is the product of the force and the distance the object moves in the direction of the force. DOE-1010 (DOE-HDBK-1010-92), Energy and Work, CP-05 p. 5

Report a problem
Objective 1.3Q612

A constant force of 300 lbf is applied to a box, but the box does not move. How much work is done?

The answer is A. W = F x d = 300 lbf x 0 ft = 0 ft-lbf. If no movement is achieved, no work has been accomplished. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.4

Report a problem
Objective 1.3Q613

A horizontal force of 100 lbf moves a box 6 feet horizontally. How much work is done?

The answer is C. W = F x d = 100 lbf x 6 ft = 600 ft-lbf. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.4

Conservation of energy

Objectives 1.4

The First Law of Thermodynamics is simply stated: DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.6

"energy cannot be created or destroyed, only altered in form." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.6

The energy expended in raising an object equals the potential energy it gains because of its height. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.6

Energy(initial) + Energy(added) - Energy(removed) = Energy(final) DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.6

Neglecting heat, the balance can be written with work terms alone. This simplified energy balance applies only to mechanical problems. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.7

KE1 + PE1 + Won = KE2 + PE2 + Wby DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.7

"Any energy balance is a statement of the Law of Conservation of Energy." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.7

Power

Objectives 1.1, 1.2, 1.3

"Power is defined as the amount of energy used per unit time or rate of doing work." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.8
"Thermal power is the measure of thermal energy used per unit time." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.8

Thermal power is the rate of heat transfer or heat flow rate, calculated as heat used divided by time required. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.8

"Mechanical power is the rate at which work is done." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.9

Mechanical power is expressed in joules/sec or watts in the mks system, and in ft-lbf/s or horsepower in the English system. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.9

P = work done / time required DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.9

P = Fd / t DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.9

"One horsepower is equivalent to 550 ft-lbf/s and 745.7 watts." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.9

P (hp) = Fv / 550 DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.9

These equations assume force and velocity are constant, or that average values are used. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.9

Knowledge check: Power

Objective 1.1Q489

Power is the amount of energy used:

The answer is A. Power is the amount of energy used per unit time. DOE-1010 (DOE-HDBK-1010-92), Power, CP-05 p. 11

Report a problem
Objective 1.2Q615

With force in lbf and velocity in ft/s, which expression gives power in horsepower?

The answer is C. P = Fv / 550, because one horsepower is equivalent to 550 ft-lbf/s. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.9

Report a problem
Objective 1.3Q616

A steady force of 100 lbf moves an object at a constant 11 ft/s. What is the power in horsepower?

The answer is B. P = Fv / 550 = (100 lbf)(11 ft/s) / 550 = 2 hp. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.9

Worked examples

Objective 1.3

Potential energy

What is the potential energy of a 50 lbm object suspended 10 feet above the ground?

  1. PE = mgz / gc.
  2. PE = (50 lbm)(32.17 ft/sec²)(10 ft) / (32.17 lbm-ft/lbf-sec²).

Answer: 500 ft-lbf

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.2

Objective 1.3

Kinetic energy

What is the kinetic energy of a 10 lbm object that has a velocity of 8 ft/sec?

  1. KE = mv² / 2gc.
  2. KE = (10 lbm)(8 ft/sec)² / (2)(32.17 lbm-ft/lbf-sec²).

Answer: 9.95 ft-lbf

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.3

Objective 1.3

Work on a box

You apply a horizontal force of 200 lbf to a box, and it moves five feet horizontally. How much work have you done?

  1. W = F x d.
  2. W = 200 lbf x 5 ft.

Answer: 1000 ft-lbf

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.4

Objective 1.3

Power of a pump

A pump does 1.5 x 10⁸ ft-lbf of work every 100 minutes. What is the power of the pump in hp?

  1. Power = work done / time required.
  2. P = (1.5 x 10⁸ ft-lbf / 100 min)(1 min / 60 sec)(1 hp / 550 ft-lbf/sec).

Answer: 45.5 hp

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.10

Objective 1.3

Power from force and velocity

A steady force of 1 lbf gives a ball a constant velocity of 5 ft/s. What is the power used?

  1. P = Fv / 550.
  2. P = (1 lbf)(5 ft/sec) / 550.

Answer: 9 x 10⁻³ hp

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.10

Summary

  • Energy is the ability to do work. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.5
  • The work done by a force on an object is the product of the force and the distance the object moves in the direction of the force. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.5
  • Kinetic energy is the energy an object has because of its motion. Potential energy is the energy of an object due to its position. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.5
  • Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only altered in form. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.7
  • Power is the amount of energy used per unit time. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-05 p.11
