Gauge glasses

Objectives 1.1

"Liquid level measuring devices are classified into two groups: (a) direct method, and (b) inferred method." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.1

A dipstick is an example of the direct method. A pressure gauge at the bottom of a tank, reading hydrostatic head, is an example of the inferred method. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.1

"In the gauge glass method, a transparent tube is attached to the bottom and top (top connection not needed in a tank open to atmosphere) of the tank that is monitored." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.1

Gauge glasses effectively form a U tube manometer, where DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.2

"the liquid seeks its own level due to the pressure of the liquid in the vessel." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.2
"Gauge glasses made from tubular glass or plastic are used for service up to 450 psig and 400°F." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.2

For higher temperatures and pressures, a metal-bodied gauge glass with a heavy glass or quartz section is used. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.2

Reflex and refraction gauge glasses

Objectives 1.1

"The presence or absence of liquid in the chamber determines if the light rays are refracted into the chamber or reflected back to the outer surface of the glass." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.3

In a reflex gauge glass, light is reflected back at an air-glass interface. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.3

"This causes the gauge glass to appear silvery-white." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.3

Where water fills the chamber, the light is refracted into the chamber. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.3

"This results in the glass appearing black" DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.3
"Light is bent, or refracted, to a greater extent in water than in steam." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.4
"The portion of the gauge containing water appears green; the portion of the gauge from that level upward appears red." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.4

Float detectors

Objectives 1.1

The ball float is a direct reading mechanism. A ball float is attached to a rod connected to a rotating shaft that indicates level on a calibrated scale. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.4

"If the liquid level changes, the float will follow and change the position of the pointer attached to the rotating shaft." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.4
"The actual level range is determined by the length of the connecting arm." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.5

A chain float is used where the small level limitations of ball floats must be exceeded. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.5

"The range of level measured will be limited only by the size of the vessel." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.5
"The magnetic bond method was developed to overcome the problems of cages and stuffing boxes." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.6

The float travels outside a non-magnetic tube housing an inner magnet. The outer magnet attracts the inner magnet, which follows the level. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.6

A float switch gives a simple, accurate, and reliable signal, such as to start a sump pump. Word: Level Detectors.docx, slide 1.1 Introduction and float switch

"The primary disadvantage is that you only get level indication at specific points" Word: Level Detectors.docx, slide 1.1 Introduction and float switch

Conductivity probes

Objectives 1.1

A conductivity probe system has one or more level detectors, an operating relay, and a controller. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.6

"When the liquid makes contact with any of the electrodes, an electric current will flow between the electrode and ground." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.6

The current energizes a relay, whose contacts open or close. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.6

"The relay in turn will actuate an alarm, a pump, a control valve, or all three." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.6
"A typical system has three probes: a low level probe, a high level probe, and a high level alarm probe." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.6

Differential pressure: open tank and dry reference leg

Objectives 1.1

"The higher pressure, caused by the fluid in the tank, is compared to a lower reference pressure (usually atmospheric)." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.7

With an open tank, the low pressure side is vented to the atmosphere, DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.7

"therefore, the pressure differential is the hydrostatic head, or weight, of the liquid in the tank." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.7

For a pressurized tank, both the high and low pressure sides must be connected. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.7

"The reference leg must be maintained dry so that there is no liquid head pressure on the low pressure side of the transmitter." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.8
"The gas or vapor pressure is equally applied to the low and high pressure sides." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.8

The transmitter output is therefore directly proportional to the hydrostatic head pressure, which is the level in the tank. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.8

"when differential pressure rises, indicated level rises for level detectors in a dry reference leg." Word: Level Detectors.docx, slide 1.8 The most important slide of this section!!

In a dry reference leg level detector, what happens to indicated level once tank level rises enough to spill over into the reference leg?

Wet reference leg

Objectives 1.1

Where a tank contains a condensible fluid, condensation is greatly increased in the reference leg. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.8

"To compensate for this effect, the reference leg is filled with the same fluid as the tank." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.8

The filled leg applies a constant hydrostatic pressure to the high pressure side, equal to the maximum level to be measured. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.9

"The ∆P transmitter is exposed to equal pressure on the high and low pressure sides when the liquid level is at its maximum; therefore, the differential pressure is zero." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.9
"the differential pressure and the transmitter output are inversely proportional to the tank level." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.9
"a differential pressure drop causes indicated level to rise for level detectors installed with a wet reference leg." Word: Level Detectors.docx, slide 1.8 The most important slide of this section!!

A leak in the reference leg reduces differential pressure, causing indicated level to rise above actual level. Word: Level Detectors.docx, slide 1.9 Your turn to practice!!

A rise in reference leg temperature makes its fluid expand and spill over, which also causes indicated level to rise above actual level. Word: Level Detectors.docx, slide 1.8 The most important slide of this section!!

Knowledge check: Wet reference leg

Objective 1.1Q387

In the wet reference leg system shown, what happens to differential pressure as tank level goes down?

The answer is C. As the tank level goes down, the pressure on the low pressure side goes down also, and the differential pressure increases. DOE-1013-V1 (DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92), Level Detectors, IC-03 p. 9

Report a problem
Objective 1.1Q388

In a wet reference leg system, what is the differential pressure when liquid level is at its maximum?

The answer is D. At maximum level the high and low pressure sides see equal pressure, so the differential pressure is zero. DOE-1013-V1 (DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92), Level Detectors, IC-03 p. 9

Report a problem
Objective 1.1Q267

In a wet reference leg level detector, a leak in the reference leg causes indicated level to:

The answer is B. The leak lowers the liquid height on the high side, reducing D/P; with a wet reference leg, lower D/P means higher indicated level. Approved lesson plan: Level Detectors (Level Detectors.docx), slide 1.9 Your turn to practice!!

Report a problem
Objective 1.1Q268

The full, wet reference leg of a tank level detector heats up, for example from a nearby sodium fire. Actual level is constant. Indicated level becomes:

The answer is C. The heated reference leg fluid expands and spills into the tank, lowering high-side pressure and D/P; with a wet reference leg, indicated level rises above actual. Approved lesson plan: Level Detectors (Level Detectors.docx), slide 1.8 The most important slide of this section!!

Density compensation

Objectives 1.2

If a vapor with a significant density exists above the liquid, the hydrostatic pressure it adds needs to be considered. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.11

"As the density of the steam or vapor increases, the weight increases and causes an increase in hydrostatic head even though the actual level of the tank has not changed." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.11
"As the temperature of the fluid in the tank is increased, the density of the fluid decreases." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.12

The mass in the tank stays the same, but the density changes while the reference leg density stays about constant. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.12

"The density of the fluid in the reference leg is dependent upon the ambient temperature of the room in which the tank is located" DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.12
"some means of density compensation must be incorporated in order to have an accurate indication of tank level." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.12

Density compensation is accomplished by one of the following: DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.14

  • Electronic circuitry
  • Pressure detector input
  • Instrument calibration

DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.14

"Density compensation may also be accomplished through electronic circuitry." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.13

Calibration charts are also available to correct indications for changes in reference leg temperature. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.13

Which is one way density compensation is accomplished?

Remote indication and environmental concerns

Objectives 1.3, 1.4, 1.5

"Remote indication is necessary to provide transmittal of vital level information to a central location" DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.15
  • Level measurements may be taken at locations far from the main facility
  • The level to be controlled may be a long distance from the point of control
  • The level being measured may be in an unsafe/radioactive area

DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.15

The D/P transmitter has a diaphragm with high and low pressure inputs on opposite sides, which moves as differential pressure changes. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.15

"The transducer changes this mechanical motion into an electrical signal." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.15

The amplifier amplifies the signal, and the indicator shows level at a remote location. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.17

Fluid density, ambient temperature, and humidity can affect level detection instruments. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.17

"It primarily affects level sensing instruments which utilize a wet reference leg." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.16
"Ambient temperature variations will affect the accuracy and reliability of level detection instrumentation." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.16
"High humidity causes moisture to collect on the equipment." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-03 p.16
