Purpose of the three drawing types

Objectives 1.1

"These three types of drawings represent the category of drawings commonly referred to as blueprints." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.1
"Fabrication, construction, and architectural drawings differ from P&IDs, electrical prints, and logic diagrams in that they are drawn to scale and provide the component's physical dimensions so that the part, component, or structure can be manufactured or assembled." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.1
"A fabrication drawing provides information on how a single part is machined or fabricated in a machine shop, whereas a construction drawing provides the construction or assembly of large multi-component structures or systems." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.1

Fabrication drawings, also called machine drawings, are found mainly in and around machine and fabrication shops. They usually show the part as an orthographic projection, with each view holding the needed dimensions. DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.1

Construction drawings are found mainly at construction sites, and usually show each structure or system as an orthographic projection with the dimensions needed for assembly. DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.3

Architectural drawings are used by architects in the conceptual design of buildings and structures. Examples of their content are the location, size, and type of doors, windows, and rooms, the flow of people, storage areas, and the location of equipment. DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.3

"These drawings do not provide detailed information on how the structure or building is to be built, but rather they provide information on how the designer wants the building to appear and how it will function." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.3
"The purpose of a fabrication drawing is to provide the information necessary to manufacture and machine components." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.12
"The purpose of construction drawings is to provide the information necessary to build and assemble structures and systems." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.12
"The purpose of architectural drawings is to provide conceptual information about buildings and structures." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.12

Dimensioning a drawing

Objectives 1.2

Drawings usually show dimensions and tolerances per American National Standards Institute (ANSI) standards. DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.5

"Dimensions on a drawing can be expressed in one of two ways." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.5

In the first method, the drawing is drafted to scale and the measurement is taken from the drawing and corrected for the scale. In the second, the actual dimensions are specified on the drawing. DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.5

"The second method is the preferred method because it reduces the chances of error and allows greater accuracy and drawing flexibility." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.5
"When actual dimensions are specified on a print, the basic line symbols that are illustrated by Figure 4 are used." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.6
  • Visible outline (full line): shows the shape of the part and all visible features
  • Hidden line (dashed line): shows hidden features
  • Center line: locates the centers of holes and round features
  • Extension line: extends from the object to locate dimension lines
  • Dimension line: shows the size of the object

DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.6

"Figure 5 provides examples of the various methods used on drawings to indicate linear, circular and angular dimensions." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.7

Tolerances

Objectives 1.3

Each dimension on a drawing must have a tolerance. In many cases it is not stated, but is set to an implied standard, as with a house blueprint built to the normal tolerances of the trade. DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.8

"Another method of expressing tolerances on a drawing is to state in the title block, or in a note, a global tolerance for all measurements on the drawing." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.8
"The last method is to state the tolerance for a specified dimension with the measurement." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.8

This method is usually used along with one of the other two. DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.8

"This type of notation is commonly used for a dimension that requires a higher level of accuracy than the remainder of the drawing." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.8

Tolerances apply to more than linear dimensions. They can apply to the radius, the degree of out-of-round, the allowable out-of-square, the surface condition, or any other parameter that affects the shape and size of the object. These are called geometric tolerances. DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.8

"Geometric tolerances state the maximum allowable variation of a form or its position from the perfect geometry implied on the drawing." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.8
  • Straightness: a straight line
  • Circularity (roundness): a circle
  • Cylindricity: a circle with two parallel bars
  • Parallelism: two slanted parallel lines
  • Perpendicularity: an inverted T

DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.9

Why one dimension is left off

Objectives 1.3

Because tolerances let a part vary, not all of an object's dimensions can be specified. DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.11

"This allows the unspecified, and therefor non- toleranced, dimension to absorb the errors in the critical dimensions." DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.11

In Figure 8 (A), each step plus its maximum tolerance is 1 1/10 inches, for a maximum length of 3 3/10 inches, but the total length cannot exceed 3 1/10 inches. A drawing dimensioned this way is not correct. DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.11

The designer must either give each dimension a tolerance that keeps the sum inside the overall dimension and its tolerance, or leave one dimension off, as in Figure 8 (B). Leaving one off is the preferred method. DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.11

Worked examples

Objective 1.2

Read a dimensioned structure

What is the overall height of the structure, its width front to back, and the difference between its width front to back and side to side at the base?

  1. Read the overall height from the 5'-6" dimension.
  2. Read the front-to-back width from the 4'-1" dimension.
  3. Subtract: 4'-10" side to side minus 4'-1" front to back.

Answer: 5' 6" overall height, 4' 1" front to back, and a difference of 9".

DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.16

Objective 1.3

Read a shaft tolerance

What geometric characteristic is being given a tolerance, and what are the maximum and minimum shaft diameters?

  1. Use the symbol chart: a straight line in the box is straightness.
  2. The surface must be straight to within 0.02 inches.
  3. Read the two limits stacked beside the diameter symbol.

Answer: Straightness; maximum diameter 16.00 inches; minimum diameter 15.89 inches.

DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.16

Objective 1.3

Read a cylinder tolerance

What geometric characteristic is being given a tolerance, and what are the maximum and minimum cylinder lengths?

  1. Use the symbol chart: the circle with two parallel bars is cylindricity, here 0.25 inches.
  2. Maximum: the nominal length of 4.1 plus the tolerance of 0.05.
  3. Minimum: the nominal length of 4.1 minus the tolerance of 0.05.

Answer: Cylindricity; maximum length 4.15 inches; minimum length 4.05 inches.

DOE-HDBK-1016/2-93 PR-06 p.16
