RTD construction and operation

Objectives 1.1, 1.2, 1.3

"temperature is a measure of the kinetic energy of the material in question." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.1
"RTDs act somewhat like an electrical transducer, converting changes in temperature to voltage signals by the measurement of resistance." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.2

RTDs use pure metals or certain alloys that increase in resistance as temperature increases and decrease in resistance as temperature decreases. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.2

"RTD elements are normally constructed of platinum, copper, or nickel." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.2

These metals are best suited for RTD applications DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.2

"because of their linear resistance-temperature characteristics (as shown in Figure 1), their high coefficient of resistance, and their ability to withstand repeated temperature cycles." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.2
"RTD elements are usually long, spring-like wires surrounded by an insulator and enclosed in a sheath of metal." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.3
"The insulator prevents a short circuit between the wire and the metal sheath." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.3
"Inconel, a nickel-iron-chromium alloy, is normally used in manufacturing the RTD sheath because of its inherent corrosion resistance." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.3

The sheath reaches the temperature of the medium, and the platinum wire heats or cools, producing a proportional change in resistance. A precision measuring device, normally a bridge circuit, is calibrated to give the temperature. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.3

"The well protects the RTD from damage by the gas or liquid being measured." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.4

Thermocouples

Objectives 1.4, 1.5

"A thermocouple is constructed of two dissimilar metal wires joined at one end." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.5

When one end of each wire is connected to a measuring instrument, the thermocouple becomes a sensitive and highly accurate device. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.5

"The most important factor to be considered when selecting a pair of materials is the "thermoelectric difference" between the two materials." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.5

The leads are encased in a rigid metal sheath, and the measuring junction is normally formed at the bottom of the housing. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.6

"Magnesium oxide surrounds the thermocouple wires to prevent vibration that could damage the fine wires and to enhance heat transfer between the measuring junction and the medium surrounding the thermocouple." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.6
"The amount of current that will be produced is dependent on the temperature difference between the measurement and reference junction" DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.6
"Heating the measuring junction of the thermocouple produces a voltage that is greater than the voltage across the reference junction." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.7

The difference between the two voltages is proportional to the difference in temperature and can be measured on a voltmeter. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.7

"In order to convert the millivolt reading to its corresponding temperature, you must refer to tables" DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.7

RTDs are more accurate than thermocouples. Word: Temperature Detectors.docx, slide 1.23 Fill in the Blank Q4

A short or an open in a thermocouple causes it to fail to the reference junction temperature. Word: Temperature Detectors.docx, slide 1.27 Quiz

Uses and detector problems

Objectives 1.6, 1.7, 1.8

"temperature detectors are used to provide three basic functions: indication, alarm, and control." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.8

Temperatures may be displayed in a central location, with audible and visual alarms at preset limits. Control functions start or stop equipment, or cause a protective action. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.8

If key temperature sensing instruments fail, some applications use installed spare detectors or dual-element RTDs. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.8

"The dual-element RTD has two sensing elements of which only one is normally connected." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.8
"a contact pyrometer (portable thermocouple) may be used to obtain temperature readings on those pieces of equipment or systems that are accessible." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.8

If only the circuitry has failed, an external bridge circuit can be connected to the detector, and readings taken from the calibration curves. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.9

"Ambient temperature variations will affect the accuracy and reliability of temperature detection instrumentation." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.9

Ambient variations can directly affect the resistance of bridge components and of the thermocouple reference junction. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.9

"High humidity causes moisture to collect on the equipment." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.9
"This moisture can cause short circuits, grounds, and corrosion, which, in turn, may damage components." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.9

The bridge circuit

Objectives 1.9, 1.10

A basic bridge circuit has three known resistances, R1, R2, and R3 (variable), an unknown variable resistor Rx (the RTD), a voltage source, and a sensitive ammeter. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.10

"Resistors R1 and R2 are the ratio arms of the bridge." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.11
"R3 is a variable resistor known as the standard arm that is adjusted to match the unknown resistor." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.11
"The sensing ammeter visually displays the current that is flowing through the bridge circuit." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.11

R1 / R3 = R2 / Rx, so Rx = R2R3 / R1 DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.11

"When this condition exists, there is no current flow through the ammeter, and the bridge is said to be balanced." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.12

Most of the time the bridge is constructed so that R1 = R2. When this is the case and the bridge is balanced, DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.12

"then the resistance of Rx is the same as R3, or Rx = R3." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.12

When balance exists, R3 equals the unknown resistance even if the voltage source is unstable or not accurately known. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.12

Unbalanced and balanced bridges

Objectives 1.9, 1.10

"The unbalanced bridge circuit (Figure 9) uses a millivoltmeter that is calibrated in units of temperature that correspond to the RTD resistance." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.12

As the RTD's resistance changes, the voltage at points X and Y changes, and the meter detects the unequal division of current. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.13

"The balanced bridge circuit (Figure 10) uses a galvanometer to compare the RTD resistance with that of a fixed resistor." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.13
"A slidewire resistor is used to balance the arms of the bridge." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.13

The resistance of the slide wire is adjusted until the galvanometer indicates zero, and that value determines the temperature. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.13

Detection circuit and faults

Objectives 1.11, 1.12

The RTD is felt as resistance to the bridge network. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.14

"The bridge network converts this resistance to a DC voltage signal." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.14

The DC voltage is converted to an AC voltage. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.14

"The AC voltage is then amplified to a higher (usable) voltage that is used to drive a bi-directional motor." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.14
"The bi-directional motor positions the slider on the slidewire to balance the circuit resistance." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.14
"If the RTD becomes open in either the unbalanced and balanced bridge circuits, the resistance will be infinite, and the meter will indicate a very high temperature." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.14
"If it becomes shorted, resistance will be zero, and the meter will indicate a very low temperature." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.14

Compensation and calibration

Objectives 1.13

Changes in ambient temperature mean the circuitry must be compensated, in three ways. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.15

"The resistors that are used in the measuring circuitry are selected so that their resistance will remain constant over the range of temperature expected." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.15
"Temperature compensation is also accomplished through the design of the electronic circuitry" DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.15
"To compensate for this change, three and four wire RTD circuits are used." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.15

The same amount of lead wire is then used in both branches of the bridge, so the change in resistance is felt on both and is negated. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.15

"When calibrating the circuit, a precision resistor of known value is substituted for the resistance bulb" DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.15

Battery voltage is then adjusted until the meter indication is correct for the known resistance. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.15

An RTD in a bridge circuit becomes open. What does the operator see?

  1. An open means the resistance is infinite
  2. High resistance is read as high temperature

Balancing a bridge with equal ratio arms

A bridge has R1 = R2 and the ammeter reads zero. How does Rx compare with R3?

  1. Zero ammeter current means the bridge is balanced
  2. With R1 = R2, the arms are equal

Summary

  • The resistance of an RTD varies directly with temperature, and it is constructed of a fine, pure, metallic wire in an insulator and metal sheath. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.4
  • A thermocouple's measuring junction produces a voltage greater than the reference junction, and the difference is proportional to temperature difference. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.7
  • Temperature detectors are used for indication, alarm, and control functions. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.9
  • The bridge circuit is balanced when the sensing ammeter reads zero current. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.16
  • An open circuit reads very high temperature, and a short circuit reads very low temperature. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-01 p.16
