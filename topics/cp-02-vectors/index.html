Scalars and vectors

Objectives 1.1

"A scalar quantity is defined as a quantity that has magnitude only." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.1
"Typical examples of scalar quantities are time, speed, temperature, and volume." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.1

Density, mass, and energy are additional examples of scalar quantities. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.1

"A vector quantity is defined as a quantity that has both magnitude and direction." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.1
"Displacement, velocity, acceleration, and force are examples of vector quantities." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.2

A car moving at 50 miles per hour describes speed, a scalar. The same car traveling at 50 mph due east describes velocity, a vector, because it has magnitude and direction. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.2

Vectors have a starting point at one end (tail) and an arrow at the opposite end (head). DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.2

"The length of the line represents the magnitude of the vector, and the arrow represents the direction of the vector." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.2

Locating vectors on a graph

Objectives 1.1

Vector quantities are graphed on the rectangular coordinate system, a two-dimensional system that uses an x-axis and a y-axis. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.4

"The intersection of the axes is called the point of origin." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.4

Values to the right of the origin on the x-axis are positive and values to the left are negative. On the y-axis, values above the origin are positive and values below it are negative. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.4

It is very important to use the same units on both axes. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.4

Quadrant I is above and to the right of the origin, quadrant II is above and to the left, quadrant III is below and to the left, and quadrant IV is below and to the right. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.4

A vector's tail goes at the point of origin. Its head is located from (x,y) coordinates, or by rotating the line segment counterclockwise from the x-axis when the vector is described in degrees. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.5

Resultants and components

Objectives 1.1, 1.2

"A resultant vector is the result or sum of vector addition." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.8

A resultant is a single vector that can replace two or more vectors. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.15

Walking five miles east and then three miles east gives a resultant displacement of eight miles. Walking five miles east and then three miles west gives two miles east. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.8

Four miles east and then three miles north gives a resultant of 5 miles at 37 degrees north of east. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.9

"Components of a vector are vectors, which when added, yield the vector." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.10

Components can be obtained for any two non-parallel directions if the vectors are in the same plane. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.15

To find components graphically, plot the resultant on rectangular coordinates and project it to each axis. The length along the x-axis is Fx and the length along the y-axis is Fy. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.11

Trigonometry also gives the components, using the ratios of a right triangle: DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.12

sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.12

cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.12

tan θ = opposite / adjacent DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.12

For a resultant FR at angle θ, Fx = FR cos θ and Fy = FR sin θ. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.13

Graphic method of addition

Objectives 1.3

The graphic method uses linear graph paper, a ruler, a protractor, and a pencil, in five steps: DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.16

  • Plot the first vector on the x-y axes, with its tail at the origin and the same scale on both axes.
  • Draw the second vector with its tail at the head of the first, to scale and at the proper angle.
  • Add other vectors one at a time.
  • Draw and label the resultant, FR, from the origin to the head of the final vector.
  • Measure the displacement and angle of the resultant from the graph.

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.16

"Always start the tail of the new vector at the head of the previous vector." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.17
"Either one of these methods will provide fairly accurate results." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.16

Component addition method

Objectives 1.3

The method adds vector coordinates on a rectangular (x,y) coordinate system, in four steps: DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.20

  • Determine the x- and y-axes components of all original vectors.
  • Mathematically combine all x-axis components.
  • Mathematically combine all y-axis components.
  • The resulting (x,y) components are the components of the resultant.

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.20

A positive x component at 180° is equivalent to a negative x component at 0°, and a positive y component at 270° is equivalent to a negative y component at 90°. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.20

Objective 1.3Q592

Using component addition, what is the sum of F1 = (3,5) and F2 = (-1,2)?

The answer is C. Combine the x components: 3 + (-1) = 2. Combine the y components: 5 + 2 = 7. The resultant is (2,7). DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.20

Analytical method

Objectives 1.3

"If a high degree of accuracy is required, an analytical method using geometric and trigonometric functions is required." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.16

The graphic and component methods can be hard to use and time consuming, and their accuracy depends on the scale of the diagram. The analytical method can be simpler and far more accurate. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.23

"The Pythagorean Theorem states that in any right triangle, the square of the length of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the lengths of the other two sides." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.23

c² = a² + b² DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.23

A man walks 3 miles, turns 90°, and walks 4 more miles. His new location is 5 miles at 53° from his starting point. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.25

Sine values are positive in quadrants I and II. Cosine values are positive in quadrants I and IV. Tangent values are positive in quadrants I and III. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.24

A caution when solving for tan θ: DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.24

"calculators will specify angles in quadrants I and IV only." DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.24

Actual angles may be in quadrants II and III, so each problem should be analyzed graphically. Those angles may be obtained by adding or subtracting 180° from the calculated value. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.24

The model for several vectors has five steps: DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.27

  • Draw x and y coordinates and the forces from the point of origin.
  • Resolve each vector into its rectangular components.
  • Sum the x and y components.
  • Calculate the magnitude: FR = √(FRx² + FRy²).
  • Calculate the angle: θ = tan⁻¹(FRy / FRx).

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.27

Worked examples

Objective 1.2

Components by trigonometry

Determine the component vectors, Fx and Fy, for FR = 50 lbf at 53°.

  1. Fx = (50)(cos 53°) = (50)(0.6018) = 30 lbf on the x-axis.
  2. Fy = (50)(sin 53°) = (50)(0.7986) = 40 lbf on the y-axis.

Answer: Fx = 30 lbf at 0° and Fy = 40 lbf at 90°

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.13

Objective 1.2

Components in quadrant III

What are the component vectors, given FR = 80 lbf at 220°?

  1. Fx = (80)(cos 220°) = (80)(-0.766) = -61 lbf at 0°, or 61 lbf at 180°.
  2. Fy = (80)(sin 220°) = (80)(-0.6428) = -51 lbf at 90°, or 51 lbf at 270°.

Answer: Fx = 61 lbf at 180° and Fy = 51 lbf at 270°

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.15

Objective 1.3

Component addition

Find the resultant of F1 = (4,10), F2 = (-6,4), F3 = (2,-4), and F4 = (10,-2).

  1. Fx = 4 + (-6) + 2 + 10 = 10.
  2. Fy = 10 + 4 + (-4) + (-2) = 8.

Answer: FR = (10,8)

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.21

Objective 1.3

Analytical addition of three forces

Find the resultant of F1 = 90 lbf at 39°, F2 = 50 lbf at 120°, and F3 = 125 lbf at 250°.

  1. Resolve each force: F1x = 69.9, F1y = 56.6; F2x = -25, F2y = 43.3; F3x = -42.8, F3y = -117.5 (all lbf).
  2. Sum: FRx = 2.1 lbf and FRy = -17.6 lbf.
  3. Magnitude: FR = √((2.1)² + (-17.6)²) = 17.7 lbf.
  4. Angle: tan θ = -17.6/2.1 = -8.381, so θ = -83.2°. A negative angle means a clockwise rotation from the zero axis.

Answer: FR = 17.7 lbf at -83.2° or 276.8°

DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.29

Summary

  • Scalars have magnitude only. Vectors have both magnitude and direction. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.3
  • A resultant is a single vector that can replace two or more vectors, and components are determined graphically or analytically. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.15
  • In the graphic method, each new vector starts at the head of the preceding vector, and the resultant runs from the origin to the head of the final vector. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.19
  • In the component method, x components and y components are combined separately, and the results are the components of the resultant. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.22
  • The analytical method resolves vectors into rectangular components, sums them, and then calculates the magnitude and angle of the resultant. DOE-HDBK-1010-92 CP-02 p.30
