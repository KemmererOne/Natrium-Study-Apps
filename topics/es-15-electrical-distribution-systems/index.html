Distribution system terms

Objectives 1.1

"A single, or one-line diagram of a distribution system is a simple and easy-to-read diagram showing power supplies, loads, and major components in the distribution system" DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.1
"Commercial or utility power is electrical power that is provided by commercial generating systems to the facility." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.1
"Diesel-driven generators are the most economical and practical source of "standby power."" DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.1
"Failure-free power is accomplished by providing vital equipment with automatic switching between two or more power supplies so that interruption of power is minimized." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.3

Neutral grounding helps prevent accidents to personnel and damage to property. It also reduces the amount of insulation required for high-voltage lines. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.3

Voltage in distribution systems is classified into high, intermediate, and low voltage. The sources differ at exactly 600 volts. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.3

The handbook says: DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.3

"High voltage is voltage that is above 15,000 volts, intermediate voltage is voltage between 15,000 volts and 600 volts, and low voltage is voltage at 600 volts or less." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.3

The lesson plan, which notes its levels are from IEEE C84.1, lists these classes: Word: Breakers, Relays, and Disconnects Part 2.docx, slide 1.5 Circuit Breaker Classifications

  • >15,000 volts: above 15,000
  • 600 to 15,000 volts: between
  • <600 volts: below 600

Word: Breakers, Relays, and Disconnects Part 2.docx, slide 1.6 Circuit Breaker Classifications

So the handbook puts exactly 600 volts in low voltage, and the lesson plan puts it in the middle class. Both are shown here, and neither is tested. Word: Breakers, Relays, and Disconnects Part 2.docx, slide 1.6 Circuit Breaker Classifications

"Protective relays are designed to cause the prompt removal of any part of a power system that might cause damage or interfere with the effective and continuous operation of the rest of the system." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.3

A separate zone of protection is provided around each system element. A failure within a zone trips all breakers in that zone. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.4

"if there were no overlap of protective zones, a fault in a region between the two zones would result in no protective action at all." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.4

Fuses and protective relays

Objectives 1.2

"A fuse is a device that protects a circuit from an overcurrent condition only." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.5

An overcurrent or short-circuit current melts the fusible link and opens the circuit. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.5

Protective relays are aided by circuit breakers capable of disconnecting faulty components. They can protect against undesirable conditions such as undervoltage and underfrequency. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.3

There are only two operating principles for protective relays: electromagnetic attraction and electromagnetic induction. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.3

The lesson plan describes these relays. An undervoltage relay is Word: Breakers, Relays, and Disconnects Part 1.docx, slide 1.19 Breaker Protective Relays

"an adjustable device generally set to trip the breaker when voltage drops to 60 to 70 percent of normal value." Word: Breakers, Relays, and Disconnects Part 1.docx, slide 1.19 Breaker Protective Relays

An underfrequency relay is a Word: Breakers, Relays, and Disconnects Part 1.docx, slide 1.19 Breaker Protective Relays

"device that will trip the breaker when frequency drops below a preset value to protect frequency-sensitive loads on a system." Word: Breakers, Relays, and Disconnects Part 1.docx, slide 1.19 Breaker Protective Relays

A reverse-power relay: Word: Breakers, Relays, and Disconnects Part 1.docx, slide 1.19 Breaker Protective Relays

"device protects a generator from damage from motoring by tripping the generator output breaker." Word: Breakers, Relays, and Disconnects Part 1.docx, slide 1.19 Breaker Protective Relays

Circuit breakers

Objectives 1.3, 1.4, 1.5

"The purpose of a circuit breaker is to break the circuit and stop the current flow when the current exceeds a predetermined value without causing damage to the circuit or the circuit breaker." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.7

A circuit breaker trips to break the circuit and may be reset, while a fuse melts and must be replaced. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.7

If a breaker opens on a short circuit or overload, the handle goes to the trip-free position, midway between ON and OFF. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.8

"To reset a breaker in the TRIP FREE position, it must be positioned to the OPEN position first." Word: Breakers, Relays, and Disconnects Part 2.docx, slide 1.41 Resetting a Breaker

A bimetallic thermal trip element has an inverse-time characteristic. An extremely high current trips the breaker very rapidly. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.8

For moderate overload currents, it operates more slowly. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.9

An arc chute divides the arc into small segments and quenches it. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.9

Remote operation needs a control circuit. Control power is supplied by an AC source and rectified to DC. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.11

Turning the control switch to close energizes the closing relay. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.12

"The closing relay shuts an auxiliary contact, which energizes the closing coil (CC), which, in turn, shuts the circuit breaker" DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.12

When the breaker closes, the "b" contact opens and de-energizes the closing relay, and the "a" contact closes to enable the trip circuit. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.12

"This action energizes the trip coil (TC), which acts directly on the circuit breaker to release the latching mechanism that holds the circuit breaker closed." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.12
"The three most commonly-used automatic trip features for a circuit breaker are overcurrent (as discussed previously), underfrequency, and undervoltage." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.13
"Never operate a disconnect under load." Word: Breakers, Relays, and Disconnects Part 1.docx, slide 1.24 Knowledge Check

Motor controllers

Objectives 1.6, 1.7, 1.8

"The basic functions of a motor controller are to control and protect the operation of a motor." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.15

This includes starting and stopping the motor, and protecting it from overcurrent, undervoltage, and overheating. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.15

The two basic categories are the manual controller and the magnetic controller. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.15

"Manual controllers do NOT provide low voltage protection or low voltage release." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.15

When power fails, the manual controller contacts remain closed, and the motor restarts when power is restored. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.15

A magnetic controller automatically performs all operations in the proper sequence after the closure of a master switch. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.16

Overload devices protect the motor from overcurrent. Those commonly used are: DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.17

  • Fuses
  • Thermal overloads
  • Magnetic overloads

DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.17

An LVP controller de-energizes the motor in a low voltage condition and keeps it from re-starting automatically. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.18

Pushing START energizes coil M and closes the M and Ma contacts. The Ma contact keeps the circuit complete when the button is released. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.18

On low voltage, the M coil drops out and the contacts open. START must then be pushed to restart the motor. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.19

An LVR controller de-energizes the motor in a low voltage condition and restarts it when normal voltage is restored. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.19

"The LVRE controller maintains the motor across the line at all times." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.19

Wiring terms

Objectives 1.9

"Ampacity - the current in amperes that a conductor can carry continuously under the conditions of use without exceeding its temperature rating." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.22
"Bond - the permanent joining of metallic parts or circuits assuring electrical continuity and capacity to safely conduct any current likely to be imposed." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.22

A conductor is any wire, cable, or substance capable of carrying an electrical current. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.22

"Ground - a conducting connection, whether intentional or accidental, between a circuit or piece of equipment and the earth, or some body serving as earth; a place of zero electrical potential." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.22

Ground voltage is the voltage between any given conductor and any point at ground potential. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.22

"Leg - a current-carrying conductor intended to deliver power to or from a load normally at an electrical potential other than ground." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.22
"Neutral - a current-carrying conductor normally tied to ground so that the electrical potential is zero." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.22

Phase voltage is the greatest root mean square (effective) difference of potential between any two legs of the circuit. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.22

Wiring schemes

Objectives 1.10, 1.11

Single-phase loads can be connected to three-phase systems by two methods. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.22

A load connected from a phase leg to any ground point DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.23

"is referred to as a phase-to-ground scheme." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.23

A load connected between any two legs of the three-phase source is DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.23

"referred to as a phase-to-phase connection." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.23
"The only approved method of wiring single-phase power is the scheme commonly referred to as the 3-wire, single-phase Edison system." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.23

A center-tapped transformer with the center tap grounded provides half voltage (120 V) on either side or full voltage (240 V) across both sides. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.23

The 3-wire, three-phase Delta system is the simplest three-phase system, DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.24

Objective 1.4

Closing a breaker remotely

Follow the control circuit when the control switch is turned to close.

  1. The switch completes a path through the closing relay (CR).
  2. The closing relay shuts an auxiliary contact, which energizes the closing coil (CC).
  3. The closing coil shuts the breaker, which latches closed.
  4. The "b" contact opens, de-energizing the closing relay and closing coil.
  5. The "a" contact closes, enabling the trip circuit.

Answer: The breaker is closed and the trip circuit is enabled

DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.12

Objective 1.8

LVP controller on low voltage

A motor on an LVP controller sees a low voltage condition. What happens?

  1. The M coil drops out at a pre-determined voltage.
  2. The M and Ma contacts open.
  3. Normal voltage returns, but the Ma contact stays open.

Answer: The START button must be pushed to restart the motor

DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.19

Summary

  • Protective relays cause prompt removal of any part of a power system that might cause damage. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.3
  • The three most commonly-used automatic trip features for a circuit breaker are overcurrent, underfrequency, and undervoltage. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.14
  • A motor controller controls and protects the operation of a motor. Its protective features are fuses, thermal overloads, and magnetic overloads. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.20
  • Single-phase loads connect to a three-phase system phase-to-ground or phase-to-phase. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-15 p.23
