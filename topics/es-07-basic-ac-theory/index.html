The simple AC generator

Objectives 1.1, 1.2

"The elementary AC generator (Figure 1) consists of a conductor, or loop of wire in a magnetic field that is produced by an electromagnet." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.1
"The two ends of the loop are connected to slip rings, and they are in contact with two brushes." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.1

When the loop rotates it cuts magnetic lines of force, first in one direction and then the other. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.1

In the vertical position, there is no voltage induced in the loop: DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.1

"the coil sides are moving parallel to the field and do not cut magnetic lines of force." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.1

Voltage and current increase to a maximum when the coil is horizontal to the magnetic lines of force, at 90°. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.2

"The horizontal coil is moving perpendicular to the field and is cutting the greatest number of magnetic lines of force." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.2

As the coil continues to turn, voltage and current decrease to zero at 180°. In the other half revolution, an equal voltage is produced with the polarity reversed. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.2

"rotation of the coil through 360° results in an AC sine wave output." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.3

Cycle, frequency, and angular velocity

Objectives 1.3

As the generator coil rotates 360°, the output voltage goes through one complete cycle. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.5

"The time it takes for the generator to complete one cycle is called the period, and the number of cycles per second is called the frequency (measured in hertz)." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.5

Angular velocity is expressed in radians per second, and 2π radians is a full revolution. One radian equals 57.3 degrees. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.9

"One cycle of the sine wave is generated when the coil rotates 2π radians." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.9

ω = 2πf DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.10

e = Emax sin (ωt) DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.8

Effective and average values

Objectives 1.4, 1.5

The peak value is the maximum voltage or current for an AC sine wave. The peak-to-peak value is the range spanned by the sine wave. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.5

The value most commonly used for AC is the effective value. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.5

"Effective value of AC is the amount of AC that produces the same heating effect as an equal amount of DC." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.5

One ampere effective value of AC produces the same amount of heat in a conductor, in a given time, as one ampere of DC. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.5

The effective value is the root of the mean square of the currents, so it is known as the RMS value. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.5

"Effective value (RMS) = peak value x 0.707" DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.5
"Average value = peak value x 0.637" DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.5

Other conversions: peak = average x 1.57, RMS = average x 1.11, peak = RMS x 1.414, and average = RMS x 0.9. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.5

"The values of current (I) and voltage (E) that are normally encountered are assumed to be RMS values" DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.5

Knowledge check: Effective and average values

Objective 1.4Q490

The effective (RMS) value of an AC current equals:

The answer is B. RMS values equate AC to DC equivalents. Effective current is 0.707 Imax. Average current is 0.636 Imax. DOE-1011-V3 (DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92), Voltage, Current, and Frequency, ES-07 p. 10

Phase relationships

Objectives 1.6

"Phase angle is the fraction of a cycle, in degrees, that has gone by since a voltage or current has passed through a given value." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.7

The given value is normally zero. Phase difference describes two voltages of the same frequency that pass through zero in the same direction at different times. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.7

"The voltage e1 is said to lead e2 by 60 electrical degrees, or it can be said that e2 lags e1 by 60 electrical degrees." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.7
"If the phase difference between two currents, two voltages, or a voltage and a current is zero degrees, they are said to be "in-phase."" DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.8

If the phase difference is an amount other than zero, they are out-of-phase. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.8

Worked examples

Objective 1.5

RMS from peak

The peak value of voltage in an AC circuit is 200 V. What is the RMS value?

  1. E = 0.707 Emax.
  2. E = 0.707 (200 V).

Answer: 141.4 V

DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.6

Objective 1.5

Average from peak

The peak current in an AC circuit is 10 amps. What is the average value?

  1. Iav = 0.637 Imax.
  2. Iav = 0.637 (10 amps).

Answer: 6.37 amps

DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.7

Objective 1.2

Induced EMF at an angle

What is the induced EMF in a coil producing a maximum EMF of 120 V when the angle from reference is 45°?

  1. e = Emax sin θ.
  2. e = 120 V (sin 45°).

Answer: 84.84 V

DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.8

Angular velocity

The frequency of a 120 V AC circuit is 60 Hz. Find the angular velocity.

  1. ω = 2πf.
  2. ω = 2 (3.14) (60 Hz).

Summary

  • A simple generator is a conductor loop turning in a magnetic field, cutting across the magnetic lines of force. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.3
  • Each half turn of rotation produces one half of the cycle of AC generation. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.3
  • Radians/second is the velocity the loop turns, hertz is the number of cycles in one second, and period is the time to complete one cycle. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.10
  • I = 0.707 Imax is the effective current, and E = 0.707 Emax is the effective voltage. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.10
  • Phase angle is used to compare two wave forms by degrees of rotation. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-07 p.10
