The rotating magnetic field

Objectives 1.1

"The principle of operation for all AC motors relies on the interaction of a revolving magnetic field created in the stator by AC current, with an opposing magnetic field either induced on the rotor or provided by a separate DC current source." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.1

The magnetic field of one phase depends on the current through that phase. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.1

"Since the currents in the three windings are 120° out of phase, the magnetic fields produced will also be 120° out of phase." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.1

The three magnetic fields combine to produce one field, which acts upon the rotor. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.1

At the end of one cycle of alternating current, the magnetic field will have shifted through 360°, or one revolution. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.2

"by applying three-phase AC to three windings symmetrically spaced around a stator, a rotating magnetic field is generated." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.4

Torque, slip, and speed

Objectives 1.2, 1.3, 1.4

The rotating magnetic field cuts the bars of the rotor and induces a current in them due to generator action. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.5

This induced current produces a magnetic field around the rotor conductors, which tries to line up with the magnetic field of the stator. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.5

Since the stator field is rotating continuously, DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.5

"the rotor cannot line up with, or lock onto, the stator field" DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.5

If the rotor turned at the same speed as the stator field, there would be no relative motion and no induced EMF. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.5

"The rotor must, therefore, rotate at some speed less than that of the stator if relative motion is to exist between the two." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.5
"The percentage difference between the speed of the rotor and the speed of the rotating magnetic field is called slip." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.5

% Slip = (NS - NR) / NS x 100% DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.6

NS = 120 f / P DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.6

Torque depends on the strength of the interacting rotor and stator fields and the phase relationship between them. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.7

"torque is directly proportional to the rotor current." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.7

As slip increases from zero to about 10%, torque increases linearly. Torque reaches a maximum value at about 25% slip. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.7

"The maximum value of torque is called the breakdown torque of the motor." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.7
"The typical induction motor breakdown torque varies from 200 to 300% of full-load torque." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.8

Starting torque is the value of torque at 100% slip and is normally 150 to 200% of full-load torque. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.7

An AC induction motor has a synchronous speed of 1800 rpm and a rotor speed of 1746 rpm. What is the percent slip?

The answer is B. Slip = (1800 - 1746) / 1800 x 100% = 3%. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.5

What happens if an induction motor's load is increased beyond the point of maximum torque?

During normal operation of an induction motor, what is torque directly proportional to?

Single-phase and induction motors

Objectives 1.5, 1.9

"The induction motor is the most commonly used AC motor in industrial applications because of its simplicity, rugged construction, and relatively low manufacturing costs." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.9

The rotor is a self-contained unit with no external connections. The most commonly used type is the squirrel-cage rotor. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.9

If two stator windings of unequal impedance are spaced 90 electrical degrees apart and connected in parallel to a single-phase source, the field produced will appear to rotate. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.11

"In a split-phase motor, a starting winding is utilized." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.11

The starting winding has a higher resistance and lower reactance than the main winding, so the current in the main winding lags the starting winding current. This phase difference provides a rotating magnetic field to produce a starting torque. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.11

At 70 to 80% of synchronous speed, a centrifugal switch on the motor shaft opens. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.11

"Single-phase motors are used for very small commercial applications such as household appliances and buffers." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.11

How does a split-phase motor produce a starting torque?

Synchronous motors

Objectives 1.6, 1.7, 1.8, 1.9

A synchronous motor is excited by an external DC source, so it requires slip rings and brushes. Its rotor locks into step with the rotating magnetic field. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.12

"A synchronous motor is not a self-starting motor because torque is only developed when running at synchronous speed" DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.12

It may be started by a DC motor on a common shaft. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.13

"Synchronous motors are more often started by means of a squirrel-cage winding embedded in the face of the rotor poles." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.13

The motor is then started as an induction motor and brought to about 95% of synchronous speed, at which time direct current is applied. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.13

As load is applied, the rotor pole shifts backward relative to the stator pole, with no change in speed. The angle between them is the torque angle. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.13

"The maximum value of torque that a motor can develop without losing synchronism is called its pull-out torque." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.13

For a constant load, the power factor can be varied from leading to lagging by adjusting the DC field excitation. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.14

When field excitation is increased, the motor operates at a leading power factor. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.14

"If we reduce field excitation, the motor will operate at a lagging power factor" DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.14
"Synchronous motors are used to accommodate large loads and to improve the power factor of transformers in large industrial complexes." DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.14

Objective 1.8Q438

At the same load, increasing field excitation on a synchronous motor makes it operate at:

Worked examples

Objective 1.3

Percent slip

A two pole, 60 Hz AC induction motor has a full load speed of 3554 rpm. What is the percent slip at full load?

  1. NS = 120 f / P = 120 (60 Hz) / 2 = 3600 rpm.
  2. Slip = (3600 - 3554 rpm) / 3600 rpm x 100%.

Answer: 1.3%

DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.6

Summary

  • Three-phase voltage applied to the stator produces a magnetic field that shifts through one revolution each cycle. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.8

  • A synchronous motor is not self-starting. It operates at a leading power factor when field excitation is increased, and at a lagging power factor when it is reduced. DOE-HDBK-1011/4-92 ES-12 p.15
