Atoms and subatomic particles

Objectives 1.1, 1.2

"Protons are particles that have a positive charge, have about the same mass as a hydrogen atom, and exist in the nucleus of an atom." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.10
"Neutrons are particles that have no electrical charge, have about the same mass as a hydrogen atom, and exist in the nucleus of an atom." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.10
"Electrons are particles that have a negative charge, have a mass about eighteen hundred times smaller than the mass of a hydrogen atom, and exist in orbital shells around the nucleus of an atom." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.10
  • Neutron: nucleus, no charge, 1.008665 amu
  • Proton: nucleus, +1, 1.007277 amu
  • Electron: shells around the nucleus, -1, 0.0005486 amu

DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.4

"The mass of a neutron is slightly larger than the mass of a proton (by more than the mass of an electron), which means the Beta-minus decay does not require any energy to undergo." Word: Neutrons.docx, slide 1.5 Neutrons and Protons
"The Bohr model of the atom consists of a dense nucleus of protons and neutrons (nucleons) surrounded by electrons traveling in discrete orbits at fixed distances from the nucleus." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.10
"When an electron moves from one allowed orbit to another allowed orbit, the energy difference between the two states is emitted or absorbed in the form of a single quantum of radiant energy called a photon." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.3

Nuclides and notation

Objectives 1.3, 1.4

"Nuclides are atoms that contain a particular number of protons and neutrons." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.10

Forces in the nucleus

Objectives 1.5

Three forces act in the nucleus: gravitational, electrostatic, and nuclear. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.9

Gravity is a very weak attraction between all nucleons and is negligible compared to the other forces. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.10

"The electrostatic force is a relatively long-range, strong, repulsive force that acts between the positively charged protons." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.10
"The nuclear force is a relatively short-range attractive force between all nucleons." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.10
"In stable atoms, the attractive and repulsive forces in the nucleus balance." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.9

If the forces do not balance, the atom cannot be stable, and the nucleus emits radiation to reach a more stable configuration. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.9

Mass defect and binding energy

Objectives 1.7, 1.8

"Mass defect is the difference between the mass of the atom and the sum of the masses of its constituent parts." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.21

Δm = [ Z(mp + me) + (A - Z)mn ] - m(atom) DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.17

"Binding energy is the amount of energy that must be supplied to a nucleus to completely separate its nuclear particles." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.21

Binding energy is the energy equivalent of the mass defect. The mass defect was converted to binding energy when the nucleus formed. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.18

"Binding energy can be calculated by multiplying the mass defect by the factor of 931.5 MeV per amu." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.21

Use full accuracy in the masses, because rounding to three or four significant digits gives a calculated mass defect of zero. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.17

Nucleons in the nucleus also occupy energy states, and an excited nucleus drops toward its lowest energy by emitting a gamma ray. Nuclear energy levels are typically measured in MeV. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.20

Modes of radioactive decay

Objectives 2.1, 2.3

"Alpha decay is the emission of an alpha particle (2 protons and 2 neutrons) from an unstable nucleus." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.29

The daughter has an atomic number 2 less and a mass number 4 less than the parent. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.29

"Beta-minus decay effectively converts a neutron to a proton and an electron, which is immediately ejected from the nucleus." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.29

The daughter's atomic number rises by 1 and the mass number is unchanged. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.29

"Beta-plus decay effectively converts a proton to a neutron and a positron, which is immediately ejected from the nucleus." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.29

The daughter's atomic number falls by 1 and the mass number is unchanged. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.29

"In electron capture, the nucleus absorbs an electron from the innermost orbit." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.29

That electron combines with a proton to form a neutron. Electron capture and positron emission give the same daughter and compete with each other. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.25

"Internal conversion occurs when a gamma ray, emitted by the nucleus as it goes from the excited state to the ground state, interacts with one of the innermost electrons of the same atom." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.29

The electron is ejected from the atom. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.29

"An isomeric transition is the decay of an excited nucleus to a lower-energy level by the emission of a gamma ray." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.29
"Many modes of radioactive decay result in a daughter nuclide that has an energy level above the ground state." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.29
"This excitation energy is usually released immediately in the form of a gamma ray." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.29

Activity, half-life and decay

Objectives 2.5, 2.6, 2.7, 2.8, 2.9

"Radioactivity is the property of certain nuclides of spontaneously emitting particles or gamma radiation." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.30
"This probability per unit time that an atom of a nuclide will decay is known as the radioactive decay constant," DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.30

A = λN (activity = decay constant x number of atoms) DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.31

A curie (Ci) is 3.7 x 10^10 disintegrations per second, and 1 curie = 3.7 x 10^10 becquerels. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.31

"A becquerel (Bq) is a unit of radioactivity equal to 1 disintegration per second." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.42

A = A₀ e^(-λt) DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.32

"The radioactive half-life is defined as the amount of time required for the activity to decrease to one-half of its original value." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.32

t½ = ln 2 / λ = 0.693 / λ DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.32

"The number of atoms existing after 5 to 7 half-lives can usually be assumed to be negligible." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.33
"The decay of the activity of a single nuclide on a logarithmic scale will plot as a straight line because the decay is exponential." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.35

With more than one nuclide, total activity is the sum of the individual activities. The shortest-lived nuclide dominates first and the longest-lived dominates last. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.37

Why uranium-235 and uranium-238 differ

Objectives 4.7

"Uranium-235 fissions with thermal neutrons because the binding energy released by the absorption of a neutron is greater than the critical energy for fission." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.55
"The binding energy released by uranium-238 absorbing a neutron is less than the critical energy, so additional kinetic energy must be possessed by the neutron for fission to be possible." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.55

The reason is the odd-even effect: adding a neutron to a nucleus with an odd number of neutrons gives an appreciably higher binding energy. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.51

For fission to be possible, the change in binding energy plus the neutron's kinetic energy must equal or exceed the critical energy. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.51

Objective 1.8

Mass defect of lithium-7

Calculate the mass defect for lithium-7. The mass of lithium-7 is 7.016003 amu.

  1. Δm = Z(mp + me) + (A - Z)mn - m(atom)
  2. Δm = 3(1.007826 amu) + (7 - 3)(1.008665 amu) - 7.016003 amu

Answer: Δm = 0.0421335 amu

DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.18

Objective 1.8

Binding energy of uranium-235

One uranium-235 atom has a mass of 235.043924 amu. Calculate its mass defect and binding energy.

  1. Δm = 92(1.007826 amu) + (235 - 92)(1.008665 amu) - 235.043924 amu = 1.91517 amu
  2. B.E. = 1.91517 amu x 931.5 MeV/amu

Answer: B.E. = 1784 MeV

DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.19

Objective 2.7,2.8

Decay of californium-252

A sample holds 4.78 x 10^16 atoms of californium-252, with a half-life of 2.638 years. How many atoms remain in 12 years?

  1. λ = 0.693 / 2.638 years = 0.263 per year
  2. N = N₀ e^(-λt) = (4.78 x 10^16) e^(-(0.263/yr)(12 yr))

Answer: N = 2.04 x 10^15 atoms

DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.35

Objective 2.7,2.8

Nitrogen-16 activity

Nitrogen-16 has a half-life of 7.13 seconds and an initial activity of 142 curies. What is the activity at 20 seconds?

  1. λ = 0.693 / 7.13 seconds = 0.0972 per second
  2. A = A₀ e^(-λt) = (142 Ci) e^(-(0.0972/s)(20 s))

Answer: A = 20.3 Ci

DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.36

Objective 4.10

Fission energy from the binding energy curve

For uranium-235 splitting into rubidium-93 and cesium-140, use BE/A from the curve: Rb-93 8.7 MeV, Cs-140 8.4 MeV, U-235 7.6 MeV.

  1. Rb-93: 8.7 MeV x 93 = 809 MeV
  2. Cs-140: 8.4 MeV x 140 = 1176 MeV
  3. U-235: 7.6 MeV x 235 = 1786 MeV
  4. ΔBE = 809 MeV + 1176 MeV - 1786 MeV

Answer: ΔBE = 199 MeV

DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.59

Objective 4.9

Fission energy from the mass difference

The reactants total 236.052589 amu and the products total 235.85208 amu. Find the instantaneous energy.

  1. Mass difference = 236.052589 amu - 235.85208 amu = 0.200509 amu
  2. E = 0.200509 amu x 931.5 MeV/amu

Answer: E(inst) = 186.8 MeV

DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.59

Summary

  • Atoms have protons and neutrons in the nucleus and electrons in shells. Z is the proton count, A is the nucleon count, and neutrons equal A - Z. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.10
  • The nuclear force holds nucleons together against electrostatic repulsion. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.10
  • Mass defect converts to binding energy at 931.5 MeV per amu. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.21
  • Decay modes include alpha, beta-minus, beta-plus, electron capture, internal conversion and isomeric transition. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.29
  • Activity is A = λN, and half-life is 0.693 / λ. Sodium-24 in reactor coolant is the handbook's example of radioactive equilibrium. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.42
  • Neutrons scatter elastically or inelastically, or are absorbed by radiative capture, particle ejection or fission. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.47
  • Fission occurs when excitation energy exceeds critical energy. Fertile material can be converted or bred into fissile material. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.55
  • Fission releases about 200 MeV, and about seven percent appears later as decay energy. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.62
  • Alpha, beta and positrons ionize directly. Gammas interact by the photoelectric effect, Compton scattering and pair production. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-01 p.67
