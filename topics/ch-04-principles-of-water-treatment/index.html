Why water is treated

Objectives 1.1

Water as found in nature contains impurities that must be controlled or eliminated. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.1

"To minimize corrosion, which is enhanced by impurities." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.1
"To minimize radiation levels in a reactor facility." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.1
"To minimize fouling of heat transfer surfaces." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.1

Deaeration strips dissolved gases, filtration removes insoluble solids, and ion exchange replaces undesirable ions with acceptable ones. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.2

Ion exchange and resins

Objectives 1.2, 1.3

"An ion exchange is the reversible exchange of ions between a liquid and a solid." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.3

Ion exchange devices are commonly called demineralizers. To demineralize is DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.3

"the process whereby impurities present in the incoming fluid (water) are removed by exchanging impure ions with H+ and OH- ions, resulting in the formation of pure water." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.3
"There are two general types of ion exchange resins: those that exchange positive ions, called cation resins, and those that exchange negative ions, called anion resins." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.4

A cation is an ion with a positive charge, and an anion is an ion with a negative charge. Both resins belong to a group of compounds called polymers, DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.4

"which are extremely large molecules that are formed by the combination of many molecules of one or two compounds in a repeating structure that produces long chains." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.4

Resin beads are very small and look like damp, transparent, amber sand. Cross-linked polystyrene is the polymer commonly used. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.4

A mixed-bed demineralizer is a vessel containing a uniform mixture of cation and anion resins. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.4

"The ratio is normally 2 parts cation resin to 3 parts anion resin." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.4

A resin is usually named by the ion on its active sites, such as hydrogen form or hydroxyl form. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.7

Affinity and the ion exchanger

Objectives 1.2, 1.4

"The term affinity is often used to describe the attraction between a resin and a given ion." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.7

For cations, Na+ has a higher affinity than H+, and H+ higher than Li+. For anions, Cl- has a higher affinity than OH-. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.7

"Because of the different densities of anion and cation resins, the flow of solution (impure water) is from top to bottom." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.9
"the efficiency for removal of impurities is greater in a mixed-bed resin than a layered arrangement." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.9
"The ion exchange process is reversible." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.10

If the capacity of the resin is exceeded, previously removed substances may return to the solution. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.10

Decontamination factor

Objectives 1.2, 1.4

Decontamination factor (DF) is DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.10

"which is defined as a ratio of the concentration (or activity) of the fluid at the inlet compared to the concentration (or activity) at the effluent" DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.10

DF = influent concentration, conductivity, or radioactivity / effluent concentration, conductivity, or radioactivity DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.10

To find percent of impurities removed, divide the amount removed by the amount entering. Word: Demineralizers and Ion Exchangers.docx, slide 1.29 Decontamination Factor

Objective 1.4Q252

How is the decontamination factor (DF) of a demineralizer calculated?

The answer is B. The DF is inlet conductivity divided by outlet conductivity. DOE-1015-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93), Water Treatment Processes, CH-04 p. 13

On a resin history curve, what does point X mark?

The answer is A. Resin performance is monitored with a history curve, with the resin considered exhausted at point X. DOE-1015-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93), Water Treatment Processes, CH-04 p. 11

An ion exchanger has 15 ppm chloride at the inlet and 0.1 ppm at the outlet. What is the DF?

Mixed-bed reactions

Objectives 1.5

H-R + R-OH + Na+ + Cl- → Na-R + R-Cl + H+ + OH- DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.12

H-R + R-OH + Ca++ + SO4-- → Ca-R + R-SO4 + H+ + OH- DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.12

"In the reaction with NaCl, both Na+ and Cl- ions are removed from solution and replaced by H+ and OH- ions, respectively" DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.12

H+ and OH- ions from the resin mostly combine to form water. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.12

"By this process, the original NaCl solution becomes pure water." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.12

Dissolved gases and suspended solids

Objectives 1.6, 1.7

Dissolved gases are removed by aeration, deaeration, or scavengers. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.27

In aeration, water is sprayed through a tower or air is blown through it, and DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.17

"the CO2 is "stripped" from the water and vented to the atmosphere." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.17

In deaeration, water is stored in vented tanks. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.17

"The water is heated to a temperature sufficient for slow boiling to occur." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.17

Scavengers such as hydrogen, hydrazine, and morpholine combine with the gas and remove it chemically. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.27

Filtration removes insoluble solids by passing water through a porous filter medium. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.20

"Numerous materials are used as filter media and include sand, activated charcoal, anthracite, diatomaceous earth, and to some extent resin in an ion exchanger." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.20
"Sand is not normally used in nuclear applications because of the silicate ion" DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.20

Pressure filters are the most widely used, because they can be installed in a pressurized system. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.20

"(90% or less for filtration versus approximately 100% for most ion exchange reactions)." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.21

pH control and resin malfunctions

Objectives 1.8, 1.9

A hydroxyl form anion resin and a cation resin in a form other than hydrogen can release hydroxyl ions to help control pH. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.21

"Thus, the resin serves both to purify the coolant and to help maintain the pH of the coolant by releasing NH4OH." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.21
"Channeling is a condition in which the resin allows a direct flow of water through the ion exchanger." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.22
"Channeling most often results from improper filling of the ion exchanger with resin." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.22
"Eventually, as the exchange zone approaches the end of the column, small amounts of NH4 + begin to appear in the effluent. The point at which this occurs is called breakthrough." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.24
"This condition is called exhaustion and indicates that essentially all the exchange capacity of the resin has been used." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.24

The first indication of depletion is breakthrough. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.27

Overheated anion resin releases trimethylamine (TMA), and overheated cation resin destroys its exchange capacity and produces an acid. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.25

  • Very Pure: 1 µmhos/cm maximum, 0.2-0.5 mg/l

Summary

  • There are three general reasons to treat water for its impurities. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.2
  • Decontamination factor defines the effectiveness of the ion exchange process. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.13
  • Dissolved gases are removed by aeration, deaeration, or scavengers. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.27
  • Suspended solids are removed by mechanical filters. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.27
  • When impurity concentration is the same before and after the resin, the resin has reached exhaustion. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-04 p.27
