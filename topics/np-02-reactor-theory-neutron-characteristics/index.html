Why source neutrons matter

Objectives 1.1

Source neutrons are neutrons produced by reactions other than neutron-induced fission. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.1

"Source neutrons are important because they ensure that the neutron population remains high enough to allow a visible indication of neutron level on the most sensitive monitoring instruments while the reactor is shutdown and during the startup sequence." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.1

This verifies instrument operability and allows monitoring of neutron population changes. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.1

"Spontaneous fission of U-238 from the fuel provides more then enough neutrons to start a chain reaction." Word: Source Neutrons.docx, slide 1.9 Knowledge Check #1
"Intrinsic neutron sources are those neutron-producing reactions that always occur in reactor materials." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.1
"An installed neutron source is an assembly placed in or near the reactor for the sole purpose of producing source neutrons." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.3

Objective 1.1Q244

Why are neutron sources installed in a reactor?

The answer is C. The lesson plan says this is exactly why neutron sources are installed. Spontaneous fission of U-238 already provides more than enough neutrons to start a chain reaction. DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Reactor Operation, NP-04 p. 24

Objective 1.1Q566

What are installed neutron sources?

The answer is B. Installed neutron sources are materials or components placed in the reactor specifically for the purpose of producing source neutrons. Intrinsic sources are materials in the reactor for other purposes. DOE-1019-V1 (DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93), Neutron Sources, NP-02 p. 4

Installed neutron sources

Objectives 1.3

Intrinsic sources can be weak or depend on recent power history, so many reactors have installed sources to keep shutdown neutron levels detectable. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.3

"Spontaneous fission of californium-252 results in fission fragments and free neutrons." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.4

Californium-252's drawbacks for some applications are high cost and a short half-life of 2.65 years. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.3

Atom density and cross sections

Objectives 2.1, 2.2

"The atom density is the number of atoms of a given type per unit volume of the material." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.6

N = ρ NA / M (N in atoms/cm³) DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.6

"The probability of a particular reaction occurring between a neutron and a nucleus is called the microscopic cross section" DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.7
"The microscopic cross section may also be regarded as the effective area the nucleus presents to the neutron for the particular reaction." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.7

Microscopic cross sections are measured in barns, where 1 barn = 10^-24 cm². DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.17

"The macroscopic cross section is the probability of a given reaction occurring per unit travel of the neutron." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.8

Σ = Nσ (Σ in cm⁻¹) DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.8

The total microscopic cross section is the sum of absorption and scattering. Absorption divides into fission and capture. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.8

Knowledge check: Atom density and cross sections

Objective 2.1Q504

What does the microscopic cross section represent?

The answer is D. Microscopic cross section is the probability of a given reaction occurring between a neutron and a nucleus. Macroscopic cross section is that probability per unit length of travel. DOE-1019-V1 (DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93), Nuclear Cross Sections and Neutron Flux, NP-02 p. 17

Report a problem
Objective 2.1Q506

If atomic density increases, the probability of neutron interaction:

The answer is B. If atomic density increases, the number of target nuclei per unit volume increases and the probability of interaction increases. DOE-1019-V1 (DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93), Neutron Sources, NP-02 p. 8

Report a problem
Objective 2.2Q1559

How is macroscopic cross section related to microscopic cross section?

The answer is C. The macroscopic cross section is the atom density times the microscopic cross section. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.8

Absorption cross section and neutron energy

Objectives 2.3, 2.4, 2.5

"At low neutron energies (<1 eV) the cross section is inversely proportional to the neutron velocity." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.17
"Resonance absorption occurs when the sum of the kinetic energy of the neutron and its binding energy is equal to an allowed nuclear energy level of the nucleus." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.17
"This is because heavy nuclei are complex and have more possible configurations and corresponding energy states." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.10
"Light nuclei, being less complex, have fewer possible energy states and fewer resonances that are sparsely distributed at higher energy levels." DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.10

For higher neutron energies, the absorption cross section steadily decreases as the energy increases. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.10

"This is called the "fast neutron region."" DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.10

A fast reactor works at the high energy end of this curve, as the slides below show. NRC slides ML19148A793, slide 3

"One-group cross sections are significantly reduced in fast system" NRC slides ML19148A793, slide 12
"Threshold fission at ~1 MeV (~10% of total fission rate in a fast reactor)" NRC slides ML19148A793, slide 8

  • Uranium-238: 0.0148
  • Uranium-233: 0.0026

DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.30

Objectives 4.1, 4.2, 4.3

"In SFR, moderation is avoided – no thermal neutrons; most fissions occur at higher energies (>10 keV)" NRC slides ML19148A793, slide 3
"Neutron leakage is increased (>20% in typical designs, reactivity coefficient)" NRC slides ML19148A801, slide 3

The thermal reactor spectrum and its region-by-region shape stay off this page, so objectives 4.2 (part b) and 4.3 are marked for review. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.34

Worked examples

Objective 2.1

Atom density of aluminum

A block of aluminum has a density of 2.699 g/cm³ and a gram atomic weight of 26.9815 g. Calculate the atom density.

  1. N = ρ NA / M
  2. N = (2.699 g/cm³)(6.022 x 10^23 atoms/mole) / (26.9815 g/mole)

Answer: N = 6.024 x 10^22 atoms/cm³

DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.7

Objective 2.7,2.6Needs sodium review

Cross sections and mean free paths of an alloy

An alloy has 5.64 x 10^22 aluminum atoms/cm³ and 2.85 x 10^21 silicon atoms/cm³. Aluminum has σa = 0.23 barns and σs = 1.49 barns. Silicon has σa = 0.16 barns and σs = 2.20 barns. These are the handbook's thermal values, used here only to show the method.

  1. Σa = (5.64 x 10^22)(0.23 x 10^-24 cm²) + (2.85 x 10^21)(0.16 x 10^-24 cm²) = 0.0134 cm⁻¹
  2. Σs = (5.64 x 10^22)(1.49 x 10^-24 cm²) + (2.85 x 10^21)(2.20 x 10^-24 cm²) = 0.0903 cm⁻¹
  3. λa = 1 / Σa and λs = 1 / Σs

Answer: λa = 74.3 cm and λs = 11.1 cm

DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.13

Objective 3.5Needs sodium review

Average neutron generation time

A reactor has a prompt generation time of 5 x 10^-5 seconds, a delayed generation time of 12.5 seconds, and β = 0.0065. These are the handbook's values for a thermal system. Calculate the average generation time.

  1. Delayed neutrons dominate the average: 0.0065 x 12.5 seconds is about 0.081 seconds
  2. The prompt term is very small beside it

Answer: About 0.0813 seconds, compared with about 5 x 10^-5 seconds on prompt neutrons alone

DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.31

Summary

  • Source neutrons keep neutron level visible on instruments. Sources are intrinsic or installed. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.4
  • Microscopic cross section is a probability per nucleus, and macroscopic cross section is Σ = Nσ. Mean free path is 1 / Σ. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.17
  • Reaction rate is R = φΣ. Over several days, flux is directly proportional to reactor power. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.22
  • In a fast reactor, moderation is avoided and most fissions occur above 10 keV. NRC slides ML19148A793, slide 3
  • Delayed neutrons come from precursors, and they make power changes controllable. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.32
  • Prompt neutrons average about 2 MeV. A fast reactor's spectrum has the same shape as the prompt spectrum. DOE-HDBK-1019/1-93 NP-02 p.37
