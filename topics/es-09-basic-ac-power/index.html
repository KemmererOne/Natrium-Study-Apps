The power triangle

Objectives 1.1

In AC circuits, current and voltage are normally out of phase, so not all the power produced by the generator can be used to accomplish work. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.1

"Apparent power (S) is the power delivered to an electrical circuit." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.2

Apparent power is measured in volt-amperes (VA). DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.2

S = I²Z = ITE DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.2

"Total Power supplied to an electrical circuit. It is a combination of the true and reactive power, measured in Volt Amps (VA)." Word: AC Generator Theory and Operation.docx, slide 1.23 Power Factor Recall
"True power (P) is the power consumed by the resistive loads in an electrical circuit." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.3

True power is measured in watts. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.3

P = I²R = EI cos θ DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.3

Reactive power (Q) is expressed in volt-amperes-reactive (VAR). It comes from the expansion and collapse of magnetic and electrostatic fields. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.3

Q = I²X = EI sin θ DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.3

"Unlike true power, reactive power is not useful power because it is stored in the circuit itself." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.3
"Does not perform any useful work but is essential for maintaining magnetic and electric fields through inductors and capacitors." Word: AC Generator Theory and Operation.docx, slide 1.23 Power Factor Recall
"The total power delivered by the source is the apparent power." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.4

Part of the apparent power, called true power, is dissipated by the circuit resistance as heat. The rest is returned to the source by the circuit inductance and capacitance. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.4

On the power triangle, which side represents apparent power?

The answer is A. Figure 1 shows apparent power S as the hypotenuse, between true power P and reactive power Q. The total power delivered by the source is the apparent power. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.4

Power factor

Objectives 1.2, 1.3, 1.4

"Power factor (pf) is the ratio between true power and apparent power." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.4

pf = cos θ = P / S DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.4

"It can vary from 1, when the phase angle is 0°, to 0, when the phase angle is 90°." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.4

In an inductive circuit, the current lags the voltage and is said to have a lagging power factor. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.4

In a capacitive circuit, the current leads the voltage and is said to have a leading power factor. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.4

The memory aid is ELI the ICE man. ELI refers to an: DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.4

"inductive circuit (L) where current (I) lags voltage (E)." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.4

ICE refers to a capacitive circuit (C) where current (I) leads voltage (E). DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.4

Knowledge check: Power factor

Objective 1.2Q279

What is power factor?

The answer is D. The other options are the lesson plan's definitions of apparent power, reactive power and true power. DOE-1011-V3 (DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92), Power Triangle, ES-09 p. 4

Report a problem
Objective 1.3Q735

A circuit has E = 100 volts, I = 5 amps, and a power factor of 0.8. What is the true power?

The answer is B. P = EI cos θ = (100)(5)(0.8) = 400 watts. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.3

Report a problem
Objective 1.3Q736

A circuit has E = 100 volts and I = 5 amps. What is the apparent power?

The answer is D. S = ITE = (5)(100) = 500 VA. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.2

Report a problem
Objective 1.3Q737

A circuit has true power of 300 watts and apparent power of 500 VA. What is the power factor?

The answer is A. pf = P / S = 300 / 500 = 0.6. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.4

Report a problem
Objective 1.4Q738

In an inductive circuit, the power factor is:

The answer is B. In an inductive circuit, the current lags the voltage and is said to have a lagging power factor. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.4

Three-phase systems

Objectives 1.5, 1.6, 1.7

"A three-phase (3φ) system is a combination of three single-phase systems." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.17

A balanced system gets its power from a 3φ AC generator that: DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.17

"produces three separate and equal voltages, each of which is 120° out of phase with the other voltages" DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.17

Three-phase equipment weighs less than single-phase equipment of the same power rating. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.18

"They have a wide range of voltages and can be used for single-phase loads." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.18
"Three-phase equipment is smaller in size, weighs less, and is more efficient than single-phase equipment." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.18
"If the three common ends of each phase are connected at a common point and the other three ends are connected to a 3φ line, it is called a wye, or Y-, connection" DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.18
"If the three phases are connected in series to form a closed loop, it is called a delta, or ∆-, connection." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.18
"In a balanced delta load, the line voltage (VL) is equal to the phase voltage (Vφ )" DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.20

In a balanced delta load, the line current is the square root of three times the phase current. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.20

In a balanced wye load, the line voltage is the square root of three times the phase voltage, and: DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.20

"line current (IL) is equal to the phase current (Iφ )." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.20
"the total power formulas for delta- and wye-connected loads are identical." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.21

PT = √3 VL IL cos θ DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.20

ST = √3 VT IL DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.21

QT = √3 VT IL sin θ DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.21

Which is a reason three-phase power systems are used?

Objective 1.6Q740

A balanced three-phase system has VL = 100 volts, IL = 10 amps, and a power factor of 0.8. Using √3 = 1.73, what is the real power?

The answer is B. PT = √3 VL IL cos θ = (1.73)(100)(10)(0.8) = 1384 watts. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.21

A balanced three-phase system has VL = 100 volts and IL = 10 amps. Using √3 = 1.73, what is the apparent power?

Unbalanced loads

Objectives 1.8

In a balanced system, the phasor sum of the three line or phase voltages is zero, and the phasor sum of the three line or phase currents is zero. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.23

When the three load impedances are not equal, the phasor sums and the neutral current are not zero, and the load is unbalanced. This occurs when an open or short circuit appears at the load. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.23

"the neutral connection in a wye-connected load will carry more current than the phase under a balanced load." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.25
"Unbalanced three-phase circuits are indicated by abnormally high currents in one or more of the phases." DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.25

This may cause damage to equipment if the imbalance is allowed to continue. DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.25

Worked examples

Objective 1.3

Power in a series R-C circuit

An 80 Ω XC and a 60 Ω resistance are in series with a 120 V source, and the current is 1.2 amps. Find the power factor, true power, reactive power, and apparent power.

  1. pf = cos θ, where θ = arctan(-80/60) = -53°, so pf = 0.60.
  2. P = EI cos θ = (120)(1.2)(0.60) = 86.4 watts.
  3. Q = EI sin θ = (120)(1.2)(0.798) = 114.9 VAR.
  4. S = EI = (120)(1.2) = 144 VA.

Answer: pf 0.60, P 86.4 watts, Q 114.9 VAR, S 144 VA

DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.9

Objective 1.3

Power in a series R-C-L circuit

A series R-C-L circuit has 71.6 V applied, a 4 amp current, and a phase angle of 63.4°. Find the power factor, true power, and apparent power.

  1. pf = cos 63.4° = 0.45.
  2. P = EI cos θ = (71.6)(4)(0.45) = 128.9 watts.
  3. S = EI = (71.6)(4) = 286.4 VA.

Answer: pf 0.45, P 128.9 watts, S 286.4 VA

DOE-HDBK-1011/3-92 ES-09 p.13
