Ductile and brittle fracture

Objectives 1.1

"Ductile fracture is exhibited when metals can sustain substantial plastic strain or deformation before fracturing." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.6
"Brittle fracture is exhibited when metals fracture with a relatively small or negligible amount of plastic strain." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.6

In brittle fracture, cracks propagate rapidly, and failure results from cleavage, or splitting along definite planes. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.1

"Ductile fracture is better than brittle fracture, because ductile fracture occurs over a period of time, where as brittle fracture is fast, and can occur (with flaws) at lower stress levels than a ductile fracture." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.1

Nil-ductility transition temperature

Objectives 1.1, 1.2, 1.3

Many steels are ductile at elevated temperatures and brittle at low temperatures. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.2

"Nil-Ductility Transition (NDT) temperature is the temperature above which a material is ductile and below which it is brittle." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.6

NDT is not precise. It varies with prior mechanical and heat treatment and with the nature and amounts of impurity elements, and is found by a drop-weight test such as the Izod or Charpy test. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.2

"Small grain size tends to increase ductility and results in a decrease in NDT temperature." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.2

Grain size is controlled by heat treatment in the specifications and manufacturing of reactor vessels. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.2

"The NDT temperature can also be lowered by small additions of selected alloying elements such as nickel and manganese to low-carbon steels." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.2

The reactor vessel is continuously exposed to fast neutrons that escape from the core. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.2

"Consequently, during operation the reactor vessel is subjected to an increasing fluence (flux) of fast neutrons, and as a result the NDT temperature increases steadily." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.2

During shutdown or an abnormal cooldown, the temperature may fall below the NDT value while the internal pressure is still high, and the vessel is then susceptible to brittle fracture. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.2

Conditions for brittle fracture

Objectives 1.4, 1.7

"One of the biggest concerns with brittle fracture is that it can occur at stresses well below the yield strength (stress corresponding to the transition from elastic to plastic behavior) of the material, provided certain conditions are present." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.3
  • A flaw such as a crack
  • Stress sufficient to develop a small deformation at the crack tip
  • Temperature at or below NDT

DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.6

As temperature goes down, tensile strength (Curve A) and yield strength (Curve B) increase. At some low temperature, on the order of 10°F for carbon steel, they coincide. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.3

"The temperature at which the yield and tensile strength coincide is the NDT temperature." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.3

When a small flaw is present, tensile strength follows dashed Curve C. About 50°F above the no-flaw NDT temperature, it drops to the yield curve, and where Curves C and B meet there is a new NDT temperature. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4

"Therefore, if a flaw exists, any failure at a temperature equal or below the NDT temperature for flawed material will be brittle." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4

Crack initiation and fracture toughness

Objectives 1.5, 1.6

Brittle failure generally occurs because a flaw or crack propagates through the material. If a plastic flaw exists at the tip, the metal around the crack supports the stress and the structure is not endangered. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4

When brittle fracture occurs, DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4

"the crack will initiate and propagate through the material at great speeds (speed of sound)." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4
"It should be noted that smaller grain size, higher temperature, and lower stress tend to mitigate crack initiation." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4
"Larger grain size, lower temperatures, and higher stress tend to favor crack propagation." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4
"There is a stress level below which a crack will not propagate at any temperature." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4

This stress level is called the lower fracture propagation stress. As temperature increases, a higher stress is needed for a crack to propagate. That relationship is the crack arrest curve, Curve D. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4

"Fracture toughness is an indication of the amount of stress required to propagate a preexisting flaw." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4
  • Metal composition
  • Metal temperature
  • Extent of deformations to the crystal structure
  • Metal grain size
  • Metal crystalline form

DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4

FTE and FTP

Objectives 1.7

"The intersection of the crack arrest curve with the yield curve (Curve B) is called the fracture transition elastic (FTE) point." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4

The FTE temperature is normally about 60°F above the NDT temperature, and is also known as the Reference Temperature - Nil-ductility Transition (RTNDT). It is the temperature above which plastic deformation accompanies all fractures. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4

The intersection of the crack arrest curve (Curve D) and the tensile strength curve (Curve A) is the fracture transition plastic (FTP) point. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4

"The temperature corresponding with this point is normally about 120°F above the NDT temperature." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4
"Above this temperature, only ductile fractures occur." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.4

Operation must be above the NDT temperature to be certain that no brittle fracture can occur. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.5

"For greater safety, it is desirable that operation be limited above the FTE temperature, or NDT + 60°F." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.5

Under those conditions, no brittle fracture can occur for purely elastic loads. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.5

Minimum pressurization-temperature curves

Objectives 1.8, 1.9, 1.10, 1.11

"Minimum pressurization-temperature (MPT) curves specify the temperature and pressure limitations for reactor plant operation." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.7
"They are based on reactor vessel and head stress limitations and the need to preclude reactor vessel and head brittle fracture." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.7
"Note that the safe operating region is to the right of the reactor vessel MPT curve." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.7

The curve ensures adequate operating margin away from the crack arrest curve. Curves used by operations also include instrument error. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.7

"MPT curve will shift to the right due to irradiation." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.10

This shift accounts for the increased brittleness or decreased ductility from neutron irradiation. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.7

  • Scram reactor
  • Cool down and depressurize
  • Conduct engineering evaluation prior to further plant operation

DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.10

"The precaution to be observed when performing a hydrostatic test is to make sure the pressure is consistent with plant temperatures." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.10

Knowledge check: Minimum pressurization-temperature curves

Objective 1.9Q380

What does irradiation do to the minimum pressurization-temperature (MPT) curve?

The answer is D. The MPT curve will shift to the right due to irradiation, and the safe operating region is to the right of the curve. DOE-1017-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93), Minimum Pressurization-Temperature Curves, MS-04 p. 10

Report a problem
Objective 1.10Q381

What is the first normal action if MPT curves are exceeded during critical operation?

The answer is A. Normal actions are to scram the reactor, cool down and depressurize, and conduct an engineering evaluation before further plant operation. DOE-1017-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93), Minimum Pressurization-Temperature Curves, MS-04 p. 10

What are minimum pressurization-temperature curves based on?

The answer is C. They are based on reactor vessel and head stress limitations and the need to preclude reactor vessel and head brittle fracture. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.7

Soak times

Objectives 1.15, 1.16

Soak times may be required during heatup, especially when large limiting components are involved. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.12

"Soak times are used so that heating can be carefully controlled." DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.12

This minimizes thermal stresses. For example, the coolant is heated to a specified temperature and held there for a set time, so the metal in a large component heats more evenly from the hot side to the cold side. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.12

"Become very significant if system is at room temperature or below and very close to RTNDT temperature limitations" DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.13

Objective 1.7

Find the temperature after an NDT shift

The FTE was 60°F at the beginning of vessel life, and the NDT changed by 300°F over time. What temperature must the coolant be above before full system pressure can be applied?

  1. Irradiation raises the NDT, and the FTE moves with it.

Summary

  • Ductile fracture follows substantial plastic strain, and brittle fracture follows a small or negligible amount. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.6
  • NDT decreases due to smaller grain size and increases due to irradiation. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.6
  • Brittle fracture requires a flaw such as a crack, stress sufficient to develop a small deformation at the crack tip, and a temperature at or below NDT. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.6
  • MPT curves are based on reactor vessel and head stress limitations, and the safe operating region is to the right of the curve. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.10
  • Heatup and cooldown rate limits are based upon impact on the future fatigue life of the plant. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.13
  • Soak times are used to minimize thermal stresses by controlling the heating rate. DOE-HDBK-1017/2-93 MS-04 p.13
