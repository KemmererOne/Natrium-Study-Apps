Subcritical multiplication

Objectives 1.1, 1.2

"Subcritical multiplication is the effect of fissions in the fuel increasing the effective source strength of a reactor with a keff less than one." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.9
"Subcritical multiplication factor is the factor that relates the source level to the steady-state neutron level of the core." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.9
"It is the factor by which source neutrons are multiplied to get neutron population" Word: Subcritical Multiplication.docx, slide 1.15 Subcritical Multiplication factor (M)

M = 1 / (1 - keff) and N = S / (1 - keff) DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.9

"A neutron source strength of 100 neutrons per generation will result in 250 neutrons per generation being produced from a combination of sources and fission in a shutdown reactor with a keff of 0.6." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.2

The factor rises as positive reactivity is added to a shutdown reactor. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.3

Knowledge check: Subcritical multiplication

Objective 1.2Q24

A subcritical reactor has keff = 0.9 and source strength S. Its steady-state neutron level is approximately:

The answer is D. Subcritical multiplication gives N = S / (1 - keff) = S / 0.1 = 10 S. DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Subcritical Multiplication, NP-04 p. 2

Report a problem
Objective 1.1Q292

In a shutdown reactor with keff about 0.8, most of the neutrons in the core are:

The answer is C. With keff above 0.5, fission neutrons outnumber source neutrons; typical shutdown keff is about 0.8. DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Subcritical Multiplication, NP-04 p. 2

Report a problem
Objective 1.1Q293

In a subcritical reactor with keff = 0.95, what percentage of the neutrons are source neutrons?

The answer is B. At equilibrium the fission neutron fraction equals keff, so 95% are fission neutrons and 5% are source neutrons. Approved lesson plan: Subcritical Multiplication (Subcritical Multiplication.docx), slide 1.10 Drop and Drag Keff

Report a problem
Objective 1.2Q295

In a subcritical reactor, how does the steady-state subcritical neutron level relate to the neutron source strength?

The answer is B. If source strength (So) is doubled (for a given keff), total neutron population (Nt) would also double (directly proportional). DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Subcritical Multiplication, NP-04 p. 5

Count rate and 1/M plots

Objectives 1.3, 1.4

CR1 / CR2 = (1 - k2) / (1 - k1) DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.5

Instrument count rate is proportional to neutron level, so the source strength cancels out of the equation. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.5

"Notice from this example that the count rate doubled as the reactivity was halved (e.g., reactivity was changed from -1000 pcm to -500 pcm)." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.6

M grows without limit near criticality, so its inverse is plotted, using 1/M = CRo / CR with a reference count rate. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.7

"For a critical reactor, 1/M is equal to zero." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.7
"1/M plots can be used to predict the point of criticality." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.9
"The neutron population will typically reach its new steady-state value within 1-2 minutes, but the closer the reactor is to criticality, the longer the time will be to stabilize the neutron population." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.7

Period, doubling time and startup rate

Objectives 2.1, 2.5, 2.6

"Reactor period is the time required for reactor power to change by a factor of e (2.718)." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.21
"Doubling time is the time required for reactor power to double." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.21
"Reactor startup rate is the number of factors of ten that reactor power changes in one minute." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.21
"If the reactor period is positive, reactor power is increasing." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.11

P = Po e^(t/τ) DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.11

P = Po 10^(SUR t) DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.17

P = Po 2^(t/DT) DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.18

SUR = 26.06 / τ and DT = τ (ln 2) DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.22

Knowledge check: Period, doubling time and startup rate

Objective 2.1Q22

Reactor period is defined as the time for reactor power to change by a factor of:

The answer is B. The period is the e-folding time. Doubling time is a different quantity, about 0.69 times the period. DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Reactor Kinetics, NP-04 p. 11

Report a problem
Objective 2.1Q23

A reactor has a stable period of 100 seconds. Approximately how long does power take to double?

The answer is B. Doubling time equals ln(2) times the period, which is 0.693 x 100 = about 69 seconds. DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Reactor Kinetics, NP-04 p. 17

Report a problem
Objective 2.5Q1578

A reactor at 1000 watts has a doubling time of 2 minutes. What is its power 4 minutes later?

The answer is B. P = (1000 W)(2)^(4/2) = 4000 W. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.21

Delayed neutron fractions

Objectives 2.2

β is the fraction of all fission neutrons born delayed for a particular type of fuel. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.21

"is the weighted average of the total delayed neutron fractions of the different types of fuel used in a particular reactor." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.21

The effective fraction is the average fraction multiplied by an importance factor, which accounts for the fact that DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.21

"delayed neutrons are born at lower average energies than fast neutrons." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.21

Delayed neutrons average about 0.5 MeV, compared with about 2 MeV for prompt neutrons. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.12

"At low values, response to small changes in the reactivity is magnified and power can change more quickly" NRC slides ML19148A801, slide 5

Knowledge check: Delayed neutron fractions

Objective 2.2Q296

The effective delayed neutron fraction differs from the delayed neutron fraction because, compared with prompt neutrons, delayed neutrons:

The answer is A. The difference stems from birth energy: delayed neutrons cause fewer fast fissions and give fewer neutrons per fission. Generation time has nothing to do with it. DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Reactor Kinetics, NP-04 p. 12

Report a problem
Objective 2.2Q297

What mainly changes the core's delayed neutron fraction over core life?

The answer is D. Poisons and axial power shifts do not significantly change the fraction of fissions caused by delayed neutrons; the fuel mixture does. DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Reactor Kinetics, NP-04 p. 12

Report a problem
Objective 2.2Q139

Why do Pu-bearing fast reactor fuels have a lower margin to prompt criticality during reactivity transients?

The answer is B. Pu-bearing fuels have a lower beta-eff, which lowers the margin to prompt criticality; in breeders the equilibrium core beta-eff can be significantly lower than at beginning of life. NRC Fast Reactor Technology Training (Mar 2019), ML19149A618 Fast Reactor Safety, slide 3

The period equation

Objectives 2.3, 2.4

τ = ℓ*/ρ + (β̄eff - ρ) / (λeff ρ + ρ̇) (prompt term plus delayed term) DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.21

τ is reactor period, ℓ* is prompt generation lifetime, β̄eff is the effective delayed neutron fraction, ρ is reactivity, λeff is the effective precursor decay constant, and ρ̇ is the rate of change of reactivity. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.21

Stable period: τ = (β̄eff - ρ) / (λeff ρ) DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.22

Approximate values for λeff are 0.08 per second at steady state, 0.1 per second for a power increase, and 0.05 per second for a power decrease. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.13

Knowledge check: The period equation

Objective 2.4Q378

For positive and negative reactivity additions of equal magnitude, the power increase has the shorter period because:

The answer is D. Beta-eff is not changed by power changes; the effective precursor decay constant is larger on a power increase, shortening the period. Approved lesson plan: Power Changes (Power Changes.docx), slide 1.5 KC P7797

Report a problem
Objective 2.4Q1579

If a stable reactor period is 26.06 seconds, what is the startup rate?

The answer is B. SUR = 26.06 / τ = 26.06 / 26.06 = 1 DPM. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.21

Over-insertion of control rods in one part of the core is one cause. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.27

Worked examples

Objective 1.1

Subcritical multiplication factor

Calculate M for keff = 0.6, and the neutron level with a source of 1000 neutrons/sec.

  1. M = 1 / (1 - 0.6) = 2.5
  2. N = S x M = (1000 neutrons/sec)(2.5)

Answer: 2500 neutrons/sec

DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.3

Objective 1.2

Steady-state neutron level

A source produces 110,000 neutrons per second and keff is 0.986. Calculate the stable total neutron production rate.

  1. N = S / (1 - keff)
  2. N = (110,000 neutrons/sec) / (1 - 0.986)

Answer: 7.86 x 10^6 neutrons per second

DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.4

Objective 1.3

Count rate after a reactivity addition

A reactor at -1000 pcm reads 42 cps. Find the count rate after adding 500 pcm from control rod withdrawal.

  1. k1 = 1 / (1 + 0.01000) and k2 = 1 / (1 + 0.00500)
  2. CR2 = CR1 (1 - k1) / (1 - k2)

Answer: 83 cps

DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.6

Objective 2.4

Stable period and startup rate

A reactor has λeff of 0.10 per second and an effective delayed neutron fraction of 0.0070. If keff is 1.0025, find the stable period and SUR.

  1. ρ = (keff - 1) / keff = 0.00249 Δk/k
  2. τ = (0.0070 - 0.00249) / ((0.10 per sec)(0.00249)) = 18.1 sec
  3. SUR = 26.06 / 18.1 sec

Answer: τ = 18.1 sec and SUR = 1.44 DPM

DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.18

Objective 2.6

Power after a negative reactivity addition

130 pcm of negative reactivity is added to a reactor critical at 100 watts. The reactor period is -124.6 seconds. Find power 2 minutes later.

  1. P = Po e^(t/τ)
  2. P = (100 W) e^(120 s / -124.6 s)

Answer: 38.2 W

DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.19

Objective 2.5

Power from doubling time

A reactor has a power level of 1000 watts and a doubling time of 2 minutes. What is the power 10 minutes later?

  1. P = Po 2^(t/DT)
  2. P = (1000 W)(2)^(10 min / 2 min)

Answer: 32,000 W

DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.20

Summary

  • A subcritical reactor multiplies its source by M = 1 / (1 - keff). 1/M plots predict criticality. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.9
  • Period, doubling time and startup rate describe power change. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.21
  • Prompt jump and prompt drop are small, immediate changes. One dollar of reactivity is prompt critical. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.22
  • Fast reactor reflectors are not moderating materials. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.25
  • Shutdown margin assumes the highest worth rod is fully withdrawn. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.34
  • Decay heat comes from fission product decay and falls to less than 1% about one hour after shutdown. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-04 p.33
