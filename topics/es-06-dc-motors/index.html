Force on a conductor

Objectives 1.1

"There are two conditions which are necessary to produce a force on a conductor." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.1
  • The conductor must be carrying current.
  • The conductor must be within a magnetic field.

DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.1

The force attempts to move the conductor in a direction perpendicular to the magnetic field. This is the basic theory by which all DC motors operate. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.1

A force is developed on the conductor that moves it in the direction of the weakened field. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.2

The right-hand rule for motors shows the direction in which a current-carrying conductor moves in a magnetic field. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.4

"When the forefinger is pointed in the direction of the magnetic field lines, and the center finger is pointed in the direction of current flow, the thumb will point in the direction of force (motion)." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.4

Torque

Objectives 1.2, 1.7

"Torque is defined as that force which tends to produce and maintain rotation." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.4
"The function of torque in a DC motor is to provide the mechanical output or drive the piece of equipment that the DC motor is attached to." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.4

When voltage is applied, current flows through the field winding and establishes a magnetic field. Current also flows through the armature winding. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.4

The armature is a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field, so a force is exerted on it. The sum of the forces multiplied by the radius of the armature equals the torque. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.4

"if the armature current were reversed, but the field were the same, torque would be developed in the opposite direction." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.4

Torque depends on the field flux and the armature current. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.5

T = KΦIa DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.5

CEMF and speed control

Objectives 1.3, 1.4, 1.5, 1.6

A generator action is developed in every motor. When a conductor cuts lines of force, an EMF is induced in that conductor. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.5

"The induced EMF, as a result of motor operation, is called counterelectromotive force, or CEMF" DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.5

The value of CEMF depends on field strength and armature speed. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.5

ECEMF = KΦN DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.5

"The CEMF opposes the applied voltage and functions to lower armature current." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.5

The effective voltage acting in the armature is the applied voltage minus the counter EMF. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.5

Ia = (Et - ECEMF) / Ra DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.6

The field of a DC motor is varied using external devices, usually field resistors. Lowering the field resistance increases the field current. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.6

"An increase in field current will cause field flux (Φf) to increase." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.6
"If the field flux of a DC motor is decreased, the motor speed will increase." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.6

Less field strength reduces the CEMF. That allows more armature current, which develops a larger torque and increases the motor speed. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.6

Speed and CEMF continue to increase until armature current and torque are just large enough to supply the load at a new constant speed. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.7

"The speed of a DC motor may be changed by using resistors to vary the field current and, therefore, the field strength." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.7

Types of DC motors

Objectives 1.8, 1.9, 1.10

"The motor is called a "shunt" motor because the field is in parallel, or "shunts" the armature." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.9
"The motor field windings for a series motor are in series with the armature." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.9
"A compounded DC motor is constructed so that it contains both a shunt and a series field." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.9

In a cumulatively-compounded motor the shunt and series fields aid one another, and in a differentially-compounded motor they oppose one another. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.9

A shunt-wound DC motor has a decreasing torque when speed increases. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.9

"it is classified as a constant speed motor, even though the speed does slightly decrease as load is increased." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.9

As the speed of a series-wound motor decreases, its torque increases sharply. As load is removed, its speed increases sharply. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.10

"series-wound motors must have a load connected to prevent damage from high speed conditions." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.10
"The advantage of a series-wound motor is that it develops a large torque and can be operated at low speed." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.10

The compounded motor has a greater torque than a shunt motor due to the series field, and a fairly constant speed due to the shunt field winding. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.10

Starting and ratings

Objectives 1.11, 1.12

"At the moment a DC motor is started the armature is stationary and there is no counter EMF being generated." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.12

The only thing limiting starting current is the armature resistance, which in most DC motors is a very low value. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.12

This high current would, in all probability, cause severe damage to the brushes, commutator, or windings. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.12

"Starting resistors are usually incorporated into the motor design to limit starting current to 125 to 200 percent of full load current." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.12

Maximum resistance is inserted when the motor is first started. As speed and counter EMF increase, the starting resistors are cut out in steps until the motor reaches full running speed. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.13

"The nameplate ratings of a DC motor refer to the conditions of voltage, current, speed, and power at which the motor is normally operated." DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.13

The continuous power rating is a thermal rating. The speed rating is the upper limit at which a motor can be operated without mechanical damage. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.13

Objective 1.11Q705

A motor has 200 volts applied and an armature resistance of 0.5 ohms. What is the armature current at the moment of starting?

The answer is C. There is no counter EMF at start, so Ia = Et / Ra = 200 / 0.5 = 400 amps. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.12

Worked examples

Objective 1.11

Starting current

A 10-hp motor has an armature resistance of 0.4 ohms and a 260 VDC source. Find the starting current with no starting resistance.

  1. There is no counter EMF at the moment of starting.
  2. Ia = (260 VDC - 0) / 0.4 Ω.

Answer: 650 amps

DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.12

Objective 1.11

Sizing a starting resistor

The same motor has a full load current of 50 amps. Find the resistance to add in series to limit starting current to 125 percent of this value.

  1. Rs = (Et / Is) - Ra.
  2. Rs = 260 VDC / (125%)(50 amps) - 0.4 Ω.

Answer: 3.76 Ω

DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.13

Summary

  • A force is produced on a conductor that is carrying current and is within a magnetic field. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.7
  • The function of torque is to provide the mechanical output, and CEMF lowers armature current. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.7
  • The speed of a DC motor may be changed by varying the field current. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.7
  • A shunt-wound motor has a decreasing torque as speed increases. A series-wound motor has a rapidly increasing torque when speed decreases. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.11
  • Starting resistors are necessary for large DC motors to prevent damage due to high currents while starting the motor. DOE-HDBK-1011/2-92 ES-06 p.14
