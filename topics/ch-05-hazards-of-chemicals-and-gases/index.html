DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93, Module 5.

Terminal objective

1.0 Without references, DISCUSS the hazards associated with chemicals (liquid and gas) found in a nuclear plant. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.v

Enabling objectives

1.1 STATE the hazards associated with the use of corrosives. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.v

1.4 LIST the criteria used to determine if a compound is a health hazard. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.v

  • a. Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS)
  • b. Toxic Substance List

1.7 DEFINE the following terms: DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.v

  • a. Compressed gas
  • b. Non-liquified gases
  • c. Liquified gases
  • d. Dissolved gases

1.9 STATE the general safety precautions regarding the use, handling, and storage of gases. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.v

1.10 STATE the safety precautions for working with cryogenic liquids. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.v

  • a. Hydrogen

1.15 LIST four sources of ignition of flammable liquids. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.vi

Hazards of acids and alkalies

Objectives 1.1

"Acids are corrosive in any form, and in high concentrations destroy body tissue and cause severe burns on contact with the skin." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.1

The eyes are very susceptible, and permanent damage or loss of sight may result. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.1

"Acids also react with many metals resulting in the liberation of hydrogen, a highly flammable gas, which upon ignition in air may cause an explosion." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.1
"The alkalies, whether in solid form or concentrated liquid solution, are more destructive to tissue than most acids." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.2

Alkali dusts, mists, and sprays may irritate the eyes and respiratory tract, and strong alkalies cause severe burns and scarring. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.2

"Therefore, cold water must be used to dissolve solid alkalies, otherwise the solution may boil, and splatter corrosive liquid over a wide area." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.2
"Alkalies are much more injurious to the eyes than acids because strong acids tend to precipitate a protein barrier, which prevents further penetration into the tissue." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.3

Objective 1.1Q497

What can inhalation of acidic vapors do?

The answer is A. Inhalation of acidic vapors can irritate the respiratory system. DOE-1015-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93), Corrosives, CH-05 p. 6

Objective 1.1Q1158

Why are alkalies more injurious to the eyes than acids?

The answer is A. Strong acids tend to precipitate a protein barrier. Alkalies continue to soak into the tissue. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.3

Report a problem
Objective 1.1Q1159

What gas do acids liberate when they react with many metals?

The answer is B. Acids react with many metals, liberating hydrogen, a highly flammable gas. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.1

Report a problem
Objective 1.1Q1160

Why must cold water be used to dissolve solid alkalies?

The answer is D. Much heat is evolved, so with warmer water the solution may boil and splatter corrosive liquid. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.2

Handling and storing corrosives

Objectives 1.2

If a corrosive enters the eyes, irrigate them with water for at least 15 minutes and consult a physician immediately. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.3

Treatment of local contact starts with immediate removal of the acid with a large amount of water, DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.1

"usually a minimum washing time of 15 minutes." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.1
"No attempt should be made to neutralize the corrosive prior to treatment with water." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.4

Chemical safety goggles should be worn, and eyewash fountains and safety showers must be available. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.3

"Under no circumstance should corrosives be transferred from the original labeled container to an unmarked container." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.4
"must be opened slowly and cautiously because of the possible buildup of pressure within the container." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.5
  • Fire-resistant construction
  • Chemical-resistant, washable floors with adequate drainage
  • Well-lit and ventilated, with adequate outlets for water
  • Relatively cool and dry
  • Electrical fixtures protected against corrosive mists

DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.5

Toxic compounds

Objectives 1.3, 1.4, 1.5

Proper ventilation, hygienic practices, housekeeping, protective clothing, and training diminish many of the hazards. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.8

"Toxicity is the capacity of a material to produce injury or harm to a living organism." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.8
"Hazard is the possibility that a material will cause injury when a specific quantity is used under specific conditions." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.9
  • Toxicity of the materials used
  • Physical properties of these materials
  • Absorption probabilities of these materials by individuals
  • Extent and intensity of exposure to these materials
  • Control measures used

DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.9

"In general, industrial poisonings usually result from inhalation, ingestion, and absorption." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.9
"Acute poisoning is characterized by rapid absorption of the material and sudden, severe exposure." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.10
"Chronic poisoning is characterized by absorption of a harmful material in small doses over a long period of time" DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.10

MSDS and the Toxic Substances List

Objectives 1.6

"The purpose of the Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) is to ensure the individuals working with chemicals and in the vicinity of chemicals have specific information on these chemicals." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.12

The MSDS identifies the chemical by technical and common name, and lists physical and chemical characteristics and fire, explosion, and reactivity hazards. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.12

It also specifies health hazards, first aid procedures, and safe handling and control measures. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.12

"The purpose of The Toxic Substances List is to identify "all known toxic substances"" DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.10
"A listing does mean, however, that the substance has the documented potential of being hazardous if misused" DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.10

Absence from the list does not necessarily mean a substance is not toxic. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.12

Compressed gases

Objectives 1.7, 1.8

A compressed gas gives a pressure reading of either 25 psig at 70 °F or 125 psig at 130 °F, or if flammable, 25 psig at 100 °F. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.17

"Non-liquified gases, which are gases that do not liquify in containers at ambient temperatures and under pressures attained in commercially used containers" DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.17
"Liquified gases, which are gases that do become liquids to a very large extent in containers at ordinary ambient temperatures" DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.17
"A dissolved gas is defined as a gas that is dissolved into a solution." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.17

Acetylene is sometimes referred to as a dissolved gas. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.17

"The major families of gases are atmospheric gases, fuel gases, refrigerant gases, aerosol gases, and poison gases." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.18

Gas safety and cryogenic liquids

Objectives 1.9, 1.10

"Practically all gases can act as simple asphyxiants by displacing the oxygen in air." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.19

The chief precaution is adequate ventilation of enclosed areas. Do not enter unventilated areas without breathing apparatus. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.19

"Although oxygen does not ignite, it lowers the ignition point of flammable substances and greatly accelerates combustion." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.19

Oxygen should be stored no closer than 10 feet to cylinders or tanks containing flammable gases. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.19

"Cylinders should not be dragged or rolled across the floor; they should be moved by a hand truck." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.20

Cylinders should be chained so they cannot fall, and a broken valve could make a cylinder a projectile. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.20

Cryogenic liquids and their boil-off vapor can rapidly freeze human tissue and make materials brittle. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.20

"All cryogenic liquids produce large volumes of gas when they vaporize." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.20

Vaporized in a sealed container, they produce enormous pressures, so containers usually have two pressure-relief devices. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.20

Stand clear of boiling or splashing liquid, and never let an unprotected body part touch uninsulated pipes or vessels. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.21

"Tongs, or a similar device, should be used to withdraw objects immersed in a cryogenic liquid." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.21
"Gloves should be loose fitting so that they can be removed quickly if liquids are spilled into them." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.21

Hydrogen, nitrogen, and oxygen

Objectives 1.11

Hydrogen is the lightest of all elements and cannot be detected by any of the senses. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.25

"It is flammable in oxygen or air, and has a flammable range of from 4.1 percent to 74.2 percent by volume in air." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.25
"Pure hydrogen burns quietly in air with an almost invisible flame" DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.25
"Metallic sodium and potassium are examples of some chemicals that react violently when exposed to water, producing hydrogen, which may flame spontaneously due to the heat of the reaction." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.25
"A lead-acid battery will produce hydrogen when it is being charged." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.25

Precautions include adequate ventilation and eliminating possible sources of ignition. Hydrogen is classified as an asphyxiant. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.25

Nitrogen makes up more than 78 percent of the atmosphere, and it is frequently used to inert systems. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.25

"It will not burn or support combustion." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.25
"If a large amount of nitrogen were released into the air of an enclosed space, it could cause a serious oxygen deficiency." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.25
"Oxygen (O2) supports combustion, but does not burn." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.26
"When oxygen comes in contact with oil, grease, or fuel oils, it may ignite violently." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.26
"Finally, oxygen valves should be operated slowly." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.26

Oxygen should never be used to run pneumatic equipment, and it should always be called oxygen, not air. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.26

Fire and ignition sources

Objectives 1.15

Fire needs three factors in the right quantity at one place and time. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.27

"These factors are fuel, heat, and oxygen (which is normally supplied by air)." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.27

To prevent a fire, keep one of these factors away from the other two. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.27

  • All sources of static electricity
  • Improper type of electrical equipment, or poorly maintained electrical equipment
  • Flame-producing operations such as smoking, grinding, or welding
  • Improper hand tools that cause sparks

Flammable and combustible liquids

Objectives 1.12, 1.13, 1.16

A flammable liquid has a flash point below 100 °F and a vapor pressure not exceeding 40 psia at 100 °F. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.30

Combustible liquids have flash points at or above 100 °F, but below 200 °F. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.30

Flash point is DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.31

"The minimum temperature at which a liquid gives off vapor in sufficient concentration to form an ignitible mixture with air near the surface of the liquid" DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.31
"Since it is the vapor-air mixture formed from the evaporation of the liquid that poses the hazard" DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.31
"combustible liquids should be treated as flammable when they are heated" DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.32
"Avoid accidental mixture of flammable and combustible liquids." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.33

Lines, valves, and tanks should be labeled so different substances are not mixed. Smoking and spark-producing devices should not be permitted. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.33

"Storage of flammable and combustible liquids in open containers should not be permitted." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.40
"Most vapors from flammable and combustible liquids are heavier than air and will flow into pits, tank openings, confined areas, and low places" DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.39
"Irritation results from the solvent action of many flammable liquids on the natural skin oils and tissue." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.39

Containers should be aired and tested for toxic and flammable atmosphere and oxygen level before entry. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.39

Bonding and grounding

Objectives 1.14

"Static electricity is generated by the contact and separation of dissimilar material." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.33
"The principal hazards created by static electricity are fire and explosion, which are caused by spark discharges." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.33

A spark occurs when there is a poor conductive path between two bodies, so flammable liquid containers need grounding or bonding. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.33

"bonding eliminates a difference in potential between objects. Grounding eliminates a difference in potential between an object and ground." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.34
"Bonding and grounding are effective only when the bonded objects are conductive." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.34
"a wire bond should be provided between the storage container and the container being filled, unless a metallic path between the container is otherwise present." DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.36

High static charge is controlled by reducing flow rates, avoiding splashing, and allowing relaxation time. DOE-HDBK-1015/2-93 CH-05 p.37
