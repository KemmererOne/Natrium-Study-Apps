What a heat exchanger does

Objectives 1.0

"A heat exchanger is a component that allows the transfer of heat from one fluid (liquid or gas) to another fluid." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.1
  • To heat a cooler fluid by means of a hotter fluid
  • To reduce the temperature of a hot fluid by means of a cooler fluid
  • To boil a liquid by means of a hotter fluid
  • To condense a gaseous fluid by means of a cooler fluid
  • To boil a liquid while condensing a hotter gaseous fluid

DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.1

To transfer heat, the fluids must be at different temperatures and must come into thermal contact. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.1

"Heat can flow only from the hotter to the cooler fluid." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.1
"In a heat exchanger there is no direct contact between the two fluids." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.2

The heat moves from the hot fluid to the metal isolating the two fluids, and then to the cooler fluid. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.2

Construction

Objectives 1.1, 1.2

"Although heat exchangers come in every shape and size imaginable, the construction of most heat exchangers fall into one of two categories: tube and shell, or plate." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.2

The tube and shell is the most basic and most common type. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.2

"This type of heat exchanger consists of a set of tubes in a container called a shell." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.2

The fluid inside the tubes is the tube side fluid, and the fluid outside them is the shell side fluid. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.2

"At the ends of the tubes, the tube side fluid is separated from the shell side fluid by the tube sheet(s)." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.2

Tubes are rolled and press-fitted or welded into the tube sheet to give a leak tight seal. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.2

"In systems where the two fluids are at vastly different pressures, the higher pressure fluid is typically directed through the tubes and the lower pressure fluid is circulated on the shell side." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.2

This is due to economy, because tubes can be made to withstand higher pressures than the shell for a much lower cost. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.2

"The support plates shown on Figure 1 also act as baffles to direct the flow of fluid within the shell back and forth across the tubes." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.2
"A plate type heat exchanger, as illustrated in Figure 2, consists of plates instead of tubes to separate the hot and cold fluids." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.3

Hot and cold fluids alternate between the plates, and baffles direct the flow. The large plate area lets a plate unit transfer much more heat than a similarly sized tube and shell unit. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.3

Parallel, counter, and cross flow

Objectives 1.3

Parallel flow exists when both fluids flow in the same direction. They enter from the same end with a large temperature difference, and their temperatures approach each other. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.4

"Note that the hottest cold-fluid temperature is always less than the coldest hot-fluid temperature." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.4

Counter flow exists when the two fluids flow in opposite directions, entering at opposite ends. The cooler fluid leaves where the hot fluid enters, so it approaches the hot fluid's inlet temperature. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.5

"Counter flow heat exchangers are the most efficient of the three types." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.5

A counter flow unit can have the hottest cold-fluid temperature greater than the coldest hot-fluid temperature. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.5

"Cross flow, as illustrated in Figure 5, exists when one fluid flows perpendicular to the second fluid; that is, one fluid flows through tubes and the second fluid passes around the tubes at 90° angle." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.6
"Cross flow heat exchangers are usually found in applications where one of the fluids changes state (2-phase flow)." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.6

Counter flow is the most efficient per unit surface area because the average temperature difference between the fluids over the length of the exchanger is maximized. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.6

"In actuality, most large heat exchangers are not purely parallel flow, counter flow, or cross flow; they are usually a combination of the two or all three types of heat exchangers." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.8

In a parallel flow heat exchanger, how do the two fluids flow?

In which arrangement can the hottest cold-fluid temperature exceed the coldest hot-fluid temperature?

The answer is B. In contrast to parallel flow, a counter flow heat exchanger can have the hottest cold-fluid temperature greater than the coldest hot-fluid temperature. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.5

Objective 1.3Q1401

Where are cross flow heat exchangers usually found?

Report a problem
Objective 1.3Q43

For the same heat transfer area, which flow arrangement removes the most heat?

The answer is B. Counter-flow exchangers keep a more even temperature difference along their length, so they usually transfer more heat than parallel-flow exchangers. DOE-1018-V1 (DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93), Types of Heat Exchangers, ME-02 p. 6

Objective 1.3Q284

Why is a counter-flow heat exchanger the most efficient flow arrangement?

The answer is D. Counter flow has the largest log mean temperature difference; in counter flow the hottest cold-fluid temperature can exceed the coldest hot-fluid temperature, unlike parallel flow. DOE-1012-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1012/2-92), Heat Exchangers, HT-02 p. 33

Single-pass, multi-pass, regenerative, and non-regenerative

Objectives 1.4

"If the fluids pass each other only once, the heat exchanger is called a single-pass heat exchanger." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.8
"When a heat exchanger's fluids pass each other more than once, a heat exchanger is called a multi-pass heat exchanger." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.8

A multi-pass unit commonly reverses the flow in the tubes with U bends. Baffles on the shell side give the same effect for the shell side fluid. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.8

"A regenerative heat exchanger is one in which the same fluid is both the cooling fluid and the cooled fluid, as illustrated in Figure 7." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.9

The hot fluid leaving a system gives up its heat to reheat the fluid returning to the system. These units are usually found in high temperature systems where part of the fluid is removed from the main process and then returned. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.9

"In a nonregenerative heat exchanger, as illustrated in Figure 7, the hot fluid is cooled by fluid from a separate system and the energy (heat) removed is not returned to the system." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.10
"It is important to remember that the term regenerative/nonregenerative only refers to "how" a heat exchanger functions in a system, and does not indicate any single type (tube and shell, plate, parallel flow, counter flow, etc.)." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.9

Applications

Objectives 1.5

"Some of the more common applications are found in heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC) systems, radiators on internal combustion engines, boilers, condensers, and as preheaters or coolers in fluid systems." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.12

A radiator is an air-to-liquid heat exchanger. Hot coolant flows through the tubes, and cooler air over the outside of the tubes picks up the heat. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.13

Air is a poor conductor of heat, so fins on the outside of the tubes maximize the heat transfer area. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.14

"The fins improve the efficiency of a heat exchanger" DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.14

Fins are common on most liquid-to-air heat exchangers and in some high efficiency liquid-to-liquid units. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.14

"All air conditioning systems contain at least two heat exchangers, usually called the evaporator and the condenser." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.14

In the condenser, hot, high pressure refrigerant gas condenses to a subcooled liquid. In the evaporator, cool refrigerant absorbs heat from air flowing over the tubes, which cools the air and boils the refrigerant. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.14

Preheaters

Objectives 1.5

"Preheating in stages increases the plant's efficiency and minimizes thermal shock stress to components, as compared to injecting ambient temperature liquid into a boiler or other device that operates at high temperatures." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.12

In a steam system, process steam is tapped off to heat the feedwater in preheater stages. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.12

The steam gives up sensible heat and latent heat of vaporization on the shell side. The shell side fluid level sets how many tubes are exposed to the hot steam, so it is very important to efficiency. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.13

Steam condensers

Objectives 1.6, 1.7, 1.8

"It is a closed space into which the steam exits the turbine and is forced to give up its latent heat of vaporization." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.14

A condenser turns used steam back into water for return as feedwater, and it raises cycle efficiency by letting the cycle operate with the largest possible delta-T and delta-P between source and heat sink. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.14

"The bottom area of the condenser is the hotwell, as shown in Figure 9." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.15

Condensate collects in the hotwell, and the condensate pump takes its suction there. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.15

"The difference between the saturation temperature for the existing condenser vacuum and the temperature of the condensate is termed condensate depression." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.14

A few degrees of subcooling prevents condensate pump cavitation. Excessive condensate depression lowers plant efficiency, because the subcooled condensate must be reheated. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.14

"Condensers operate at a vacuum to ensure the temperature (and thus the pressure) of the steam is as low as possible." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.18
"This maximizes the ∆T and ∆P between the source and the heat sink, ensuring the highest cycle efficiency possible." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.18
"If noncondensable gasses are allowed to build up in the condenser, vacuum will decrease and the saturation temperature at which the steam will condense increases." DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.15

An air ejector or vacuum pump establishes and helps maintain condenser vacuum, because air and other noncondensable gasses cannot be kept out. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.16

Startup, temperature control, and fouling

From the lesson plan

Consider the difference in operating temperature between the two fluids when placing a heat exchanger in service, to prevent thermal shock. Word: Heat Exchangers.docx, slide 1.24 Heat Exchanger Startup & Operation

"Thermal shock is a severe stress produced in a body or in a material because of a sudden, unequally distributed change in temperature." Word: Heat Exchangers.docx, slide 1.25 Heat Exchanger Startup & Operation

Thermal shock is most severe where different metals are joined. Fluids are admitted slowly to allow uniform expansion. Word: Heat Exchangers.docx, slide 1.25 Heat Exchanger Startup & Operation

"Admit the colder fluid to the heat exchanger first, followed by the hotter fluid." Word: Heat Exchangers.docx, slide 1.25 Heat Exchanger Startup & Operation
"Vent prior to placing heat exchanger in service to remove air and noncondensable gases." Word: Heat Exchangers.docx, slide 1.25 Heat Exchanger Startup & Operation

Pockets of air or gas decrease the cooling surface area and may disrupt flow. Word: Heat Exchangers.docx, slide 1.25 Heat Exchanger Startup & Operation

"When securing a heat exchanger, first stop the hot fluid by shutting the discharge valve." Word: Heat Exchangers.docx, slide 1.25 Heat Exchanger Startup & Operation

After that, stop the colder fluid flow. Word: Heat Exchangers.docx, slide 1.25 Heat Exchanger Startup & Operation

"A heat exchanger that contains liquid should not be isolated in such a manner that it does not have overpressure protection." Word: Heat Exchangers.docx, slide 1.25 Heat Exchanger Startup & Operation

Liquid isolated in the exchanger could warm up and expand. A relief valve, or leaving the discharge valve open, gives overpressure protection. Word: Heat Exchangers.docx, slide 1.25 Heat Exchanger Startup & Operation

"The flow of the cooling fluid also affects the heat exchanger outlet temperature because increasing the flow of cooling fluid lowers the outlet temperature of the cooled fluid." Word: Heat Exchangers.docx, slide 1.26 Temperature Control

Fouling is foreign material such as algae, scale, or debris building up in a heat exchanger. Word: Heat Exchangers.docx, slide 1.28 Fouling of Heat Exchange Surfaces

"Fouling of heat exchanger tubes lowers the efficiency of a heat exchanger by decreasing the thermal conductivity of the tubes." Word: Heat Exchangers.docx, slide 1.28 Fouling of Heat Exchange Surfaces
  • Hydro lancing
  • Chemical cleaning
  • Operating practices, the most effective method

Worked examples

Objective 1.3

Counter flow against parallel flow

The same heat exchanger runs first as counter flow and then as parallel flow. Hot fluid: 200°F in, 145°F out. Cold fluid: 80°F in, 120°F out. Uo = 70 BTU/hr-ft²-°F and Ao = 75 ft². Compare the heat transfer rates.

  1. ΔTlm = (ΔT2 - ΔT1) / ln(ΔT2 / ΔT1), and Q = Uo Ao ΔTlm.
  2. Counter flow: the end differences are 200 - 120 = 80°F and 145 - 80 = 65°F, so ΔTlm = 15 / ln(80/65) = 72°F.
  3. Parallel flow: the end differences are 200 - 80 = 120°F and 145 - 120 = 25°F, so ΔTlm = 95 / ln(120/25) = 61°F.
  4. Counter flow: Q = 70 x 75 x 72 = 3.8 x 10^5 BTU/hr.
  5. Parallel flow: Q = 70 x 75 x 61 = 3.2 x 10^5 BTU/hr.

Answer: Counter flow transfers more heat under the same conditions: 3.8 x 10^5 BTU/hr against 3.2 x 10^5 BTU/hr.

DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.7

Summary

  • Most heat exchangers are tube and shell or plate. The main internal parts are the tubes, tube sheet, shell, and baffles. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.11
  • Parallel flow runs in the same direction, counter flow in opposite directions, and cross flow at 90°. Counter flow is the most efficient. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.11
  • Multi-pass units pass the fluids more than once. Regenerative units use the same fluid for heating and cooling. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.11
  • Applications include preheaters, radiators, air conditioning evaporators and condensers, and steam condensers. DOE-HDBK-1018/1-93 ME-02 p.18
  • Admit the colder fluid first, stop the hot fluid first, and vent before placing the unit in service. Word: Heat Exchangers.docx, slide 1.25 Heat Exchanger Startup & Operation
