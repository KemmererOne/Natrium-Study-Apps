Multiplication factors

Objectives 1.1

"The infinite multiplication factor, k∞, is the ratio of the neutrons produced by fission in one generation to the number of neutrons lost through absorption in the preceding generation." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.15
"The effective multiplication factor, keff, is the ratio of the number of neutrons produced by fission in one generation to the number of neutrons lost through absorption and leakage in the preceding generation." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.15
"Critical is the condition where the neutron chain reaction is self-sustaining and the neutron population is neither increasing nor decreasing." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.15
"Subcritical is the condition in which the neutron population is decreasing each generation." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.15
"Supercritical is the condition in which the neutron population is increasing each generation." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.15

A self-sustaining chain reaction has keff = 1. A supercritical reactor has keff greater than one, and a subcritical reactor has keff less than one. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.8

Knowledge check: Multiplication factors

Objective 1.1Q1

When the effective multiplication factor k_eff equals 1.0, the reactor is:

The answer is B. k_eff of 1.0 means each fission produces, on average, exactly one fission in the next generation. Power is steady. Above 1.0 power rises, and below 1.0 it falls. DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Neutron Life Cycle, NP-03 p. 15

Report a problem
Objective 1.1Q10

Which describes a reactor that is exactly critical?

The answer is C. Keff of 1 means each generation produces as many fissions as the one before. Reactivity, delta k/k, is then zero. DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Neutron Life Cycle, NP-03 p. 15

Report a problem
Objective 1.1Q1569

How does the effective multiplication factor differ from the infinite multiplication factor?

The answer is C. k∞ counts neutrons lost through absorption. keff counts neutrons lost through absorption and leakage. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.15

Neutron life cycle of a fast reactor

Objectives 1.2, 1.3, 1.4, 1.5, 1.6, 1.7

The handbook's six factor formula is written for thermal reactors, so it stays off this page and objectives 1.2, 1.3, 1.5, 1.6 and 1.7 are marked for review. The handbook describes how a fast reactor differs. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.14

"Virtually all fissions taking place in a fast reactor are caused by fast neutrons." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.14
"The resonance escape probability is not significant because very few neutrons exist at energies where resonance absorption is significant." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.14
"The thermal non-leakage probability does not exist because the reactor is designed to avoid the thermalization of neutrons." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.14
"A separate term to deal with fast fission is not necessary because all fission is fast fission and is handled by the reproduction factor." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.14
"The thermal utilization factor is modified to describe the utilization of fast neutrons instead of thermal neutrons." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.14
"The reproduction factor is similarly modified to account for fast fission instead of thermal fission." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.14

η = σf ν / σa (for a single nuclide) DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.8

"Neutron mean free path is approximately ten times greater than in a traditional LWR." Word: Reactor Neutrons.docx, slide 1.30 Factors Affecting Fast Fission
"Fast non-leakage can have a greater effect on k-effective than in a light water reactor." Word: Reactor Neutrons.docx, slide 1.31 Factors Affecting Fast Fission

Reactivity

Objectives 1.8, 1.9, 1.10, 1.11

Nn = No (keff)^n DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.17

"Reactivity is the fractional change in neutron population per generation." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.22

ρ = (keff - 1) / keff and keff = 1 / (1 - ρ) DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.22

"The larger the absolute value of reactivity in the reactor core, the further the reactor is from criticality." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.18
"Reactivity is a dimensionless number." DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.19

1% Δk/k = 0.01 Δk/k, and 1 pcm = 0.00001 Δk/k. Some reactors also use a unit of 10^-4 Δk/k. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.19

Reactivity must be in units of Δk/k when it is used to find keff. DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93 NP-03 p.20

The fractional change in neutron population from one generation to the next is called:

The answer is C. Reactivity describes how far the core is from critical. Beta is the delayed-neutron fraction, and lambda is a decay constant for delayed-neutron precursors. DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Reactivity, NP-03 p. 18

Objective 1.10Q299

A reactor has keff = 0.985. What is its reactivity?

The answer is C. ρ = (keff - 1) / keff = (0.985 - 1) / 0.985 = -1.52 %Δk/k. Approved lesson plan: Reactor Neutrons (Reactor Neutrons.docx), slide 1.49 Quiz question 5

One percent delta k/k equals how many pcm?

The answer is C. One pcm is 0.00001 delta k/k. Therefore 0.01 delta k/k (one percent) equals 1,000 pcm. DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Reactivity, NP-03 p. 19

Knowledge check: Coefficients and defects

Objective 1.12Q25

A reactivity defect equals:

The answer is B. A reactivity coefficient gives reactivity change per unit change in a parameter. Multiplying by the size of the change gives the total defect. DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Reactivity, NP-03 p. 22

Report a problem
Objective 1.12Q362

The coolant temperature coefficient is -1.2 pcm/°F and coolant temperature rises 50 °F. What is the reactivity defect?

The answer is A. A reactivity defect is the coefficient times the parameter change: -1.2 pcm/°F × 50 °F = -60 pcm. DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Reactivity, NP-03 p. 21

How are differential rod worth (DRW) and integral rod worth (IRW) related?

The answer is A. DRW is the reactivity change per unit rod movement, the slope of the IRW curve; IRW is the sum of DRWs between two positions. DOE-1019-V2 (DOE-HDBK-1019/2-93), Control Rods, NP-03 p. 52

Withdrawing control rods adds positive reactivity mainly because:
