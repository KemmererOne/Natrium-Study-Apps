Theory of head flow meters

Objectives 1.1

"Head flow meters operate on the principle of placing a restriction in the line to cause a differential pressure head." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.1

The primary element is the restriction in the line, and the secondary element is the differential pressure measuring device. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.1

"Generally, the indicating instrument extracts the square root of the differential pressure and displays the flow rate on a linear indicator." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.1
"The head flow meter actually measures volume flow rate rather than mass flow rate." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.2

Temperature and pressure affect the density of the fluid. Temperature and density are inversely proportional, while pressure and density are directly proportional. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.2

"If the volumetric flow rate signal is compensated for changes in temperature and/or pressure, a true mass flow rate signal can be obtained." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.2

Knowledge check: Theory of head flow meters

Objective 1.1Q391

Head flow meters operate by:

The answer is C. A restriction causes a pressure drop, and the resulting differential pressure is converted to a flow measurement. DOE-1013-V1 (DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92), Head Flow Meters, IC-04 p. 7

Report a problem
Objective 1.1Q325

Head flow detectors such as orifice plates, flow nozzles and venturi tubes measure flow using the principle that flow rate is:

The answer is D. Volumetric flow squared is proportional to differential pressure, so flow is proportional to the square root of D/P. DOE-1012-V3 (DOE-HDBK-1012/3-92), Bernoulli’S Equation, HT-03 p. 30

Report a problem

What are the two elements of a head flow meter?

The answer is B. The primary element is the restriction, and the secondary element is the differential pressure measuring device. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.1

Orifice plates

Objectives 1.2

The orifice plate is the simplest and most economical flowpath restriction. Plates are 1/16 to 1/4 inch thick, mounted between flanges in a straight run of smooth pipe. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.3

"Three kinds of orifice plates are used: concentric, eccentric, and segmental" DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.3
"The concentric orifice plate is the most common of the three types." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.3

As fluid passes through the orifice, velocity rises to a maximum and pressure falls to a minimum. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.3

"The pressure loss is irrecoverable; therefore, the output pressure will always be less than the input pressure." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.3
"The segmental portion of the orifice eliminates damming of foreign materials on the upstream side of the orifice when mounted in a horizontal pipe." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.3
"they cause a high permanent pressure drop (outlet pressure will be 60% to 80% of inlet pressure), and they are subject to erosion" DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.3

Knowledge check: Orifice plates

Objective 1.2Q326

If the orifice in a D/P flow sensor erodes larger and actual flow is unchanged, indicated flow will ____ due to a ____ D/P.

The answer is B. A larger opening lowers velocity through the orifice and the D/P, so indicated flow drops. Approved lesson plan: Flow Detectors (Flow Detectors.docx), slide 1.15 Multiple Choice Question

Report a problem
Objective 1.2Q327

An orifice plate is replaced with one that has a smaller orifice than design requires. Compared with the original, the indication will be:

The answer is C. A smaller orifice raises velocity and D/P, so indication reads higher. Approved lesson plan: Flow Detectors (Flow Detectors.docx), slide 1.28 Maintenance has been performed on an orifice-type differential pressure flow meter. The orifice plate was replaced with one containing a smaller orifice than what engineering design requires. What change in indication will the new orifice plate provide compared to the larger original orifice?

Report a problem
Objective 1.2Q987

What are the two distinct disadvantages of orifice plates?

The answer is A. Orifice plates cause a high permanent pressure drop and are subject to erosion. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.3

Report a problem
Objective 1.2Q988

Which orifice plate is the most common?

The answer is D. The concentric orifice plate is the most common of the three types. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.3

Venturi and dall flow tubes

Objectives 1.2

"The venturi tube, illustrated in Figure 3, is the most accurate flow-sensing element when properly calibrated." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.4
"The venturi tube has a converging conical inlet, a cylindrical throat, and a diverging recovery cone." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.4

Low pressure is measured in the throat, and high pressure upstream of the entrance cone. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.4

"The recovery cone allows for the recovery of pressure such that total pressure loss is only 10% to 25%." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.4
"The major disadvantages of this type of flow detection are the high initial costs for installation and difficulty in installation and inspection." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.4

The dall flow tube has a higher ratio of pressure developed to pressure lost than the venturi. It is more compact and is commonly used in large flow applications. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.5

"This type of flow tube has a pressure loss of about 5%." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.5

Pitot tubes, nozzles, and elbows

Objectives 1.2

In its simplest form, a pitot tube is a tube with an opening at the end, facing the flowing fluid. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.6

"The velocity of the fluid at the opening of the tube decreases to zero." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.6

This provides the high pressure input to a differential pressure detector, and a pressure tap provides the low pressure input. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.6

"The pitot tube actually measures fluid velocity instead of fluid flow rate." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.6

V̇ = KAV DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.6

"Pitot tubes must be calibrated for each specific application, as there is no standardization." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.6

A flow nozzle is similar to the venturi and normally used for high velocity flow. Its smooth contour suits fluids containing particulates. Word: Flow Detectors.docx, slide 1.8 Flow Nozzle

An elbow meter uses the slight pressure difference between the inner and outer pipe wall at a bend, and can measure flow in either direction. Word: Flow Detectors.docx, slide 1.10 Elbow Meter

Rotameters and nutating disks

Objectives 1.3

"The variation in area is produced by the rise and fall of a floating element." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.8
"The rotameter consists of a metal float and a conical glass tube, constructed such that the diameter increases with height." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.8

With no flow, the float rests at the bottom. As flow increases, pressure drop increases, and the float rises to indicate the amount of flow. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.8

"This type of flow meter is usually used to measure low flow rates." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.9
"In a displacement flow meter, all of the fluid passes through the meter in almost completely isolated quantities." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.9
"The most common type of displacement flow meter is the nutating disk, or wobble plate meter." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.9

The disk is attached to a central ball, with a shaft held in an inclined position by a cam or roller. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.9

"As the fluid flows through the chamber, the disk wobbles, or executes a nutating motion." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.9

The volume needed for one revolution is known, so total flow is the number of rotations times that volume. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.9

Objective 1.3Q992

What happens to the float in a rotameter as flow rises?

The answer is D. The higher the flow rate the greater the pressure drop, and the farther up the tube the float rises. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.8

Objective 1.3Q993

How does a nutating disk meter find total flow?

The answer is B. Total flow is calculated by multiplying the number of disk rotations by the known volume of fluid. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.9

Ultrasonic and electromagnetic meters

From the lesson plan

"As the fluid velocity increases, the rate of heat flow from the heated wire to the flow stream increases." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.10

The hot-wire anemometer is principally used for gas flow. Cooling changes the resistance of a heated platinum wire. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.10

The electromagnetic flowmeter is similar in principle to a generator: fluid in a pipe flows through a magnetic field. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.10

"For a given magnetic field, the induced voltage will be proportional to the average velocity of the fluid." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.10
"However, the fluid should have some degree of electrical conductivity." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.10
"When a conductive liquid flows through a magnetic field, a voltage is generated across the liquid." Word: Flow Detectors.docx, slide 1.19 Sodium Flow Metering: EM Flow Meters

The handbook describes ultrasonic equipment that uses DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.10

"use the Doppler frequency shift of ultrasonic signals reflected from discontinuities in the fluid stream to obtain flow measurements." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.10

The sodium flow metering slides describe transit time meters. Two transducers send pulses upstream and downstream. Word: Flow Detectors.docx, slide 1.17 Sodium Flow Metering: Ultrasonic

"The transit time downstream is less than the transit time upstream due to the velocity of the fluid flow." Word: Flow Detectors.docx, slide 1.17 Sodium Flow Metering: Ultrasonic

In an ultrasonic flow meter, the difference in transit time between signals sent against and with the flow is proportional to:

How do venturi and magnetic flowmeters compare in an SFR?

"The flow nozzle is commonly used for the measurement of steam flow and other high velocity fluid flow measurements where erosion may occur." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.12
"It is capable of measuring approximately 60% higher flow rates than an orifice plate with the same diameter." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.12

The flow nozzle requires less straight run piping than an orifice plate, but the pressure drop is about the same. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.12

"Since steam is considered to be a gas, changes in pressure and temperature greatly affect its density." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.13

ṁ = V̇ρ DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.13

Density can be calculated from upstream pressure, molecular weight, and absolute temperature. Since steam temperature is relatively constant in most steam systems, DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.13

"upstream pressure is the only variable in the above equation that changes as the system operates." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.13
"By measuring temperature and pressure, a computerized system can be used to electronically compensate a steam or gas flow indication for changes in fluid density." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.16

Flow circuitry and environmental concerns

Objectives 1.5, 1.6

The DP transmitter uses the pressure difference to position a device such as a bellows, which repositions the core of a differential transformer. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.17

"A loss of differential pressure integrity of the secondary element, the DP transmitter, will introduce an error into the indicated flow." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.18
"flow rate is proportional to the square root of the differential pressure." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.18
"The extractor is used to electronically calculate the square root of the differential pressure and provide an output proportional to system flow." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.18
"The indicator provides either a local or a remote indication of system flow." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.18
"If all means of flow indication are lost, flow can be approximated using pump differential pressure." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.18
"Pump differential pressure is proportional to the square of pump flow." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.18
"The density of the fluid, ambient temperature, and humidity are the three factors which can affect the accuracy and reliability of flow sensing instrumentation." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.19

Density matters most for gas flows. Any change in fluid temperature or pressure must be compensated for. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-04 p.18
