Bellows-type detectors

Objectives 1.1

"The metallic bellows is most accurate when measuring pressures from 0.5 to 75 psig." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.1

With a heavy range spring, some bellows can measure pressures of over 1000 psig. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.1

"The bellows is a one-piece, collapsible, seamless metallic unit that has deep folds formed from very thin-walled tubing." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.2
"System pressure is applied to the internal volume of the bellows." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.2

As inlet pressure varies, the bellows expands or contracts. Its moving end is connected to a mechanical linkage assembly. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.2

"As the bellows and linkage assembly moves, either an electrical signal is generated or a direct pressure indication is provided." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.2
"in practice, the bellows must always be opposed by a spring" DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.2

Bourdon tube detectors

Objectives 1.2

The bourdon tube is a thin-walled tube flattened to an elliptical cross-section and bent lengthwise into an arc of 270 to 300 degrees. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.2

Pressure inside the tube causes distention of the flat sections and tends to restore its original round cross-section. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.2

"This change in cross-section causes the tube to straighten slightly." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.2

Since the tube is fastened at one end, the tip traces a curve. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.2

"the movement of the tip of the tube can then be used to position a pointer or to develop an equivalent electrical signal" DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.2

Uses and environmental concerns

Objectives 1.3, 1.4, 1.5

"all pressure detectors are used to provide up to three basic functions: indication, alarm, and control." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.4
"If a pressure instrument fails, spare detector elements may be utilized if installed." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.4

If spare detectors are not installed, DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.4

"the pressure may be read at an independent local mechanical gauge, if available, or a precision pressure gauge may be installed in the system at a convenient point." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.4

If the detector is functional, voltage or current across the detector leads can be compared with calibration curves. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.4

"Pressure instruments are sensitive to variations in the atmospheric pressure surrounding the detector." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.4

This is especially apparent when the detector is in an enclosed space. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.4

"Ambient temperature variations will affect the accuracy and reliability of pressure detection instrumentation." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.5

Ambient temperature variations can directly affect circuit resistance and the calibration of electric or electronic equipment. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.5

"The presence of humidity will also affect most electrical equipment, especially electronic equipment." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.5

Strain gauge transducers

Objectives 1.6

"Transducers can produce a change in resistance, inductance, or capacitance." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.6
"A strain gauge measures the external force (pressure) applied to a fine wire." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.6
"The pressure change causes a resistance change due to the distortion of the wire." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.6

R = K (L / A) DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.6

"As the wire grid is distorted by elastic deformation, its length is increased, and its cross-sectional area decreases." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.7

These changes cause an increase in the resistance of the wire. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.7

"This change in resistance is used as the variable resistance in a bridge circuit that provides an electrical signal for indication of pressure." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.7

An increase in pressure expands the bellows, which moves a flexible beam with a strain gauge attached, changing its resistance. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.8

"The temperature compensating gauge compensates for the heat produced by current flowing through the fine wire of the strain gauge." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.8
"The balancing motor moves the slider along the slidewire, restoring the bridge to a balanced condition." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.8

Other pressure transducers

Objectives 1.6

Other resistance-type transducers combine a bellows or bourdon tube with a variable resistor. As the bellows moves, the slider changes the resistance. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.9

"The inductance-type transducer consists of three parts: a coil, a movable magnetic core, and a pressure sensing element." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.9

As the element moves the core inside the coil, the coil's inductance changes. Current through the coil increases as inductance decreases. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.9

In a differential transformer, the magnitude and direction of the output depend on how far the core is from center. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.10

"When the core is in the mid-position, there is no secondary output." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.10

Capacitive-type transducers DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.11

"consist of two flexible conductive plates and a dielectric." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.11
"As pressure increases, the flexible conductive plates will move farther apart, changing the capacitance of the transducer." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.11

Pressure detection circuit

Objectives 1.7

"The sensing element senses the pressure of the monitored system and converts the pressure to a mechanical signal." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.12
"The transducer converts the mechanical signal to an electrical signal that is proportional to system pressure." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.12

If the mechanical signal is used directly, a transducer is not required. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.12

"The detector circuitry will amplify and/or transmit this signal to the pressure indicator." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.12
"The pressure indicator provides remote indication of the system pressure being measured." DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.12

Pressure detector failures

From the lesson plan

The lesson plan gives two failure modes for pressure detectors. Overpressurization can damage sensing elements, and the detector fails high. Word: Pressure Detectors.docx, slide 1.16 Pressure Detector Failures

A sensing element leak or rupture causes the detector to fail low. Word: Pressure Detectors.docx, slide 1.16 Pressure Detector Failures

A weakened spring in a detector has to compress further to give the same counterforce, so indicated pressure increases. Word: Pressure Detectors.docx, slide 1.19 MC 5

Objective 1.6

Strain gauge under load

A wire grid is stretched by elastic deformation. What happens to R = K(L/A)?

  1. Its length increases
  2. Its cross-sectional area decreases
  3. Both changes raise L/A

Answer: The resistance increases.

DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.7

Objective 1.7

Signal path of a pressure reading

Follow the signal from the system to the operator.

  1. The sensing element converts pressure to a mechanical signal
  2. The transducer converts it to an electrical signal
  3. The detector circuitry amplifies or transmits it
  4. The indicator gives a remote reading

Answer: Sensing element, transducer, detector circuitry, pressure indicator.

Summary

  • In a bellows detector, pressure moves the bellows and linkage to produce a signal or move a pointer. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.3
  • In a bourdon tube detector, pressure tends to restore the tube's round cross-section, so the tube straightens and its tip moves. DOE-HDBK-1013/1-92 IC-02 p.3
