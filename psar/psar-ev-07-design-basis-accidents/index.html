DBAs set the design criteria for safety-related equipment. Only safety-related equipment is credited, so every sequence is harder than the DBE it came from. PSAR Rev 1 (NAT-9400), Sec. 3.9, pp. 3.9-1 to 3.9-11, with Secs. 3.3.1, 3.3.3 and 3.5.2

1 State the event initiator and safety-analysis assumptions.

Taught in: What a DBA is, Seismic DBA, DBAs with radiological release, Core blockage and the Major Accident, Fuel handling DBAs and cooling

2 Identify the challenged safety functions and acceptance criteria.

Taught in: DBAs with radiological release

3 Describe the event chronology.

Taught in: Reactor DBAs without release, Core blockage and the Major Accident

4 Predict key parameter trends.

Taught in: Seismic DBA

5 Identify automatic protective actions.

Taught in: Reactor DBAs without release

Not in PSAR Rev 1. This lesson does not teach it.

7 Select and apply the appropriate procedure.

Not in PSAR Rev 1. This lesson does not teach it.

8 Verify operation of credited equipment.

Taught in: Reactor DBAs without release

Not in PSAR Rev 1. This lesson does not teach it.

10 Establish and verify the required end state.

Taught in: Reactor DBAs without release, Fuel handling DBAs and cooling

11 Explain the consequences of delayed or incorrect actions.

What a DBA is

Objectives 1

"DBAs are derived from the design basis events by prescriptively assuming that only safety-related structures, systems, or components are available to mitigate postulated accident consequences to within the 10 CFR 50.34 dose limits." PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.9, p. 3.9-1

DBAs are postulated accidents used to set design criteria and performance objectives for safety-related SSCs. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.9, p. 3.9-1

Bounding parameters are chosen for DBA evaluation. Final parameters and results will be provided at the operating license stage. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.1, p. 3.3-1

The calculated DBA dose consequences are within the limits of 10 CFR 50.34(a)(1)(ii)(D). PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.5.2, p. 3.5-1

Seismic DBA

Objectives 1, 4

The seismic DBA starts with a safe shutdown earthquake and is analyzed at full power with a range of reactivity profiles. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.9.3.1.1, p. 3.9-4

The peak reactivity insertion for the safe shutdown earthquake is approximately $0.30. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.9.3.1.1, p. 3.9-4

The control rod drop time is assumed to be degraded by the seismic event. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.9.3.1.1, p. 3.9-4

Core blockage and the Major Accident

Objectives 1, 3, 11

The core blockage DBA postulates a blockage of fuel subchannels or other local faults at full power, resulting in fuel damage. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.9.6.1, p. 3.9-10

"Operators fail to shut down the reactor and the reactor continues operating at full power." PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.9.6.1, p. 3.9-10

The source term assumes all fuel pins in a single high burnup assembly fail. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.9.6.1.1, p. 3.9-10

A partial flow blockage is evaluated because of the tight spacing of fuel pins, high power density, and high burnup in sodium-cooled fast reactor assemblies. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.1.3, p. 3.3-2

This DBA has the maximum core damage and in-vessel release, and it was selected as the Major Accident. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.3, p. 3.3-4

For Kemmerer Unit 1 the Exclusion Area Boundary and Low Population Zone are the same boundary, a 0.25-mile radius from the Reactor Building centerpoint. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.3, p. 3.3-4

Fuel handling DBAs and cooling

Objectives 1, 10

In the in-vessel drop, the dropped and impacted assemblies are both damaged. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.9.5.1.1, p. 3.9-7

Fuel movement is assumed not to occur for at least 2.5 days after shutdown from full power. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.9.5.1.2, p. 3.9-7

In the EVST cooling DBA, analysis shows no fuel damage for at least 72 hours after the loss of cooling. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.9.5.4.1, p. 3.9-9
