How classification works

Objectives 1

Classification follows NEI 18-04, endorsed by RG 1.233. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 5.1, p. 5.1-1

PRA Safety Functions are initially assigned to systems, and a structured approach identifies the SSCs that perform them. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 5.1, p. 5.1-1

The safety significance of a PRA Safety Function determines the classification of the SSCs credited to perform it. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 5.1, p. 5.1-1

SR, NSRST, and NST

Objectives 2

ClassMeaning
SRPRA safety functions credited to keep the consequence of a DBE, or the frequency of a high-consequence BDBE, inside the F-C Target, and to conservatively ensure 10 CFR 50.34 dose requirements can be met PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 5.2.1, p. 5.2-1
NSRSTNon-SR PSFs that are risk-significant or required for DID adequacy PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 5.2.2, p. 5.2-1
NSTPSFs that are not SR or NSRST. They are not safety-significant and are not listed in Chapter 5 PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 5.2.3, p. 5.2-1

An SSC is risk-significant if its function is needed to keep any LBE inside the F-C Target, or if the total frequency of LBEs with the SSC failed is within one percent of any of the three cumulative risk targets. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 5.2.2, p. 5.2-1

The term SR function is synonymous with required safety function in NEI 18-04. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 5.2.1, p. 5.2-1

A non-SR function required for defense-in-depth adequacy is classified as what?

An example SR function

Objectives 2

DL3-RC1 is scram by gravity driven absorber insertion by latch release. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 5.2.3, p. 5.2-2

  • CRD: releases the control rod assemblies on a scram signal from the RPS
  • RCC: control rod assemblies insert to a depth and within a time limit
  • RES: provides support and position control so rod release is uninhibited
  • RPS: generates a scram signal on exceeding a scram setpoint

PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 5.2.3, p. 5.2-2

One SR function often spans several systems. When you study a system, ask which function it is credited for.

Design basis hazard levels

Objectives 3

DBHLs specify the severity of hazards SR SSCs are designed to withstand with no adverse impact on their SR functions. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 6.1.1, p. 6.1-1

  • Wind and tornado
  • External flood
  • Internal flood
  • Missiles
  • Pipe rupture
  • Seismic
  • Extreme winter precipitation
  • Internal fire

PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 6.1.3.2, p. 6.1-8

The design basis earthquake is the safe shutdown earthquake. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 6.1.1.6, p. 6.1-4

SR structures are located below grade, and they are damp-proofed and waterproofed to minimize groundwater intrusion. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 6.1.1.2, p. 6.1-1

Internal flood sources considered include sodium, molten salt, oil, and water systems. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 6.1.1.3, p. 6.1-2

SRDC

Objectives 4

The SRDC are design criteria needed to fulfill the Required Functional Design Criteria for SSCs selected to perform SR functions. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 6.1.2, p. 6.1-5

The Required Functional Design Criteria are compared to the Principal Design Criteria in Section 5.3 to confirm the PDC are complete. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 6.1.2, p. 6.1-5

Non-safety interactions

Objectives 5

  • The failure does not cause the SSC to strike an SR SSC
  • The failure does not adversely impact an SR function
  • The SSC is analyzed and designed to prevent its failure under DBHL conditions

PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 6.1.3, p. 6.1-5

Objective 5PL-04-K6

Which is an accepted way to prevent a DBHL interaction between an NST SSC and an SR SSC?

The answer is A. One criterion is that the SSC is analyzed and designed to prevent its failure under DBHL conditions. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 6.1.3, p. 6.1-5
