The FHP is where spent fuel ends up. It takes assemblies out of sodium, cleans them, and stores them under water with passive cooling as the safety function. PSAR Rev 1 (NAT-9400), Sec. 7.3.1, pp. 7.3-1 to 7.3-16

Purpose and subsystems

Objectives 1, 2

The FHP gives a water-based environment for intermediate-term storage of spent fuel and stores non-fuel irradiated core assemblies before disposal. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-1

It provides core assembly cooling, spent fuel reactivity control, fission product containment, and radiation shielding. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-1

SubsystemJob
SFP, Spent Fuel PoolWater-filled, lined concrete pool below grade in the FHB substructure PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-1
PIC, Pool Immersion CellMoves core assemblies from a sodium environment to water PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2
FPC, Fuel Pool CoolingNormal cooling for the SFP PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2
FPP, Fuel Pool PurificationFilters and purifies SFP water PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-3

The SFP has three operational zones: the PIC cavity, the cask loading pit, and the fuel storage area. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-1

Spent fuel pool and racks

Objectives 3, 4

The SFP is sized for ten years of spent fuel from normal operation plus other core assemblies. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-8

Total water inventory is approximately 574,000 gallons. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-8

A full SFP heat load is approximately 225 kilowatts. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-8

The fuel storage area holds seven intact fuel racks and one failed fuel rack. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-1

The stainless steel liner has a leak chase system. There are no pool wall penetrations below the elevation of stored core assemblies. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-8

The SFP does not use soluble boron. Racks hold fuel subcritical by geometry control and fixed borated neutron absorbers. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-1

The racks do not rely on specific zoning of fuel assemblies to stay subcritical. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-1

Pool immersion cell

Objectives 3, 4, 6

The BLTC transfers core assemblies into the PIC, where a cleaning vessel gives a controlled environment to chemically clean and remove residual sodium. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2

The cleaning vessel isolates assemblies from the surroundings to prevent sodium-water interactions. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2

During cleaning, heat passes through the cleaning vessel to the SFP water. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2

Gaseous effluent is filtered and sent to the FHB portion of the NI HVAC. Liquid effluent goes to the FPP. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2

After cleaning and rinsing, the PIC elevator lowers the assembly, and a horizontal trolley moves it upright to the cask loading pit. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2

Interlock or indicationPrevents
Pressure and flow sensorsGive positive indication that excess water is purged before an assembly transfers into the PIC PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-9
PIC rinse water pump interlockInadvertent pump operation that adds excess water to the cleaning vessel PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-9
PIC basket lock interlockLowering the cleaning vessel into the SFP before residual sodium is removed PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-10

Failed fuel

Objectives 4, 8

A fuel assembly with indicated cladding damage is sealed in an FFC inerted with helium. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2

No sodium cleaning occurs. The failed assembly goes directly into the FFC from the BLTC. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2

The FFC prevents metallic uranium-water interactions and sodium-water reactions that can occur due to cladding failure in sodium-bonded fuel. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2

Closure welds are done after the canister is voided and back-filled, while the FFC is in the PIC basket. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-10

If a spent fuel assembly fails in the SFP, the PIC can bring it back for canisterization. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-10

Normal cooling and purification

Objectives 3, 4

The FPC is NST. It has two independent trains that send heat to the NI Water System. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2

Each train is sized for the entire SFP heat load. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2

The FPP is a single purification train with anion and cation exchange resins. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-3

FPC and FPP suction and discharge lines are located to prevent draining the SFP below a minimum safe level. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2

Loss of cooling and makeup

Objectives 4, 8

If normal cooling is unavailable, core assemblies are cooled passively by natural circulation in the SFP. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.2, p. 7.3-5

If active cooling is lost, the SFP relies on evaporation and redundant makeup systems. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-7

The water volume cools spent fuel for at least 7 days without makeup at a full SFP heat load. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-8

Normal makeup comes from the NI Water System. Emergency makeup comes from offsite sources through an external fill leg. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-8

No powered SSCs are needed for emergency makeup through the fill leg. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.2, p. 7.3-6

SFP level sensors start refill on low level during normal operation and also support PAM. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.5, p. 7.3-11

Handling and controls

Objectives 3, 5, 6

The PHM is a gantry-and-hoist crane that moves assemblies underwater from the cask loading pit to the racks. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-1

A mechanical positive control system ensures core assemblies cannot be dropped. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-9

PHM vertical motion limits and interlocks keep a minimum water height above core assemblies for shielding. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-9

The PHM is operated locally, and its data is monitored by the FHC. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.5, p. 7.3-11

The FHC monitors and controls fuel handling from the Fuel Handling Control Room. It is NST. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2

The SFP has redundant level and temperature instruments. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.5, p. 7.3-11

Which FHP function is safety-related?

Summary

  • The PIC cleans sodium off assemblies and moves them into the pool. Failed fuel skips cleaning and goes into a helium-filled FFC. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-2
  • The SFP stays subcritical through rack geometry and fixed borated absorbers, with no soluble boron. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.1, p. 7.3-1
  • FPC is the normal cooling. The SR function is passive, and the pool lasts at least 7 days without makeup. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.3.1.3, p. 7.3-8
