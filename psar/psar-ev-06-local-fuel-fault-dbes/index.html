Event initiator and assumptions

Objectives 1

The initiator is a blockage of fuel subchannels or another localized fault in the core during full power operation, resulting in fuel damage. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.5.1.1, p. 3.7-15

Partial flow blockage is evaluated because of the tight pin spacing, high power density, and high burnup of sodium fast reactor assemblies. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.1.3, p. 3.3-2

  • Debris circulated in the primary sodium
  • Failure of wire-wrapped spacers
  • Swelling or bowing of the fuel pins

PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.1.3, p. 3.3-2

The assumption that an entire assembly failed is conservative and deterministic. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.4.1.5, p. 3.3-6

Knowledge check: Event initiator and assumptions

Objective 1EV-06-K1

Which is a stated cause of partial flow blockage?

The answer is B. Causes are debris, failed wire-wrapped spacers, and swelling or bowing of pins. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.1.3, p. 3.3-2

Challenged functions

Objectives 2

The event challenges cladding, so retention depends on the barriers past the fuel. Functional containment barriers perform their functions, but a release occurs. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.5.1.1, p. 3.7-16

Mean 30-day EAB TEDE is 5.70E-03 rem. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.5.1.3, p. 3.7-16

Objective 2EV-06-K2

In LFF-SAO-BL, is there a release?

The answer is A. Barriers perform their functions, but a radionuclide release occurs. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.5.1.1, p. 3.7-16

Event chronology and trends

Objectives 3, 4

StepLFF-SAO-BL
1Local fault at full power causes fuel damage PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.5.1.1, p. 3.7-15
2Operators manually shut down on exceeding failed fuel activity limits PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.5.1.1, p. 3.7-15
3Safe shutdown with IAC non-passive mode PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.5.1.1, p. 3.7-16

The fault does not cause primary sodium temperature to increase outside nominal operating conditions. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.4.1.5, p. 3.3-6

How does primary sodium temperature respond to a local fuel fault?

Protective action and operator role

Objectives 5, 12

Operators may scram the reactor if needed for failed fuel activity, but a scram is not required to keep consequences within analyzed limits. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.4.1.5, p. 3.3-6

End state and source term

Objectives 10

Eighty percent of the released non-noble gas radionuclides are postulated to enter the cover gas in bubbles without pool scrubbing. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.5.1.2, p. 3.7-16

Release from the primary boundary is 1 percent of cover gas volume per day, then 10 percent of HAA volume per day to the environment. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.5.1.2, p. 3.7-16
