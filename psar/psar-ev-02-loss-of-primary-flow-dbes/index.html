Event initiators and assumptions

Objectives 1

A DBE is an infrequent event sequence, less likely than an AOO and not expected in the life of the plant. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7, p. 3.7-1

Event sequences with mean frequencies of 1×10-4 to 1×10-2 per plant-year are classified as DBEs. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7, p. 3.7-1

DBE analysis takes into account the expected response of all structures, systems, and components, regardless of safety classification. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7, p. 3.7-1

DBEInitiatorInitial condition
DHP-L1PP-2Partial loss of primary flow from loss of a single PSPFull power PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.1.1, p. 3.7-1
DHP-LOOP-1Loss of offsite powerFull power PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.2.1, p. 3.7-1
SUD-LOOP-1Loss of offsite powerLow power PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.3.1, p. 3.7-2

Full power analysis starts at 100 percent reactor power, 100 percent core flow, and 360.1°C core inlet temperature. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.4.2, p. 3.3-7

Low power analysis starts at 5 percent reactor power, 15 percent core flow, and 309.5°C core inlet temperature. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.4.2, p. 3.3-7

Knowledge check: Event initiators and assumptions

Objective 1EV-02-K1

What frequency band defines a DBE?

The answer is C. Mean frequencies of 1×10-4 to 1×10-2 per plant-year are classified as DBEs. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7, p. 3.7-1

Report a problem
Objective 1EV-02-K2

Which equipment does DBE analysis take into account?

The answer is A. DBEs take into account the expected response of all SSCs. DBAs are the events limited to safety-related SSCs. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7, p. 3.7-1

Challenged functions and acceptance

Objectives 2

Core flow reduction raises primary sodium temperature until the reactor scrams, so the core must be shut down and its heat carried away. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.4.1.4, p. 3.3-6

For all three events, the fuel performance acceptance criteria are satisfied, fuel integrity is maintained for the peak fuel pin, and there is no radiological release. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.1.2, p. 3.7-1

The safe shutdown end state requires peak cladding temperature below 1157°F (625°C) and stable or decreasing. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.4.3, p. 3.3-7

Event chronology

Objectives 3

StepOne PSP lost (DHP-L1PP-2)
1One PSP is lost at full power PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.1.1, p. 3.7-1
2Reactor scrams on high high power-to-flow ratio PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.1.1, p. 3.7-1
3The unaffected PSP and the ISPs trip on high high primary sodium temperature PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.1.1, p. 3.7-1
4Non-passive and passive IAC cooling fail on both trains PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.1.1, p. 3.7-1
5Reactor reaches safe shutdown with RAC removing decay heat PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.1.1, p. 3.7-1

In the loss of offsite power at full power, the reactor scrams on high high power-to-flow ratio, non-passive IAC fails on both trains, and IAC passive mode removes decay heat. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.2.1, p. 3.7-1

At low power the reactor scrams because of the loss of primary flow, not on power-to-flow ratio. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.3.1, p. 3.7-2

Key parameter trends

Objectives 4

Primary sodium temperature rises from the flow reduction, then falls after the scram. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.4.1.4, p. 3.3-6

"The primary sodium temperature may increase after the PSP coast down is complete as the core transitions to natural circulation cooling, then decreases after natural circulation cooling is established and decay heat decreases." PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.4.1.1, p. 3.3-5

Study point: expect two temperature rises when the pumps trip. The first ends at the scram. The second can follow pump coast down, before natural circulation is established.

Credited equipment and failed functions

Objectives 8, 9

RAC and the available modes of IAC remove decay heat. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.4.1.1, p. 3.3-5

When passive and non-passive IAC are unavailable, the thermal capacity of the piping, components, and intermediate sodium in the intermediate loops is assumed available. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.4.1.1, p. 3.3-5

Failure assumedWhat carries decay heat
Non-passive IAC fails on both trainsIAC passive mode PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.2.1, p. 3.7-1
Non-passive and passive IAC fail on both trainsRAC PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.1.1, p. 3.7-1

A function failed in an LBE description was not necessarily failed by the initiating event. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.4.1, p. 3.3-4

Study point: read these events as a ladder. Each DBE removes one more means of cooling, and the next means carries the heat.

End state and operator role

Objectives 10, 12

All three events end with the reactor in safe shutdown. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.1.1, p. 3.7-1

Operator actions are not required to mitigate DBEs unless the DBE description says otherwise. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7, p. 3.7-1

Each DBE here is the most limiting DBE mapped to a matching DBA, where only safety-related equipment is credited. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.1.1, p. 3.7-1

In the DBA versions of these events, RAC is the decay heat removal system in all three cases. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.9.1.2.1, p. 3.9-1

Summary

  • Loss of flow DBEs scram on high high power-to-flow ratio at full power, or on loss of primary flow at low power. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.1.1, p. 3.7-1
  • Temperature can rise a second time after pump coast down, until natural circulation is established. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.3.4.1.1, p. 3.3-5
  • RAC is the last means of decay heat removal in the sequence, and these events produce no radiological release. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 3.7.1.1.1, p. 3.7-1
