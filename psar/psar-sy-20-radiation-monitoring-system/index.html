Purpose and monitor types

Objectives 1, 3

The RMS is a digital system with NSRST and NST functions. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.1, p. 7.6-60

It operates continuously and warns so that personnel exposure does not exceed 10 CFR 20 limits. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.1, p. 7.6-60

Each monitor sends activity, health, control, and status data to the Digital Radiation Monitor System (DRMS). PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.1, p. 7.6-60

The DRMS connects by direct digital link to the AMC, which interfaces with the operator in the MCR. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.1, p. 7.6-60

Monitors are area (ARM), effluent (ERM), and process and airborne (PRM). PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.1.2, p. 7.6-61

A tritium sample skid sits on the monitored plant stack. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.1, p. 7.6-61

There are no liquid effluent monitors, as no liquid effluents are possible in the design. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.1, p. 7.6-61

Operator interface and alarms

Objectives 5

MCR alarms are generated by the AMC when an activity signal from the DRMS exceeds setpoint. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.4, p. 7.6-66

ARMs give local alarms, and ERM and PRM monitors can also alarm locally. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.4, p. 7.6-66

The DRMS can do source checks, setpoint control, valve and pump manipulation, and database changes. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.4, p. 7.6-66

NSRST monitors have stand-alone, isolated Radiation Indicator Controllers. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.1.1, p. 7.6-61

Reliability and failures

Objectives 8, 10

  • Redundant monitoring points
  • Reliable power from the NUP
  • Self-testing for monitor failures
  • Quality design process under the QAPD

PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.3.2, p. 7.6-65

NSRST monitors are under surveillance testing and periodic calibration. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.3.2, p. 7.6-65

HAA and SCG monitoring points use redundant monitors. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.3.3, p. 7.6-66

The combined plant stack has two dedicated wide range gas monitors. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.3.3, p. 7.6-66

Any single component fault does not prevent NSRST functions. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.3.3, p. 7.6-66

The NUP gives 72-hour battery backup to NSRST monitors for the HAA and SCG. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.3.3, p. 7.6-66

Safety significance

Objectives 9, 10

RMS components are seismic class SCN1. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.3, p. 7.6-64

PDC 64 is met by monitoring NI building atmospheres, sodium and cover gas cleanup spaces, and effluent paths. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.2.2, p. 7.6-64

PAM is needed for defense-in-depth, to inform response after a postulated release. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.3.5, p. 7.6-66

Summary

  • Monitors feed the DRMS, the DRMS feeds the AMC, and the AMC raises the MCR alarm. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.4, p. 7.6-66
  • The RMS does not act on its own. AMC and UMC close valves and dampers on its signals. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.3, p. 7.6-64
  • HAA and SCG monitors are redundant, with 72-hour battery backup. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.6.3.3, p. 7.6-66
