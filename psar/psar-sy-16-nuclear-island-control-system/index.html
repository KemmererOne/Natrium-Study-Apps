The NIC is the plant's digital control layer. It is not one classified system. It is a group of subsystems, and only some carry NSRST functions. PSAR Rev 1 (ML25276A288), Secs. 7.6.1 and 7.6.2, pp. 7.6-1 to 7.6-17

Terms with a dotted underline are PSAR acronyms. Hover, tap or press Enter on one to see its meaning.

1 State the purpose of the NIC.

Taught in: I&C architecture, NIC subsystems

2 Identify the NIC boundaries and interfaces.

Taught in: I&C architecture, Separation from the RPS

3 Identify the major components of the NIC.

Taught in: NIC subsystems

4 Explain NIC operation.

Taught in: Reactivity limits and power runback

5 Describe the NIC controls and indications.

Taught in: Operator interface

6 Explain the NIC automatic features and interlocks.

Taught in: NSRST automatic functions, Reactivity limits and power runback

7 Apply operating procedures for the NIC.

Not in PSAR Rev 1. This lesson does not teach it.

Taught in: Reliability and failures

Taught in: Separation from the RPS, Safety significance

I&C architecture

Objectives 1, 2

I&C systems provide automatic actuation for licensing-basis events and manual actuation for defense-in-depth. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.1, p. 7.6-1

  • Independence
  • Communications and logical independence
  • Redundancy
  • Diversity
  • System Integrity
  • Reliable I&C
  • Human Factors Engineering

PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.1.2, p. 7.6-2

There are no direct digital data communications from NSRST and NST systems to SR systems. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.1.2.1, p. 7.6-2

One-way communication from SR systems goes through isolated hardwire or data-diode and gateways. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.1.2.1, p. 7.6-2

SR I&C systems have no inter-divisional communications except RPS voting. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.1.2.1, p. 7.6-2

NIC subsystems

Objectives 1, 3

The NIC is digital and runs on a Nuclear Island Distributed Control System (DCS). Some vendor supplied controls run outside the DCS. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.1, p. 7.6-6

SubsystemMonitors and controls
AMCNI auxiliary systems such as radwaste, SCG, and SPS PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.1, p. 7.6-6
CTCPHT, IHT, salt, IAC, and Energy Island Salt System flow control PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.1, p. 7.6-6
FHCFuel handling systems PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.1, p. 7.6-6
PMCPlant wide information, display, and manual command devices PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.1, p. 7.6-6
RMCCRD control and rod position PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.1, p. 7.6-6
UMCAuxiliary electrical and ancillary systems such as NHV and the standby diesel generator PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.1, p. 7.6-7

The NIC as a whole is not classified. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.2, p. 7.6-8

The data highway, HSI bus, DCS infrastructure, HSIs, and VDUs are NST, and NSRST functions do not rely on them. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.2, p. 7.6-8

Reactivity limits and power runback

Objectives 4, 6

Reactivity control in normal operation and AOOs is through power runback performed by RMC and CTC. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.2.2, p. 7.6-12

RMC and CRD control maximum rod withdrawal speed to limit the reactivity insertion rate. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.2.2, p. 7.6-12

A CRD control interlock prevents withdrawing more than one rod at a time. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.2.2, p. 7.6-12

A brake holds the rod at any selected position. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.2.2, p. 7.6-12

Separation from the RPS

Objectives 2, 9

There is no direct communication from NIC systems to the RPS. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.2.2, p. 7.6-12

The RPS sends data through a one-way data diode to the plant data highway, through an RPS-NIC gateway. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.1.1, p. 7.6-8

Hardwired signals use an isolation device. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.3.3, p. 7.6-15

Where diversity is required, redundant sensors are used: one for SR, one for NSRST or NST. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.3.5, p. 7.6-15

A shared SR sensor goes through an isolating signal splitter. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.3.5, p. 7.6-15

Operator interface

Objectives 5

NIC display and control functions on HSIs are NST. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.4, p. 7.6-15

Where display is an NSRST function, a separate NSRST display device is provided. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.4, p. 7.6-15

NSRST manual actuation uses a hardwired switch. Operators can also actuate from an MCR HSI, but that is NST. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.4, p. 7.6-15

The NIC acquires and displays PAM Type D and E variables. RPS PAM variables are also sent to the NIC, so all are displayed there. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.4, p. 7.6-16

The Safety Parameter Display System is a set of screens on NIC HSIs. It is not a stand-alone system. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.4, p. 7.6-16

There are no Type A PAM variables in the I&C design. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.1.2.1, p. 7.6-3

Reliability and failures

Objectives 8

With a redundant IO module and controller, the only common failure point is the physical termination of the field device. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.3.2, p. 7.6-14

No single failure on the HSI bus or plant data highway will fail the network. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.3.2, p. 7.6-14

Multiple HSIs and VDUs mean loss of one does not affect access to information or operations. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.3.2, p. 7.6-14

No part of the NIC is required to operate during or after a seismic event. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.3.1, p. 7.6-14

Summary

  • The NIC is a group of subsystems. AMC, CTC, and UMC are NSRST, and PMC, RMC, and FHC are NST. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.3, p. 7.6-13
  • Nothing digital goes from the NIC to the RPS. The RPS sends data one way through a data diode. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.2.2, p. 7.6-12
  • CTC trips pumps as automatic backups, and NSRST manual actions use hardwired switches. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.6.2.4, p. 7.6-15
