The Reactor Enclosure System is the vessel, head, internals, guard vessel, and supports. It holds the pool, forms a barrier, and shapes the flow path. PSAR Rev 1 (NAT-9400), Sec. 7.1.2, pp. 7.1-15 to 7.1-49

Terms with a dotted underline are PSAR acronyms. Hover, tap or press Enter on one to see its meaning.

Purpose and safety features

Objectives 1

The RES supports the reactor core and contains the primary sodium coolant. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.1, p. 7.1-15

  • Low-pressure, pool-type design with no boundary penetrations below the primary coolant operating level
  • Integral primary heat transport system inside the RV
  • Seismic isolation of the reactor from the supporting structure
  • Location in a subgrade reinforced concrete structure
  • Passive emergency core cooling by natural circulation and natural convection air cooling

PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.1, p. 7.1-15

The RES provides radionuclide retention, reactivity control, and reactor heat removal functions. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.1, p. 7.1-15

Boundaries and interfaces

Objectives 2

The primary coolant boundary includes portions of systems enclosing the primary coolant volume and cover gas volume. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.1, p. 7.1-15

  • RXB
  • RCC
  • PHT
  • CRD
  • RAC
  • Fuel Handling Systems
  • SCG
  • SPS
  • I&C systems

PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.1, p. 7.1-16

The HAA Seal and Well Seal preserve the RAC air flowpath and limit leakage to the HAA. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.3, p. 7.1-33

Reactor vessel and head

Objectives 3

The RV is a vertical cylindrical shell with an integrally welded torispherical bottom head. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.1.1, p. 7.1-16

The RV hangs from the flat, disc-shaped reactor head through a pressure-retaining structural weld. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.1.1, p. 7.1-16

The RV and reactor head are austenitic stainless steel, for compatibility with liquid sodium. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.1.1, p. 7.1-16

Rotatable plug and upper internals

Objectives 3, 4

The UIS sits above the core. It guides the CRDM drivelines and places core exit temperature monitoring instrumentation. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.1.2, p. 7.1-17

During plant operation, the RPA is locked in place and is not rotatable. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.3, p. 7.1-29

During refueling, the RPA is disconnected from the mounting flange and elevated to allow rotation. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.3, p. 7.1-29

With the RPA in the refueling position and the drivelines unlatched and fully retracted, the UIS rotates with the plug. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.1.2, p. 7.1-17

Sodium pools and flow path

Objectives 4

The hot pool liner separates the hot and cold pools and directs hot sodium toward the IHX inlets. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.3, p. 7.1-30

Stratification minimizes heat transfer between pools, increases thermal efficiency, and reduces thermal gradients. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.3, p. 7.1-30

The core inlet plenum receives cold coolant from the PSP discharge pipe. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.3, p. 7.1-31

The core barrels limit flow through the IVS to that needed to remove stored assembly decay heat. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.3, p. 7.1-31

Guard vessel

Objectives 3, 8

The GV provides a defense-in-depth coolant and functional containment boundary if the RV leaks. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.1.3, p. 7.1-17

The RV-GV annulus is inerted with argon gas from the SCG to minimize sodium-air reaction. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.1.3, p. 7.1-18

If the RV leaks, the equilibrium RV and GV sodium level remains above the IHX inlets, so heat still reaches the intermediate system or RAC. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.3, p. 7.1-32

The GV also keeps sodium away from RXB concrete and the surrounding atmosphere. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.3, p. 7.1-32

Objectives 3

The RSS transmits loads to the RXB substructure through RSBs, the MIRSS, and seismic isolators. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.1.4, p. 7.1-18

The MIRSS also supports the RAC CCA and forms a part of the RAC air flowpath. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.3, p. 7.1-33

Abnormal conditions and automatic response

Objectives 6, 8

The cover gas space accommodates increased inventory from IHX leakage, and the RPS initiates scram and ISP trip on high primary coolant level. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.2, p. 7.1-24

The IHT is maintained at a higher pressure than the primary coolant in normal operation, AOOs, shutdown, and accident conditions. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.2, p. 7.1-24

If forced primary flow is not available, the RES configuration passively establishes natural circulation. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.3, p. 7.1-28

Objective 6,8SY-02-K10

What automatic actions occur on a high primary coolant level from IHX leakage?

The answer is B. The RPS initiates reactor scram and Intermediate Sodium Pump trip on high primary coolant level. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.2, p. 7.1-24

Summary

  • The RES holds the pool, has no penetrations below the sodium level, and is seismically isolated. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.1, p. 7.1-15
  • The GV is a backup boundary with an argon annulus and a heat transfer path to RAC. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.3, p. 7.1-32
  • The RPA is locked during operation and rotates only for refueling. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.1.2.3, p. 7.1-29
