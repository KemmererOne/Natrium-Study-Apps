Sodium fires change the fire protection picture. Passive features do the work, and water stays away from sodium. This lesson pairs with SY-15. PSAR Rev 1 (NAT-9400), Sec. 8.2, pp. 8.2-1 to 8.2-8

Learning objectives

1 State the fire protection defense-in-depth approach for conventional and sodium fires.

Taught in: Defense in depth

2 Describe the sodium leak design features.

Taught in: Sodium leak design features

3 Explain how each category of sodium leak is handled.

Taught in: Leak categories

4 Describe how leak sizes and locations are postulated.

Taught in: Leak postulation

5 Explain the fire safe shutdown strategy.

Taught in: Fire safe shutdown

6 State the preliminary firefighting strategies and the role of operators and responders.

Taught in: Firefighting strategy

Defense in depth

Objectives 1

The design provides defense in depth through prevention, detection, containment, suppression, and safe shutdown. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1, p. 8.2-1

Requirements consider both conventional and sodium fires. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1, p. 8.2-1

The sodium fire strategy uses leak prevention, detection, and passive mitigation. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.2, p. 8.2-3

The radiological sodium strategy uses passive features, and adds active features only where necessary. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.1, p. 8.2-1

Sodium leak design features

Objectives 2

FeatureWhat it does
CellsRooms with limited leak rate and known oxygen volume, inerted or de facto inerted PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.2, p. 8.2-1
Guard pipesFull secondary pressure boundary for radiological sodium PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.3, p. 8.2-2
Guard enclosuresLike guard pipes, but with a drainpipe to a collection location PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.4, p. 8.2-2
ClamshellsGuard enclosures placed where pipe inspections are needed PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.5, p. 8.2-2
Catch pansSteel-lined volumes for the design-basis leak plus margin PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.6, p. 8.2-2
Inerted guard vesselsPrevent sodium-air and sodium-concrete reactions PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.7, p. 8.2-3
Leakage detectorsDetect liquid sodium, aerosols, and reaction products PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.8, p. 8.2-3

Clamshells in the HAA drain leaked IHT sodium to the RAB pipe chase, so a leak inside is not a leak into the HAA. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.5, p. 8.2-2

Suppression decks are used on catch pans where oxygen volume cannot be limited. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.6, p. 8.2-2

Conventional fire detection in non-inerted fire areas gives supplemental leak detection. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.8, p. 8.2-3

Knowledge check: Sodium leak design features

Objective 2OP-05-K2

What is the difference between a guard pipe and a guard enclosure?

The answer is C. Guard enclosures act similarly, except that a drainpipe is attached. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.4, p. 8.2-2

Report a problem
Objective 2OP-05-K3

Where does a clamshell in the HAA drain?

The answer is C. Clamshells allow leaked sodium to flow to the RAB pipe chase. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.5, p. 8.2-2

Report a problem
Objective 2OP-05-K4

When does a catch pan get a suppression deck?

The answer is D. Suppression decks are applied where environmental oxygen cannot be limited. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.6, p. 8.2-2

Leak categories

Objectives 3

CategoryHandling
Operational leakageMechanical joint leak, less than design basis, into a catch pan with no deck PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.2.1, p. 8.2-4
Into a guard enclosureContained and drained to a catch pan through a heat traced line PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.2.2, p. 8.2-4
Within a process cellContained or drained, with sloped floors and steel plates to a catch pan PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.2.3, p. 8.2-4
Beyond design basisEvaluated against the Quantitative Health Objectives PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.2.4, p. 8.2-5

Leaked radiological sodium stays within the process cell. Non-radiological sodium may drain to lower catch pans or vaults. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.2, p. 8.2-2

Detection of small leaks helps prevent large leaks. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.2, p. 8.2-4

Leak postulation

Objectives 4

Leaks are postulated for all piping that normally contains sodium. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.2, p. 8.2-4

Design basis leakage cracks are Dt/4 for seismic piping, and full circumferential breaks for non-seismic piping. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.2, p. 8.2-4

Sodium and salt systems are evaluated as moderate energy systems. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.3.3, p. 8.2-6

SSCs in the HAA are protected from the worst-case leak regardless of stress. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.3.3, p. 8.2-7

A top of pipe spray could make a faster, hotter fire. A bottom leak could make a larger volume flood. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.3.3.1, p. 8.2-7

What leak size is postulated for seismically qualified piping?

Fire safe shutdown

Objectives 5

Redundant trains of safety-significant and safe shutdown SSCs are in separate fire areas. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.10, p. 8.2-3

Damage from any single fire is limited to a single fire area. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.3.2, p. 8.2-6

The design limits fire damage to a single train. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.3.2, p. 8.2-6

Fire analyses and fire PRA will be done for the operating license application. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.3, p. 8.2-5

Firefighting strategy

Objectives 6

There is no active or manual fire suppression for large sodium fires. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.4.1, p. 8.2-8

Sodium leaks and fires are mitigated passively by NNA features. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.4.1, p. 8.2-8

Class D extinguishers with Sodium Chloride-based dry powder are staged for personnel protection. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.4.1, p. 8.2-8

Incipient conventional fires in the RAB and RXB use Class A, B, or C extinguishers. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.4.1, p. 8.2-8

Beyond the incipient stage, offsite responders with specialized training are requested. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.4.1, p. 8.2-8

Deep-seated sodium fires are contained in NNA features and are passively self-suppressing. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.4.1, p. 8.2-8

The RXB and RAB have no water-based suppression, to avoid sodium-water interaction. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.4.1, p. 8.2-8

A Fire Protection Program will be developed at the OL stage to meet 10 CFR 50.48(a). PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.4, p. 8.2-7

Summary

  • Sodium fires are handled by leak prevention, detection, and passive mitigation. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.2, p. 8.2-3
  • Guard pipes, enclosures, cells, and catch pans contain and collect sodium. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.1.6, p. 8.2-2
  • No water suppression in RXB and RAB. Class D extinguishers protect personnel. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 8.2.4.1, p. 8.2-8
