The SPS keeps three sodium inventories clean. Its monitoring and purification jobs are not safety functions, but the piping, pump trip, and leak response around them are. PSAR Rev 1 (NAT-9400), Sec. 7.2.4, pp. 7.2-41 to 7.2-57

Purpose and the three loops

Objectives 1, 2

The SPS monitors and removes impurities from the primary sodium, the intermediate sodium, and the EVST sodium. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.1, p. 7.2-41

Impurities include cesium, oxygen compounds, hydrogen compounds, debris, and corrosion products. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.1, p. 7.2-41

LoopDraws fromEquipment location
SPS-PRV, using a submersible EM pump within the RVBelow grade in the RAB PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.1, p. 7.2-41
SPS-IEach IHT loop or the IHT drain tanksGrade level in the RAB PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.1, p. 7.2-41
SPS-EEVSTBelow grade in the FHB PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.1, p. 7.2-41

The SPS-I has no cesium trap or associated radiological monitoring. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.1, p. 7.2-41

The SPS-E contains similar equipment and functions as the SPS-I. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.1, p. 7.2-41

The monitoring and impurity removal functions are non-safety-related with no special treatment. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.1, p. 7.2-41

Objective 1,2SY-08-K1

Which SPS loop has a cesium trap?

What is the safety classification of the SPS monitoring and impurity removal functions?

The answer is C. The monitoring and impurity removal functions are NST. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.1, p. 7.2-41

Why impurities matter

Objectives 1, 4, 9

ImpurityEnters byProblem or control
OxygenDissolved native oxides from steel, air and moisture ingress, and in-leakage from NSS into the IHTOxides can plug small orifices. High oxygen can accelerate corrosion at elevated temperature PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3, p. 7.2-50
HydrogenDiffusion out of steel and reaction with hydrogen bearing cover gas impuritiesSodium hydride can precipitate on cold surfaces and plug small orifices PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3, p. 7.2-50
TritiumGenerated from fission in the fuel and neutron capture in the control rodsReduced with hydrogen in the cold traps PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3, p. 7.2-50
CesiumFailed fuelControlled to prevent evaporation into cover gas and migration PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3, p. 7.2-50

Tritium in the primary coolant diffuses through the intermediate heat exchanger to the intermediate sodium. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3, p. 7.2-50

Heat tracing on SPS piping keeps sodium above minimum temperatures. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3, p. 7.2-50

Objectives 3, 4

ComponentRemovesHow
Cold trapOxygen and hydrogenPrecipitation at reduced temperature PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.6, p. 7.2-52

Monitoring

Objectives 3, 5, 8

The key process indicator for sodium purity is the saturation temperature, which correlates directly with impurity concentration. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.1, p. 7.2-51

The PTI correlates sodium flow rate through a plugging orifice at a given temperature to impurity concentration. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.1, p. 7.2-51

The SPS operates with sodium above the saturation temperature to avoid plugging passageways. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.1, p. 7.2-51

Hydrogen meters use a probe and an ion pump, or a similar technology. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.2, p. 7.2-51

The MPS collects samples for laboratory analysis and gives a point-in-time check against PTI data. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.4, p. 7.2-51

Process radiation monitors apply only to the SPS-P. There are two gamma spectrometers. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.3, p. 7.2-51

Monitor locationWhat it tells the operator
Main bypass lineCesium content of the process sodium, and whether the cesium trap may need to be brought online PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.3, p. 7.2-51
Return line downstream of the cesium trapEffectiveness of the cesium trap PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.3, p. 7.2-51

The monitors detect cesium against the normal background of Na-24 gamma rays, the dominant radionuclide in the SPS-P. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.3, p. 7.2-51

The PTI is in the SPS-P Technology Maturation Plan, because more design and testing is needed. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.1, p. 7.2-51

An SPS-P leak signal is received. Which set of automatic actions follows?

Operation and the EVST limit

Objectives 4, 8

The SPS may run continuously whenever monitoring or processing is needed. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3, p. 7.2-50

The SPS-P and SPS-I may operate in each of the four reactor modes. The SPS-E can operate independent of reactor modes. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3, p. 7.2-50

The SPS-E is not used to remove heat from the EVST. Its design prevents removing sodium below the minimum level for EVST residual heat removal. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.2.1, p. 7.2-44

The SPS-E is below grade to reduce personnel exposure if the EVST sodium contains radionuclides. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.2.1, p. 7.2-44

Summary

  • The SPS-P, SPS-I, and SPS-E clean three sodium inventories. Only the SPS-P has a cesium trap and radiation monitors. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.1, p. 7.2-41
  • Cold traps remove oxygen, hydrogen, and tritium by precipitation. The PTI tracks saturation temperature. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.3.1, p. 7.2-51
  • The SR trip on low hot pool level protects heat removal. An SPS-P leak closes the supply valve, trips pumps, and isolates cell ventilation. PSAR Rev 1, ML25276A288, Sec. 7.2.4.2.2, p. 7.2-47
